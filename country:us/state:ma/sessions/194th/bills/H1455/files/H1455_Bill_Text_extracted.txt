43 Section 2DDDDD. (a) There shall be established and set up on the books of the
44 commonwealth a separate fund to be known as the Massachusetts College in High School Trust
45 Fund. The fund shall be administered by the commissioner of the department of elementary and
46 secondary education, in consultation with the commissioner of the department of higher
47 education. The fund shall be credited with: (i) revenue from appropriations or other money
48 authorized by the general court and specifically designated to be credited to the fund; (ii) interest

50 donations to further the rates of student preparedness for workforce and postgraduate success.

51 Amounts credited to the fund shall not be subject to further appropriation and any money
52 remaining in the fund at the end of a fiscal year shall not revert to the General Fund.
53 (b) Amounts credited to the fund may focus on underserved communities across the
54 commonwealth, including those school districts with high concentrations of economically
55 disadvantaged students.
56 (c) Amounts received from private sources shall be approved by the commissioner of
57 elementary and secondary education and subject to review before being deposited in the fund to
58 ensure that pledged funds are not accompanied by conditions, explicit or implicit, on the
59 implementation of college in high school programming that may be detrimental to the neutral
60 education policy. The review shall be made publicly available.

67 69; (iii) any grants provided to institutions of higher education and other stakeholder

69 SECTION 5. Chapter 69 of the General Laws is hereby amended by inserting after
70 section 36 the following two sections:-
71 Section 37. (a) There shall be a Massachusetts Office of College in High School, herein
72 the office. The office shall be overseen by the commissioner of the department of elementary and
4 of 11

73 secondary education in consultation with the commissioner of the department of higher
74 education. The office will administer high school programs that expand student access to college
75 and postsecondary opportunities through high-quality instructional programming, hereinafter
76 referred to as college in high school, in order to increase the rates of student success in
77 postsecondary attainment and in the workforce. All programs established under this section shall:
78 (1) Offer a coherent sequence of courses that allows a student to earn a high school
79 diploma and achieve at least one of the following:
80 (i) the accumulation of transferable college credits; provided that, academic courses that
81 count for college credit shall be those agreed to by the institutions of higher education
82 participating in the partnership with the high school,
83 (ii) an industry-recognized credential or certificate, including those determined to be
84 necessary for occupations with high employment value as defined in section 38 of this chapter;
85 or
86 (iii) participation in a registered apprenticeship, pre-apprenticeship, apprentice readiness
87 program, or cooperative education program;
88 (2) Prepare students adequately for future success in the workforce or in an institution of
89 higher education;
90 (3) Encourage the cooperative or shared use of resources, personnel and facilities
91 between public high schools, public and independent colleges or universities and employers;
5 of 11

92 (4) Emphasize parental involvement and provide consistent counseling, advising and
93 parent conferencing so that parents and students can make responsible decisions regarding course
94 selection and can track the student's academic progress and success;
95 (5) Develop methods for early identification of potential participating students in the
96 middle grades and through high school and provide outreach to those students to promote
97 academic preparation and awareness of college in high school programs; and
98 (6) Develop strategies to identify and engage underserved populations including by
99 income, race, ethnicity, sex, English language learner status and students with disabilities.
100 (b) The office shall oversee all necessary college in high school programs, including but
101 not limited to programs that incorporate one or more of the following: (1) early college; (2) dual
102 enrollment; (3) industry-recognized credentialing in high school, including those for credentials
103 determined to be necessary for occupations with high employment value as defined in section 38
104 of this chapter; (4) advanced placement; (5) international baccalaureate programs; (6)
105 cooperative education programs, or (7) other approaches for college and career programs. The
106 office shall develop and, as needed, refine all college in high school program designations
107 offered in the Commonwealth and shall have the authority to set and raise credit attainment
108 targets, recommend any designations to be awarded to applicants, administer all aspects of state
109 support for the programs, track and report on the performance of participants in the aggregate
110 and identify and pursue further innovative approaches. Academic courses that count for college
111 credit shall be those agreed to by the institutions of higher education participating in any
112 partnership with the high school.
6 of 11

113 (c) Pursuant to regulations to be promulgated by the board, all high schools shall offer an
114 affordable college in high school program to students before the completion of their 12th grade
115 year that incorporates one or more of the following: (1) early college; (2) dual enrollment; (3)
116 industry-recognized credentialing in high school; (4) advanced placement courses; (5)
117 international baccalaureate programs; (6) cooperative education programs, or (7) other
118 approaches for college and career programs.
119 (d) There shall be a College in High School Joint Committee, hereinafter the CIHS Joint
120 Committee, charged with governing and overseeing the work of the office and making
121 recommendations to the board of elementary and secondary education and the board of higher
122 education. The CIHS Joint Committee shall develop recommendations that include but are not
123 limited to the following: (1) helping drive the commonwealth’s efforts to grow and expand
124 college in high school programs; (2) assisting the office in establishing and monitoring robust
125 performance requirements and targeted enrollments for all participating programs statewide and
126 (3) aiding the office in identifying and certifying new college in high school programs. The
127 CIHS Joint Committee shall consist of the secretary of the executive office of education or a
128 designee; the commissioner of the department of elementary and secondary education or
129 designee; the commissioner of the department of higher education or designee; two members of
130 the board of elementary and secondary education, who shall be appointed by the chair of the
131 board of elementary and secondary education; and two members of the board of higher
132 education, who shall be appointed by the chair of the board of higher education.
133 (e) The office shall create and annually review a set of per-credit charges that the public
134 and private institutions of higher education participating in a designated college in high school
135 program require for providing credit-earning courses. The per-credit charge may be subject to

136 factors, including but not limited to: (1) the segment of colleges offering certain courses; (2) the
137 site where the course is taught; (3) whether the course has extra costs associated with enrollment
138 and (4) whether there are minimum numbers of participating students per section offered.
139 (f) The office may develop, subject to the approval of the CIHS Joint Committee,
140 recommendations for college in high school programs to continue to enroll participating students
141 to extend and expand their graduation requirements such that they may continue to be considered
142 students of the high school for purposes of chapter 70 calculations for up to two more years in
143 order to complete certificates, degrees or up to two years of transferable credit to a four-year
144 college.
145 (g) The office shall propose a budget to the commissioner of the department of
146 elementary and secondary education that reflects the goals and objectives of the office.
147 (h) The college in high school programs shall be open to all enrolled students, on a space
148 available basis, and shall not discriminate on the basis of race, color, national origin, creed, sex,
149 gender identity, ethnicity, sexual orientation, mental or physical disability, age, ancestry, special
150 needs, proficiency in the English language or academic achievement. A lottery shall be held if
151 more students apply than can be accommodated.
152 (i) The office shall establish the information and requirements it deems necessary to be
153 included in any application for designation as a college in high school program in order to ensure
154 a high-quality and rigorous college in high school program. The office, after consultation with
155 the department of higher education, shall establish standards for approving and designating
156 college in high school programs.

157 (j) Where required, each designated college in high school program shall enter into an
158 agreement with at least one postsecondary institution that provides students with opportunities to
159 receive postsecondary credits during the student's participation in the program. Partner high
160 schools and colleges shall work together to ensure that college credits automatically transfer to
161 Massachusetts public postsecondary institutions upon completion of the program and admittance
162 to one of those public institutions. In addition, partnering high schools and colleges should
163 reduce barriers to college and consider automatic admission to the participating college upon
164 successful completion of the college in high school program. The agreement shall establish how
165 the program will be sustainable on current funding plus any anticipated annual state supplement
166 for designated college in high school programs as well as the designated duties for the high
167 school, in the areas of program coordination, student support, faculty support, career partnership
168 coordination and performance monitoring.
169 (k) Designation as a college in high school program shall be for an initial period of five
170 years, subject to review after the first three years, and renewable subject to performance. The
171 department of elementary and secondary education shall, subject to appropriation, provide
172 additional funding to designated programs for each participating student.
173 (l) Annually, by December 31, each college in high school program shall file a report
174 with the commissioner of the department of elementary and secondary education and the
175 commissioner of higher education, on performance measures, including, but not limited to the
176 following categories:
177 (i) high school graduation rates of participating students;
178 (ii) percentage of participating students who complete the program;

179 (iii) percentage of participating students who gain any postsecondary credits;
180 (iv) credits earned by participating students in college in high school programs, including

182 (v) percentage of participating students in a 6-year cohort who attain postsecondary
183 degrees; and
184 (vi) college and career outcomes of participating students.
185 The commissioners shall prepare an aggregate report for the senate and house chairs of
186 the joint committee on higher education, and the joint committee on education.

192 ethnicity, sex, English language learner status and students with disabilities. Goals should

194 annual, timely public reporting of progress statewide and by district and school towards those
195 goals.
