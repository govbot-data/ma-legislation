10 PREAMBLE
11 We, the people of Somerville, with our diversity in culture and thought, in order to
12 reaffirm the liberties of the people with respect to the conduct of our local government, adopt
13 this charter to continue and enhance the city’s strong traditions of the pursuit of justice;
1 of 51

14 empowerment of residents; ethical, transparent and responsive leadership; wise use of public
15 resources; representation for all; and an engaged populace. We expect that our government will
16 be approachable, accountable, equitable, inclusive, and respectful towards all people, and it will
17 strive to provide all residents an equal opportunity to participate fully in the economic, cultural
18 and intellectual life of the city.
19 ARTICLE 1
20 INCORPORATION; SHORT TITLE; DEFINITIONS
21 SECTION 1-1: INCORPORATION
22 The residents of the city of Somerville within the territorial limits established by law,
23 shall continue to be a municipal corporation, a body corporate and politic, under the name "City
24 of Somerville.”
25 SECTION 1-2: SHORT TITLE
26 This instrument shall be known and may be cited as the City of Somerville Charter
27 (“charter”).
28 SECTION 1-3: SEPARATION OF POWERS
29 The administration of the fiscal, prudential and municipal affairs of the city shall be
30 vested in an executive branch led by a mayor and a legislative branch consisting of a city
31 council. The legislative branch shall never exercise any executive power and the executive
32 branch shall never exercise any legislative power.
33 SECTION 1-4: POWERS OF THE CITY
2 of 51

34 Subject only to express limitations on the exercise of any power or function by a
35 municipal government in the constitution or General Laws, it is the intention and the purpose of
36 the voters of the city, through the adoption of this charter, to secure for themselves and their
37 government all of the powers it is possible to secure as fully and as completely as though each
38 power were specifically and individually enumerated in this charter.
39 SECTION 1-5: CONSTRUCTION
40 The powers of the city under this charter are to be construed liberally in favor of the city
41 and the specific mention of any particular power is not intended to limit the general powers of
42 the city as stated in section 1-4. If any provision of this charter conflicts with any special act or
43 law adopted by the city to the contrary, the provisions of this charter shall prevail.
44 SECTION 1-6: INTERGOVERNMENTAL RELATIONS
45 Subject only to express limitations in the constitution or General Laws, the city may
46 exercise any of its powers or perform any of its functions and may participate in the financing
47 thereof, jointly or in cooperation, by contract or otherwise, with the commonwealth or any of its
48 agencies or political subdivisions, or with the United States government or any of its agencies.
49 SECTION 1-7: DEFINITIONS
50 Unless the context clearly requires otherwise, the following terms shall have the
51 following meanings as used in this charter:
52 (1) “Business day”, any day that is not a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday. For the
53 purposes of this definition, legal holiday shall mean Massachusetts legal holidays as published
54 by the state secretary.
3 of 51

55 (2) “Charter”, this charter and any adopted amendments to it.
56 (3) “City”, the city of Somerville.
57 (4) “City agency”, any multiple-member body, department, division or office of the city.
58 (5) “City officer” or “department head”, a person having charge of a city office or
59 department.
60 (6) “City website”, an online site established and maintained by the city as its repository
61 of municipal information.
62 (7) “Emergency”, a sudden, generally unexpected occurrence or set of circumstances
63 demanding immediate action or response.
64 (8) “General Laws”, the Massachusetts General Laws.
65 (9) “Majority vote”, a majority of the present and voting members of a body, unless
66 otherwise provided for by law or by the body’s own rules; provided, however, that General Laws
67 related to any vote to meet in executive session shall always require a majority of the full
68 multiple-member body.
69 (10) “Measure”, any ordinance, order, other vote or proceeding adopted or proposed to be
70 adopted, by the city council or the school committee.
71 (11) “Multiple-member body”, any council, commission, committee, subcommittee or
72 other body consisting of 2 or more persons, whether elected, appointed or otherwise constituted,
73 but not including the city council or its committees, the school committee or its subcommittees
74 or an advisory committee or task force established by the mayor or city council.
4 of 51

75 (12) “Quasi-judicial body”, any multiple-member body which resolves specific disputes,
76 or makes determinations about the rights, obligations or privileges of identifiable parties.
77 (13) “Regulatory body”, any multiple-member body responsible for establishing or
78 enforcing rules or regulations.
79 (14) “Organization or reorganization plan”, a plan submitted by the mayor to the city
80 council which proposes:
81 (i) a change in the organization or the administrative structure of the city administration
82 or organization; or (ii) a change in the way in which municipal services are delivered.
83 (15) “Post”, make available publicly on the city website, at city hall, in a local newspaper
84 or as otherwise may be required by law. For the purposes of this definition, a local newspaper
85 shall be a newspaper of general circulation within the city, with either weekly or daily
86 circulation. The city council president may, from time to time, select a local newspaper for
87 posting according to a procedure that shall be set forth in the rules of the city council.
88 (16) “Quorum”, a majority of all voting members of a multiple-member body unless
89 some other number is established by law or by ordinance.
90 (17) “Voter”, anyone residing in the city of Somerville who is eligible to vote pursuant to
91 state and federal law.
92 (18) “Year”, a calendar year, unless otherwise specified.
93 ARTICLE 2
94 LEGISLATIVE BRANCH
5 of 51

95 SECTION 2-1: COMPOSITION; TERM OF OFFICE; ELIGIBILITY
96 (a) Composition - There shall be a city council consisting of 11 members which shall
97 exercise the legislative powers of the city. Four members of the city council shall be known as
98 councilors at-large and shall be nominated and elected by the voters at large. Seven members of
99 the city council shall be known as ward councilors and shall be nominated from and elected by
100 the voters in each ward, with 1 ward councilor to be elected from each of the 7 wards into which
101 the city is divided under section 7-6.
102 (b) Term of Office - The term of office for councilors shall be 2 years each, beginning on
103 the first business day in January following the municipal election.
104 (c) Eligibility - Any voter shall be eligible to hold the office of councilor at-large. Any
105 voter residing in the ward from which election is sought shall be eligible to hold the office of
106 ward councilor. If the city council determines that a ward councilor or a councilor at-large has
107 removed from the city during the councilor’s term, that office shall immediately be deemed
108 vacant and filled in the manner provided in section 2-12. A ward councilor who removes from
109 the ward in which the councilor was elected and who remains a voter may continue to serve the
110 balance of the term to which such ward councilor was elected.
111 SECTION 2-2: PROHIBITIONS
112 (a) Holding Other City Position – Except as otherwise provided by this charter, a member
113 of the city council shall hold no other compensated city position. A former member of the city
114 council shall not hold any compensated appointed city position for 1 year after termination of
115 such former member’s service on the city council, unless the city council approves a waiver of
116 this provision by affirmative vote of 8 members of the city council. This section shall not prevent
6 of 51

117 a city employee who vacated a position to serve as a member of the city council from returning
118 to the same position upon the expiration of the term for which that person was elected.
119 (b) Interference with Administration - The city council or any member of the city council
120 shall not give orders or directions to any employee of the city appointed by the mayor, either
121 publicly or privately.
122 SECTION 2-3: COMPENSATION
123 The members of the city council shall receive compensation for their services as set by
124 ordinance. An ordinance increasing or reducing the compensation of the members of the city
125 council shall not be effective unless:
126 (i) it is adopted by affirmative vote of 8 members of the city council;
127 (ii) it is adopted prior to the last 6 months of the city council’s term; and
128 (iii) it provides that the compensation increase or reduction is to take effect upon the
129 organization of the city government following the next regular municipal election.
130 SECTION 2-4: GENERAL POWERS
131 Except as otherwise provided by the General Laws or by this charter, all powers of the
132 city shall be vested in the city council which shall provide for the performance of all duties and
133 obligations imposed upon the city by law.
134 SECTION 2-5: EXERCISE OF POWERS; QUORUM; RULES
7 of 51

135 (a) Exercise of Powers - Except as otherwise provided by General Laws or by this
136 charter, the legislative powers of the city council may be exercised in a manner determined by
137 the city council.
138 (b) Quorum - Once a quorum is present, a majority vote shall be required to adopt any
139 ordinance, except as otherwise provided by General Laws or by this charter.
140 (c) Rules of Procedure - The city council shall adopt rules regulating the procedures of
141 the city council, which shall be subject to adoption, amendment or repeal by vote of the city
142 council.
143 (d) Meetings - Regular meetings of the city council shall be held at a time and place fixed
144 by ordinance.
145 (e) Special meetings of the city council shall be held at the call of the president or at the
146 call of not less than 6 members for any purpose. Except in an emergency as declared by the city
147 council president, notice of the meeting shall be delivered to the city clerk not less than 3
148 business days in advance of the time set and shall specify the date, time, location and purpose for
149 which the meeting is to be held. The city clerk shall post an agenda for such meeting not less
150 than 2 business days in advance of the time set.
151 (f) All sessions of the city council and every city council committee or subcommittee
152 shall, at all times, be open to the public, unless otherwise specified by law.
153 (g) A full, accurate, up-to-date account of the proceedings of the city council shall be
154 maintained by the city clerk, which shall include a record of each vote taken and be made
155 available with reasonable promptness following each meeting. Executive session minutes shall
8 of 51

156 be made available as soon as publication of the minutes would not defeat the purpose of the
157 executive session, unless otherwise provided by law.
158 SECTION 2-6: ELECTION, TERM, AND POWERS OF CITY COUNCIL PRESIDENT
159 AND VICE-PRESIDENT
160 (a) Election and Term - As soon as practicable after the councilors-elect have been
161 qualified following each regular municipal election, as provided in section 7-3, the city council
162 shall elect from among its members a president and vice-president who shall serve for 1-year
163 terms. The member of the council with the most years of service shall preside at the election. If 2
164 or more members have the same term of service, the councilor with the last name that is first in
165 alphabetical order from among such councilors shall preside. The conduct of all elections of the
166 city council president and vice-president shall otherwise be prescribed within the rules of the city
167 council.
168 (b) Powers and Duties - The president shall have the following powers and duties:
169 (i) determine the agenda for city council meetings;
170 (ii) preside at all meetings of the city council, regulate its proceedings and decide all
171 questions of order; provided, however, that the vice-president shall preside in the absence of the
172 president;
173 (iii) appoint all members of committees of the city council, whether special or standing;
174 (iv) have the same powers to vote upon measures coming before the city council as any
175 other member of the city council; and
9 of 51

176 (v) perform any other duties consistent with the office that are established by this charter,
177 ordinance or other vote of the city council.
178 SECTION 2-7: APPOINTMENTS OF THE CITY COUNCIL
179 (a) City Clerk - The city council shall appoint a city clerk to serve at the pleasure of the
180 city council and until a qualified successor is chosen. The city clerk shall have the powers and
181 duties as are provided to city clerks by the General Laws relating to the keeping of records and
182 vital statistics, the issuance of licenses and such additional powers and duties as may be
183 provided by law or by other vote of the city council.
184 (b) Advisory Legal Counsel - The city attorney shall be the only authorized officer of the
185 city in all legal matters involving the city’s government; provided, however, that, subject to
186 appropriation, the city council may secure outside legal services, when needed, to seek secondary
187 legal opinions. The city council shall make a reasonable request for such legal services to the
188 mayor and that request shall not be unreasonably denied by the mayor. The city attorney and any
189 advisory legal counsel shall consult to the extent practicable prior to the release of any secondary
190 opinion. The legal services provided to the council shall not include representation of the council
191 or any councilor in any litigation or the issuance of formal legal opinions on behalf of the city.
192 (c) Other Staff - Subject to appropriation, the city council may employ staff as it deems
193 necessary.
194 (d) Removals and Suspensions of City Council Staff - City council appointments may be
195 removed at the sole discretion of the city council subject to limitations and requirements imposed
196 by federal and state laws, rules or regulations.
10 of 51

197 SECTION 2-8: CITY COUNCIL CONFIRMATION OF CERTAIN APPOINTMENTS
198 (a) Department Heads - The mayor shall refer to the city council for confirmation and
199 simultaneously file with the city clerk the name of each person the mayor appoints as a
200 department head. These appointments shall be considered confirmed 30 days from the date of the
201 first regularly scheduled city council meeting after the date on which notice of the appointment
202 was filed with the city clerk, unless the city council within said 30 days rejects the appointment
203 by the affirmative vote of 8 of its members; provided, however, that if no city council meeting is
204 held within the subsequent 30 days of the first meeting, the city council shall have 30 days from
205 the second regularly scheduled meeting after the appointment to reject the appointment. The city
206 council shall not unreasonably reject an appointment and shall accompany a rejection with a
207 written statement describing the reason for the rejection, which shall be delivered to the mayor
208 and placed on file with the city clerk within 30 days of the city council’s vote rejecting an
209 appointment. The question on rejection of any appointment made by the mayor shall not be
210 subject to the procedure of charter objection provided in subsection (b) of section 2-9.
211 (b) Multiple-Member Bodies - The mayor shall refer to the city council and
212 simultaneously file with the city clerk, the name of each person the mayor desires to appoint as a
213 member of a multiple-member body. The city council shall have 60 days after the date on which
214 notice of the proposed appointment was filed with the city clerk to vote to approve or reject the
215 appointment; provided, however, that the city council shall not unreasonably withhold approval
216 of the appointment; and provided further, that if the city council does not take action on the
217 appointment within 60 days, the appointment shall be deemed approved. Multiple-member body
218 members may be removed at the discretion of their appointing authority.
11 of 51

219 (1) All appointments to quasi-judicial bodies and regulatory bodies shall be for terms
220 established by administrative order or the General Laws. Any member of a quasi-judicial body or
221 regulatory body appointed to a successive term shall be subject to confirmation by the city
222 council under subsection (b) of section 2-8 upon the expiration of each term.
223 (2) In the event of a vacancy on a multiple-member body where the seat has remained
224 vacant in excess of 1 year and the mayor has not referred to the city council any proposed
225 appointees to fill the vacancy, the city council president may present to the mayor the names of
226 not more than 3 persons as recommendations for appointment. The procedure for selecting
227 names of persons for presentation to the mayor shall be established within the rules of the city
228 council.
229 (3) The mayor shall twice annually, in February and August, post a complete list of the
230 vacancies on all multiple-member bodies, as well as the procedures for individuals to apply to
231 become a member of such bodies. This posting shall be in addition to, and not a substitute for,
232 regular posting for the purpose of filling vacancies as they arise.
233 (c) Police and Fire Employees - The mayor shall refer to the city council for confirmation
234 and simultaneously file with the city clerk, the name of each person the mayor desires to appoint
235 or promote as a member or officer of the police department or the fire department. The city
236 council shall not unreasonably withhold confirmation of appointments, shall adhere to any merit
237 principles identified in applicable law, including, but not limited to applicable civil service law,
238 and shall accompany a rejection with a written statement describing the reason for the rejection,
239 which shall be delivered to and placed on file with the city clerk within 30 days of the rejection.
12 of 51

240 The question on confirmation of any appointment submitted by the mayor shall not be subject to
241 the procedure of charter objection provided in subsection (b) of section 2-9 of this charter.
242 SECTION 2-9: ORDINANCES AND OTHER MEASURES
243 (a) Measures - Every adopted measure shall become effective at the expiration of 10 days
244 after adoption or upon the signature of the mayor, whichever occurs first, unless the ordinance or
245 this charter provides otherwise. An ordinance shall not be amended or repealed except by
246 another ordinance adopted in accordance with this charter.
247 (b) Charter Objection - On the first occasion that the question on adoption of a measure is
248 put to the city council, a single member may object to the taking of the vote and postpone the
249 vote until the next meeting of the city council, whether regular or special. If 2 or more members
250 object, the vote shall be postponed until the next regular meeting. This procedure shall not be
251 used more than once for any specific matter regardless of whether it has been amended. A
252 charter objection shall have privilege over all motions; provided, however, that it shall be raised
253 prior to or at the call for a vote by the presiding officer and at that time all debate pertaining to
254 the item shall cease.
255 SECTION 2-10: ACCESS TO INFORMATION
256 (a) In General - The city council may make investigations into the affairs of the city and
257 into the conduct and performance of any city agency. Absent compelling circumstances or an
258 emergency, which shall be declared on record and noted in the city council minutes, the city
259 council shall give a notice of not less than 14 days to any person it may require to appear before
260 it under this section; provided, however, that in the case of compelling circumstances or
261 emergency, the notice period may be shortened to not less than 7 days. The notice shall include
13 of 51

262 specific questions on which the city council seeks information and any person called to appear
263 before the city council under this section shall not be required to respond to any question not
264 relevant or related to those questions presented in advance and in writing. The mayor shall
265 receive a copy of any notice issued under this section at the same time as the person who is
266 requested to appear before the council. A majority vote of the city council shall be required to
267 issue notice under this section.
268 (b) Department Head - The city council may require, by majority vote, specific
269 information from a department head or the department head’s designee on any matter related to
270 the municipal services, functions and powers or duties which are within the scope of
271 responsibility of such department head and related to the official duties and responsibilities of the
272 city council. The department head or the department head’s designee shall not be required to
273 answer questions relating to any other matter outside the scope of the matter noticed or provide
274 information the release of which is prohibited by law.
275 (c) Mayor - The city council may request, by majority vote, specific information from the
276 mayor on any municipal matter related to the official duties and responsibilities of the city
277 council. The mayor or their designee shall appear before the city council and respond to the
278 questions. The mayor or their designee shall not be required to answer questions relating to any
279 other matter outside the scope of the matter noticed or be required to provide information the
280 release of which is prohibited by law. The mayor may bring to this meeting any assistant,
281 department head or other city officer or employee that the mayor may consider necessary to
282 assist in responding to the questions posed by the city council.
283 SECTION 2-11: GROUP PETITIONS
14 of 51

284 The city council shall hold a public hearing and act with respect to every petition which is
285 addressed to it and signed by not less than 50 voters, as certified by the board of election
286 commissioners within 5 days of the receipt of the petition. The city clerk shall accept the petition
287 following certification by the board of election commissioners and place it before the council on
288 the next agenda. The hearing shall be held by the city council, or by a committee or
289 subcommittee thereof, and the city council shall act on the petition within 3 months of the date it
290 first appeared on the agenda. Hearings on 2 or more petitions filed under this section may be held
291 at the same time and place. Not less than 14 days before the hearing, the city clerk shall notify
292 the 10 petitioners whose names first appear on each petition, publish a general summary of the
293 subject matter of the petition and post notice of the date and time of the public hearing. A
294 hearing shall not be held upon any subject more than once in a 12-month period, as determined
295 by the city council president.
296 SECTION 2-12: FILLING OF VACANCIES
297 (a) Councilor At-Large - When a vacancy exists for an at-large city council seat and more
298 than 180 days remain in the term for such seat, the city clerk shall notify the city council and the
299 board of election commissioners of the vacancy within 7 days of such vacancy. Not less than 7
300 days after notification, the chair of the board of election commissioners or a designee shall
301 certify, in writing, to the city clerk that the defeated candidate for the office of councilor at-large
302 with the next highest number of votes at the prior municipal election, if willing and able to serve,
303 shall fill the remainder of the existing term. The city clerk shall administer the oath of office to
304 such candidate within 15 days after certification and the person shall take the seat. If such
305 candidate declines the office, is not eligible and willing to serve or fails to take the oath of office
306 within the time period set forth in this section, then the candidate with the next highest number of
15 of 51

307 votes for councilor at-large at the prior municipal election who is eligible and willing to serve
308 shall take the seat under the same circumstances as above. If no such candidate for the office can
309 serve, the city council shall call a special election to fill the seat for the remainder of the term. If
310 the vacancy occurs with not more than 180 days in the existing term, the vacancy shall be filled
311 as above; provided, however, that if no candidate is available to take the seat, the seat shall
312 remain vacant until the next regular municipal election and the person elected shall take office
313 upon certification of the vote and serve for the remainder of the existing term as well as the term
314 to which elected. The city clerk shall administer the oath of office to elected candidates at the
315 next meeting of the city council.
316 (b) Ward Councilor - If a vacancy occurs in the office of ward councilor and more than
317 180 days remain on the existing term, the city council shall immediately order a special election
318 to fill the vacancy for the remainder of the unexpired term. If a vacancy occurs with nor more
319 than 180 days before the end of the term, the seat shall remain vacant until the next regular
320 municipal election for the office and the person elected shall fill the vacancy for the remainder of
321 the unexpired term and the term to which elected. The city clerk shall administer the oath of
322 office to the person at the next meeting of the city council.
323 ARTICLE 3
324 EXECUTIVE BRANCH
325 SECTION 3-1: MAYOR: ROLE; TERM OF OFFICE; ELIGIBILITY
326 (a) Role - The chief executive officer of the city shall be a mayor, elected by the voters at
327 large. The role of mayor shall be a full-time position.
16 of 51

328 (b) Term of Office - The term of office for mayor shall be 2 years, beginning on the first
329 business day in January following the municipal election.
330 (c) Eligibility - Any voter shall be eligible to hold the office of mayor.
331 SECTION 3-2: PROHIBITIONS
332 The mayor shall not hold another compensated city position or other elected public
333 office. A former mayor shall not hold a compensated appointed city office or city employment
334 for 1 year after termination of their service as the mayor. Any former mayor shall not receive
335 compensation for contracted work authorized during their tenure as the mayor. This subsection
336 shall not prevent a city officer or other city employee who has vacated a position to serve as the
337 mayor from returning to the same office or other position of city employment held when the
338 position was vacated. This prohibition shall not apply to persons covered by a leave of absence
339 under section 37 of chapter 31 of the General Laws.
340 SECTION 3-3: COMPENSATION
341 The mayor shall receive compensation for the mayor’s services as set by the city council
342 by ordinance. An ordinance increasing or reducing the compensation of the mayor shall not be
343 effective unless:
344 (i) it is adopted by affirmative vote of 8 members of the city council;
345 (ii) it is adopted prior to the last 6 months of the mayor’s term; and
346 (iii) it provides that the compensation increase or reduction is to take effect upon the
347 organization of the city government following the next regular municipal election.
17 of 51

348 SECTION 3-4: EXECUTIVE POWERS
349 (a) Executive Powers - The executive powers of the city shall be vested solely in the
350 mayor and may be exercised by the mayor either personally or through the city agencies under
351 the general supervision and control of the office of the mayor. The mayor shall cause this charter,
352 laws, ordinances and other orders of the city government to be enforced and shall cause a record
353 of all official acts of the executive branch of the city government to be kept. The mayor shall
354 supervise, direct and be responsible for the efficient administration of all city activities and
355 functions placed under the control of the mayor by law or by this charter. The mayor or the
356 mayor’s designee shall have the sole power to sign, seal, execute and deliver on behalf of the
357 city, deeds and leases of land sold or leased by the city and other deeds, agreements, contracts,
358 leases, indentures and assurances on behalf of the city.
359 (b) Supervision of City Agencies - The mayor shall exercise general supervision and
360 direction over all city agencies, unless otherwise provided by law or by this charter. Each city
361 agency shall furnish to the mayor any information or materials the mayor may request and as the
362 needs of the office of mayor and the interest of the city may require. The mayor shall be
363 responsible for the efficient and effective coordination of the activities of all city agencies and
364 may call together for consultation, conference and discussion, at reasonable times, all persons
365 serving the city.
366 (c) Multiple-Member Bodies - The mayor shall be, by virtue of the office, a member of
367 every appointed multiple-member body of the city. The mayor may attend any meeting of an
368 appointed multiple-member body of the city, including executive sessions, to participate in the
18 of 51

369 discussions of that body; provided, however, that the mayor shall not have the right to vote on
370 any matter before a multiple-member body which they serve on solely by virtue of their office.
371 SECTION 3-5: APPOINTMENTS BY THE MAYOR
372 (a) Department Heads - The mayor shall appoint, subject to confirmation by the city
373 council under section 2-8, all department heads for whom no other method of appointment or
374 selection is provided by this charter. Department heads serve at the discretion of the mayor
375 subject to the limitations and requirements imposed by federal and state laws, rules or
376 regulations.
377 (b) City Attorney - The city attorney shall be appointed by the mayor, subject to
378 confirmation by the city council under section 2-8. The city attorney shall be appointed to a 2-
379 year term. Whenever the mayor appoints or reappoints a city attorney the mayor shall submit the
380 name of the person to serve as city attorney for confirmation under section 2-8, and the
381 provisions of section 2-8 shall apply to both an appointment and reappointment; provided,
382 however, that a reappointment shall not be rejected except by negative vote of not less than 8
383 members of the city council. If the mayor fails to submit the name of the person serving as the
384 city attorney for confirmation of reappointment within 30 days of the expiration of the term, the
385 city clerk shall place an item on the agenda for such reappointment at the next regular city
386 council meeting.
387 (c) Multiple-Member Bodies - The mayor shall appoint, subject to confirmation by the
388 city council under section 2-8, all members of multiple-member bodies for whom no other
389 method of appointment or selection is provided by administrative order or General Laws. All
390 members shall serve terms as defined by administrative order or General Laws. Upon the
19 of 51

391 expiration of the term of any member of a multiple-member body, a successor shall be appointed
392 in a like manner. The mayor shall fill any vacancy for the remainder of the unexpired term of any
393 member of a multiple-member body.
394 (d) Police and Fire Employees - The mayor may appoint and promote, subject to
395 confirmation by the city council, members and officers of the police department and fire
396 department subject to requirements of the commonwealth’s civil service law.
397 (e) City Employees - The method of appointment for all other city employees shall be as
398 prescribed by administrative order.
399 SECTION 3-6: APPOINTMENT OF CHIEF ADMINISTRATIVE OFFICER
400 The mayor shall appoint a chief administrative officer, subject to confirmation by the city
401 council under section 2-8, to coordinate and direct the operations and functions of municipal
402 government. . The appointee shall be chosen on the basis of appropriate administrative and
403 executive qualifications and shall have a combination of experience, training or education to
404 perform the duties of the office. The mayor shall submit the name of the person appointed to
405 serve as a chief administrative officer for confirmation undersection 2-8.
406 SECTION 3-7: TEMPORARY APPOINTMENTS TO CITY OFFICES
407 If a temporary or permanent vacancy occurs in a city office that is appointed by the
408 mayor under subsections (a) and (b) of section 3-5, the mayor may designate a person to perform
409 the duties of the office on a temporary basis for not more than 180 days until the position can be
410 filled as provided by law or by this charter. Persons serving as temporary officers under this
20 of 51

411 section shall have only those powers that are indispensable and essential to the performance of
412 the duties of the office during the period of temporary appointment and no others.
413 (a) Filing of a Temporary Appointment - When the mayor designates a person under this
414 section, the mayor shall file a certificate with the city clerk in substantially the following form:
415 “I designate [name of person] to perform the duties of the office of [office in which

593 ARTICLE 6
594 FINANCIAL PROCEDURES
595 SECTION 6-1: FISCAL YEAR
596 The fiscal year of the city shall begin on July 1 and end on June 30, unless another period

605 council and school committee, including the superintendent of schools, to review the financial

608 SECTION 6-4: SUBMISSION OF OPERATING BUDGET; BUDGET MESSAGE
609 On or about May 30 of each year, the mayor shall submit to the city council a proposed
610 operating budget for all city agencies for the next fiscal year. The proposed operating budget
611 shall include the school budget, as adopted by the school committee, which shall be submitted to
612 the mayor on or about May 15. The proposed operating budget shall be accompanied by a budget
613 message and supporting documents. The budget message shall: (i) explain the operating budget
614 in fiscal terms and in terms of work programs for all city agencies; (ii) outline the proposed fiscal
615 policies of the city for the next fiscal year; (iii) describe important features of the proposed
616 operating budget; and (iv) include any major variations from the current operating budget, fiscal
617 policies, revenues and expenditures together with reasons for these variations. The proposed
618 operating budget shall provide a complete fiscal plan of all city funds and activities and shall be
619 in the form the mayor deems desirable; provided, however, that the budget proposals relative to
620 elected officials shall identify the cost of compensation and the cost of benefits for such officials.
30 of 51

621 The mayor and the superintendent of schools shall coordinate the dates and times of the school
622 committee's budget process under the General Laws.

624 (a) Public Hearing - The city council shall hold a public hearing on the proposed
625 operating budget as submitted by the mayor. Not less than 7 days before such public hearing, the
626 city council shall post a notice stating: (i) the times and places where copies of the entire
627 proposed operating budget are available for inspection by the public; and (ii) the date, time and
628 place when a public hearing on the proposed operating budget shall be held by the city council.
629 The city council shall not act on the budget until after the public hearing has occurred.
630 (b) Adoption of the Budget - The city council shall take action on the proposed operating
631 budget, which may have amendments, before the end of the fiscal year. In amending the
632 proposed operating budget, the city council may delete or decrease amounts except expenditures
633 required by law; provided, however, that the city council shall not increase any specific item or
634 the total of the proposed operating budget except on the recommendation of the mayor or unless
635 otherwise authorized by the General Laws. If the city council fails to act on an item in the
636 proposed operating budget prior to the beginning of the fiscal year, that amount shall, without
637 any action by the city council, become a part of the appropriations for the year and be available
638 for the purposes specified.
639 (c) Availability of the Operating Budget - In addition to any other posting requirements,
640 immediately after the submission of the proposed budget to the city council, the mayor or a
641 designee shall post the entire budget document on the city website, where it shall remain posted
642 during the city council review process contained in this article. After the enactment of the
31 of 51

643 budget, the mayor or a designee shall post the final budget on the city website, where it shall
644 remain throughout the fiscal year for which it is in effect. The final budget shall reflect any
645 amendments made by the city council and approved by the mayor and shall indicate that it is the
646 final budget.
647 SECTION 6-6: CAPITAL IMPROVEMENT PROGRAM
648 (a) Submission - The mayor shall submit a capital improvement plan to the city council

658 (iv) the estimated annual cost of operating and maintaining each facility and piece of

660 (b) Public Hearing - The city council shall post a notice stating:
661 (i) the times and places where entire copies of the capital improvements plan are
662 available for the public; and
32 of 51

663 (ii) the date, time and place of a public hearing on the plan to be held by the city council

670 SECTION 6-7: INDEPENDENT AUDIT
671 The mayor shall annually provide for an outside audit of the books and accounts of the
672 city in the form of an annual comprehensive financial report to be conducted by a certified public
673 accountant or a firm of certified public accountants, which has no personal interest, direct or

682 public accountants shall make a presentation to the city council as soon as practicable after

687 intentionally expend in a fiscal year sums in excess of the appropriations, awards, grants or gifts

865 mayor by filing a notice of the waiver with an explanation and justification with the city clerk.

867 An elected official who has been convicted of a state or federal felony while holding
868 office shall be deemed to have vacated the office.
