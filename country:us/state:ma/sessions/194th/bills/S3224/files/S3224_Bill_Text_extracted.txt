7  “(c 1/2)(1) A large frontier developer shall clearly and conspicuously publish on its 
8 internet website a risk report that provides an overall assessment of the catastrophic risks posed 
9 by: (i) any frontier models the large frontier developer deploys externally; and (ii) any internally 
10 deployed frontier models with capabilities that materially exceed those of any frontier model that 
11 frontier developer has externally deployed. 
12  (2) A risk report that is required by paragraph (1) shall include, but not be limited to: 
13  (i) a summary of assessments of capabilities of the frontier models relevant to 
14 catastrophic risk, which shall address each type of catastrophic risk and describe any material 

2 of 10 15 changes to the capabilities of the frontier models relevant to each type of catastrophic risk since 
16 the most recently published risk report; 
17  (ii) a description of the key threat models the large frontier developer tracks to identify 
18 potential catastrophic risks, how observed capabilities of those frontier models relate to each 
19 threat model and key mitigations that the large frontier developer has put in place to mitigate any 
20 such identified risks; and 
21  (iii) an assessment of the residual level of each type of catastrophic risk posed by the 
22 frontier models after accounting for the mitigations implemented pursuant to clause (ii); 
23 provided, however, that the assessment shall provide sufficient information to demonstrate the 
24 evidence and reasoning behind the risk assessment and such information shall be sufficient to 
25 allow a reasonable person to reach a similar conclusion to that which the large frontier developer 
26 would reach in analyzing the level of risk posed by its frontier model. 
27  (3) A large frontier developer shall renew and update the risk report required by 
28 paragraph (1) not less than every 180 days and include in each update a comparison of the 
29 assessed level of each type of catastrophic risk to the level assessed in the previously published 
30 risk report.”; and 
31  in said section 106, in said proposed chapter 93M, by inserting after section 3 the 
32 following section:- 
33  “Section 3A. (a) A large frontier developer shall annually retain a third party to perform 
34 an independent audit of compliance with the requirements of section 2 except for subsection (c 
35 1/2) of said section 2. The third party shall conduct audits consistent with generally accepted 
36 auditing standards and best practices and shall possess demonstrated competence to perform the 

3 of 10 37 audit, including experience employing or contracting with individuals who possess technical 
38 expertise in the safety of frontier models. 
39  (1) The third party shall be granted access to all materials reasonably necessary to comply 
40 with the third party’s obligations under this section, including, but not limited to, all unredacted 
41 versions of materials published pursuant to this chapter. To protect the large frontier developer’s 
42 trade secrets and confidential business information, cybersecurity, national security of the United 
43 States or public safety, a large frontier developer may impose reasonable security protocols on 
44 the third party, including, but not limited to, restrictions on note taking, copying, retaining, or 
45 removing materials, requirements for on-premises review and confidentiality requirements. 
46  (2) The third party shall produce a report that includes, but is not limited to: 
47  (i) a description of whether the large frontier developer has substantially complied with 
48 the requirements of section 2, except for subsection (c 1/2) of said section 2; 
49  (ii) when applicable, a description of material deviations from the requirements of section 
50 2, except for subsection (c 1/2) of said section 2, an explanation of any deviation and its rationale 
51 and any recommendations for how the developer can improve its policies and processes for 
52 ensuring compliance; 
53  (iii) a detailed assessment of the large frontier developer’s internal controls, including its 
54 designation and empowerment of senior personnel responsible for such implementation by the 
55 large frontier developer, its employees and its contractors; 
56  (iv) a list of the third party’s personnel involved in the audit; 

4 of 10 57  (v) the third party’s procedures for managing conflicts of interest and any conflicts of 
58 interest of any personnel involved in the audit; 
59  (vi) the methodology of the audit and the nature of the information reviewed by the third 
60 party to conduct the audit; and 
61  (vii) the signature of the lead auditor certifying the results of the audit. 
62  (3) The large frontier developer shall retain an unredacted copy of the report for the 
63 duration of the frontier model’s deployment plus 5 years. 
64  (4) Not later than 30 days after receiving the audit report, the large frontier developer 
65 shall conspicuously publish on its website a high-level summary of the audit findings and a copy 
66 of the third party’s report with redactions as provided in subsection (f) of section 2 and transmit a 
67 copy of the redacted report to the attorney general. 
68  (b)(1) A large frontier developer shall engage at least 1 third party to conduct an 
69 independent evaluation of the developer’s frontier models with respect to each category of 
70 catastrophic risk. A large frontier developer shall engage a third party to conduct its evaluation 
71 not more than 30 days after publishing each risk report under subsection (c 1/2) of section 2 and 
72 in any event shall conduct an independent evaluation not less than once every 120 days. 
73  (2) An independent evaluation conducted pursuant to paragraph (1) shall include, but not 
74 be limited to:- 
75  (i) an independent assessment of each type of catastrophic risk posed by the large frontier 
76 developer’s frontier models, taking into account model capabilities, applicable threat models and 
77 the large frontier developer's safeguards; 

5 of 10 78  (ii) an assessment of the ability of the large frontier developer’s frontier models to 
79 automate artificial intelligence research and development and any increased potential for 
80 catastrophic risks or challenges to risk monitoring, assessment or mitigation resulting from such 
81 ability; 
82  (iii) a review of the large frontier developer’s most recent risk report published pursuant 
83 to subsection (c 1/2) of section 2 including an assessment of: (A) the adequacy and completeness 
84 of the information disclosed in the risk report; (B) the analytical rigor of the frontier developer’s 
85 risk methodology; (C) the appropriateness and materiality of any redactions made to the publicly 
86 available version of the risk report; and (D) whether the third party disagrees with any of the 
87 report’s claims, including the overall assessment of the level of risk for each catastrophic risk; 
88  (iv) a list of the third party’s personnel involved in the evaluation; 
89  (v) the third party’s procedures for managing conflicts of interest and any conflicts of 
90 interest of any personnel involved in the evaluation; 
91  (vi) the methodology of the evaluation and the nature of the information reviewed by the 
92 third party to conduct the evaluation; and 
93  (vii) the signature of the lead evaluator certifying the results of the evaluation. 
94  (3)(A) The third party shall be granted access to all materials reasonably necessary to 
95 comply with the evaluator’s obligations under this section, including, but not limited to, all 
96 unredacted versions of materials published pursuant to this chapter and the large frontier 
97 developer’s most capable frontier models. 

6 of 10 98  (B) The third party shall have the opportunity to ask relevant questions about the frontier 
99 developer’s frontier models, likelihood of catastrophic risks and related safeguards and the large 
100 frontier developer shall provide reasonable responses. 
101  (C) To protect the frontier developer’s trade secrets and confidential business 
102 information, cybersecurity, national security of the United States or public safety, a frontier 
103 developer may impose reasonable security protocols on the third party including, but not limited 
104 to, restrictions on note taking, copying, retaining or removing materials, requirements for on-
105 premise review and confidentiality requirements. 
106  (4) The third party shall publish a public version of its report not later than 30 days after 
107 delivering the report to the large frontier developer and the large frontier developer shall 
108 conspicuously publish a link to the report on its internet website. The public version may be 
109 redacted only as provided in subsection (f) of section 2. 
110  (5) A large frontier developer may comply with this subsection by engaging multiple 
111 third parties focused on different categories of catastrophic risk or different aspects of the 
112 requirements of this subsection; provided, however, that: (i) collectively, the third parties 
113 perform all of the duties required by this subsection; (ii) each third party satisfies the 
114 independence and qualification requirements of this section; and (iii) each third party includes in 
115 its report a clear and specific statement of the scope of the assessment and the duties performed. 
116  (c)(1)(A) A third party engaged under this section shall have no financial, operational or 
117 management dependence on the large frontier developer or any of the large frontier developer's 
118 affiliates and shall be otherwise free from the large frontier developer's control in reaching 

7 of 10 119 conclusions or making recommendations, including through contractual safeguards and conflict 
120 of interest policies. 
121  (B) If no other source of funding has been established pursuant to clause (iii) of 
122 paragraph (1) of subsection (d), a large frontier developer may compensate the third party at 
123 reasonable market rates and shall not condition any payment or the amount of any payment on 
124 the results of the third party’s audit or evaluation. 
125  (2) Prior to accepting any engagement under this section, the third party shall certify in 
126 writing to the large frontier developer and the attorney general that the third party satisfies the 
127 independence requirements of this subsection. The certification shall include the third party’s 
128 sources of funding and remuneration for the engagement, any other current or recent 
129 engagements with the large frontier developer or its affiliates and any other facts that could 
130 reasonably be expected to bear on the third party’s independence. 
131  (d)(1) The attorney general, in consultation with academic institutions, nonprofit 
132 organizations and industry stakeholders, shall implement an independent evaluation ecosystem 
133 plan by: (i) developing and publishing standards for the qualification of qualified independent 
134 third party evaluators; (ii) exploring a licensing system to qualify third party evaluators; (iii) 
135 subject to government appropriation, providing government funding or arranging pooled funding 
136 to supplement other sources of evaluator funding; (iv) exploring the feasibility and benefits of 
137 licensing qualified independent evaluators to assess the adherence of artificial intelligence 
138 models to standards reflecting best practices for the prevention of personal injury, property 
139 damage and other harms that do not meet the definition of catastrophic risk, and reporting its 
140 findings and any recommendations regarding such licensing to the joint committee on advanced 

8 of 10 141 information technology, the internet and cybersecurity and the joint committee on economic 
142 development; and (v) providing resources and funding for nascent organizations seeking to 
143 become third party evaluators. 
144  (2) The attorney general may develop and publish a rating system for qualified third 
145 parties based on predefined criteria, including the rigor and quality of the evaluator's published 
146 reasoning and analysis, the thoroughness of the evaluator's methodology, the evaluator's track 
147 record of identifying material risks or deficiencies and stakeholder feedback, including from 
148 frontier developers, academic reviewers and the public.”; and
