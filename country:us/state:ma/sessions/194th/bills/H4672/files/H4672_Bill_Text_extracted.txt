2 of 10 
 13  Section 1E 1/2. (a) As used in this section and section 1E 3/4, the following words shall, 
14 unless the context clearly requires otherwise, have the following meanings: 
15  “Evidence-based literacy instruction”, literacy instruction that is: (i) grounded in 
16 scientific research methods; and (ii) demonstrated through well-designed and well-implemented 
17 studies to produce significant and positive effects on student learning outcomes.  
18  “Five research-based areas in reading instruction”, reading instruction that includes, but 
19 shall not be limited to, phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary and comprehension. 
20  “High-quality curriculum”, curriculum reviewed and approved by the department. 
21  (b) No curricula selected to meet a curriculum framework pursuant to section 1E for 
22 kindergarten through grade 3 literacy shall include implicit and incidental instruction in word 
23 reading, visual memorization of whole words, guessing from context and picture cues, also 
24 known as MSV or three-cueing. 
25  (c) Districts shall select high-quality curricula to meet the curriculum frameworks 
26 pursuant to section 1E for kindergarten through grade 3 literacy that: (i) include instruction in the 
27 five research-based areas in reading instruction; and (ii) utilize and are based upon evidence-
28 based literacy instruction. 
29  (d)(1) A district may apply to the department for a waiver to use kindergarten through 
30 grade 3 literacy instruction curriculum that does not appear on the department’s list of high-
31 quality curricula. 

3 of 10 
 32  (2) In determining whether to approve a district’s waiver for a curriculum that is not 
33 already on the department’s list of high-quality curricula, the department shall assess whether the 
34 district’s curriculum utilizes evidence-based literacy instruction. 
35  (e)(1) A district may apply to the department for a waiver to use a kindergarten through 
36 grade 3 literacy instruction curriculum pilot program that utilizes curricula that the department 
37 has not yet reviewed to determine if it is evidence-based literacy instruction. 
38  (2) A district shall report to the department: (i) the degree to which the pilot’s 
39 instructional approach is supported by some degree of scientific research; (ii) the methodology of 
40 the pilot; (iii) the population that was instructed using the pilot curriculum and any demographic 
41 differences between that population and the district’s student body at large; (iv) the duration of 
42 any such pilot; and (v) any other considerations that the department deems relevant to the quality 
43 and fairness of the pilot program’s execution and impact on student learning. 
44  (3) In determining whether to approve a district’s application to participate in a pilot 
45 program pursuant to this subsection, the department shall assess the quality of the proposed pilot 
46 program, including, information received pursuant to paragraph (2). 
47  (4) Curriculum for a pilot program that is assessed and approved by the department and 
48 deemed to meet the requirements of this section shall be allowed to continue as an approved 
49 literacy curriculum used for instruction in the district unless the pilot program is rescinded 
50 pursuant to paragraph (7). 
51  (5) A district that participates in a pilot program pursuant to this subsection shall publicly 
52 post to its website any report submitted to the department with information required pursuant to 
53 paragraph (2) and curriculum samples.

 54  (6) The department may audit approved pilot programs at any time. The audit shall 
55 include, but shall not be limited to: (i) an in-depth review of curriculum materials; (ii) 
56 instructional documentation; and (iii) instructional practices in the classroom. 
57  (7) The department may rescind approval of a pilot program based on audit findings 
58 before the expiration of the pilot program. 
59  Section 1E ¾. (a) The department shall provide tools and resources to aid districts in 
60 providing professional development aligned with evidence-based literacy instruction for literacy 
61 teachers, paraprofessionals and reading specialists in kindergarten through grade 3.  
62  (b) The department shall create and maintain: 
63  (i) a list of department-approved professional development programs and vendors aligned 
64 with evidence-based literacy instruction best practices; and  
65  (ii) a set of online training modules available and free to all teachers that provide training 
66 on the foundational practices and pedagogy aligned with evidence-based literacy instruction best 
67 practices.

125  SECTION 10. Said chapter 71 is hereby further amended by inserting after section 57A 
126 the following 2 sections:- 
127  Section 57B. (a) For the purposes of this section and section 57C, the term “screening 
128 assessments” shall mean tools and assessments that can be used to identify students at risk for 
129 poor academic outcomes as determined by the department. 
130  (b) The department, in consultation with the department of early education and care, shall 
131 issue guidelines to districts requiring screening procedures and protocols for reading ability and 
132 progress in literacy skills for all students in kindergarten through grade 3 using screening 
133 assessments. The guidelines shall include a threshold at which a student is determined to be 
134 “significantly below” the grade level benchmark. The commissioner shall annually publish data 
135 on which screening assessments are employed by each district to conduct reading screening. 
136  Section 57C. (a) Each district shall, not less than twice per year, assess each student’s 
137 reading ability and progress in literacy skills from kindergarten through at least grade 3, using a 

8 of 10 
 138 screening assessment approved by the department. The screening assessment of the student’s 
139 reading ability and progress shall be consistent with section 2 of chapter 71B, the department’s 
140 dyslexia guidelines issued pursuant to section 57A and literacy guidelines issued pursuant to 
141 section 57B. 
142  (b) If a screening assessment determines that a student is significantly below relevant 
143 grade level benchmarks in specific literacy skills, the district shall determine which actions 
144 within the general education program will meet the student’s needs, including, but not limited to, 
145 differentiated or supplementary evidence-based reading instruction and ongoing monitoring of 
146 progress. Not later than 30 days after a screening result that determines a student is significantly 
147 below the relevant grade level benchmark, the district shall inform the student’s parent or 
148 guardian of the screening results and the school’s response and shall offer the opportunity for a 
149 follow-up discussion.

152  SECTION 290. (a) As used in this section, “high-needs district” shall mean a school or 
153 district with a high percentage of low-income students and English learners, which may include a 
154 school or district implementing turnaround plans.  
155  (b) The department of elementary and secondary education shall, subject to 
156 appropriation, develop and administer a pilot program, in partnership with a college, university, 
157 school, district or other institution offering a educator preparation program approved pursuant to 
158 section 38G of chapter 71 of the General Laws, for teacher apprenticeships during which 
159 participants shall complete a paid teaching apprenticeship for school years in a high-needs

 160 district in a classroom with a highly proficient mentor teacher. The department of elementary and 
161 secondary education shall determine qualifications of a highly proficient mentor teacher using 
162 factors, including, but not limited to, recent evaluation ratings, student growth data and 
163 demonstrated evidence-based practices. The program shall provide funds to candidates to support 
164 earning a competitive wage, including, but not limited to, support for childcare, transportation, 
165 textbooks and other core expenses, to ensure a diverse cohort are able to fully participate. 
166  (c) The department of elementary and secondary education shall promulgate regulations 
167 defining the apprenticeship and the target candidates to be admitted to the program. 
168  (d)(1) Not later than 1 year after the completion of the pilot program, the department of 
169 elementary and secondary education shall file a report with the clerks of the house of 
170 representatives and the senate and the joint committee on education on the outcome of the pilot 
171 program. The report shall include, but shall not be limited to: (i) data on the demographics of 
172 participants, participants’ licensure pass rates on the MTEL and hiring rates; and (ii) an 
173 assessment of the feasibility of applying for status as a United States Registered Apprenticeship. 
174  (2) Not later than 3 years after the completion of the pilot program, the department of 
175 elementary and secondary education shall file a report with the clerks of the house of 
176 representatives and the senate and the joint committee on education, which shall include, but 
177 shall not be limited to, an evaluation of the impact of apprentices on student outcomes as 
178 compared to teachers not participating in the pilot program using student growth measures and

180 candidates or all candidates in high-needs districts.
