1  SECTION 1. Chapter 6 of the General Laws is hereby amended by inserting after section 
2 116K the following section:- 
3  Section 116L. (a) The municipal police training committee, in consultation with the 
4 executive office of aging and independence, the Alzheimer’s Disease and Related Disorders 
5 Association, Massachusetts Chapter, Inc., the Massachusetts Coalition of Police, Inc., 
6 Massachusetts Chiefs of Police Association and the Massachusetts Police Association, Inc., shall 
7 establish and implement a dementia training program for law enforcement officers. The 
8 committee may consult with other appropriate organizations and agencies with an interest and 
9 expertise in Alzheimer’s disease and other dementias, or those representing or working with first 
10 responders. The dementia training program shall include, but shall not be limited to: (i) 
11 instruction on the identification of people with Alzheimer’s disease and other dementias; (ii) 
12 risks such as wandering and elder abuse; and (iii) the best practices for interacting with people 
13 with Alzheimer’s disease and other dementias. 

2 of 16 14  (b) All law enforcement officers shall complete not less than 2 hours of initial training on 
15 dementia within the recruit basic training curriculum. The 2-hour dementia training program 
16 shall provide training on: 
17  (i) dementia and symptoms associated with dementia; 
18  (ii) communication issues, including how to communicate respectfully and effectively 
19 with the individual who has dementia to determine the most appropriate response and effective 
20 communication techniques to enhance collaboration with caregivers; 
21  (iii) techniques for understanding and approaching behavioral symptoms and identifying 
22 alternatives to physical restraints; 
23  (iv) identifying and reporting incidents of abuse, neglect and exploitation to the 
24 protective services system established in section 16 of chapter 19A; 
25  (v) protocols for contacting caregivers when a person with dementia is found wandering, 
26 or during emergency or crisis situations; and 
27  (vi) local caregiving resources that are available for people living with dementia. 
28  (c) All law enforcement officers shall complete not less than 1 hour of biannual in-service 
29 education covering dementia training. The biannual in-service training shall qualify toward the 
30 minimum credit hours required for in-service education.

145  SECTION 6. Said chapter 111 is hereby further amended by inserting after section 53H 
146 the following section:- 
147  Section 53I. (a) The department shall require acute care hospitals to allow a family 
148 member or other caregiver for patients with Alzheimer’s disease or other dementias, or for 
149 patients exhibiting symptoms of dementia or cognitive impairment, or a patient who presents 
150 with symptoms of dementia or cognitive impairment based on the assessment of the health care 
151 provider overseeing their care in the hospital, to remain with the patient at all times that are 
152 medically appropriate, including, but not limited to, while in the emergency department and 
153 while admitted as an inpatient. Caregivers for patients living with Alzheimer’s disease or other 
154 dementias shall not be required to adhere to restricted hospital visiting hours, unless it has been 
155 deemed unsafe for the patient, family member or caregiver. 
156  (b) The department shall require acute care hospitals to create policies and protocols to 
157 ensure that a family member, caregiver or personal legal representative responsible for a patient 
158 with Alzheimer’s disease or other dementia or a patient who presents with symptoms of 
159 dementia or cognitive impairment based on the assessment of the health care provider overseeing 

9 of 16 160 their care in the hospital is contacted as soon as possible following admission to the emergency 
161 department or hospital if the patient presents to the hospital without a family member, caregiver 
162 or personal legal representative; provided, however, that the hospital shall only contact a family 
163 member, caregiver or personal legal representative: (i) if the hospital has received consent from 
164 the patient, if possible, to do so; and (ii) to the extent consistent with federal and state law or 
165 regulation, and in the reasonable judgment of the hospital. If the patient is incapacitated or not 
166 able to provide consent, a health care provider may share the patient’s information with a family 
167 member, caregiver or personal legal representative responsible for a patient with Alzheimer’s 
168 disease or other dementia as long as the health care provider determines, based on their 
169 professional judgment, that it is in the best interest of the patient.  
170  (c) The department shall require acute care hospitals to create policies and protocols to 
171 ensure that a family member, caregiver or personal legal representative responsible for a patient 
172 living with Alzheimer’s disease or other dementia or a patient who presents with symptoms of 
173 dementia or cognitive impairment based on the assessment of the health care provider overseeing 
174 their care in the hospital is contacted prior to the patient’s discharge to ensure a safe discharge, 
175 including suitable transport from the hospital, and review the discharge plan; provided, however, 
176 that the hospital shall only contact a family member, caregiver or personal legal representative: 
177 (i) if the hospital has received consent from the patient, if possible, to do so; and (ii) to the extent 
178 consistent with federal and state law or regulation, and in the reasonable judgment of the 
179 hospital. If the patient is incapacitated or not able to provide consent, a health care provider may 
180 contact a family member, caregiver or personal legal representative responsible for the patient 
181 with Alzheimer’s disease or other dementia as long as the health care provider determines, based 
182 on their professional judgment, that it is in the best interest of the patient. If a family member, 

10 of 16 183 caregiver or personal legal representative is not able to be contacted or if the patient declines 
184 contact, the patient living with Alzheimer’s disease or other dementia shall meet with a hospital 
185 social worker or other professional who can assess for discharge safety and other supports 
186 needed prior to discharge. 
187  (d) Notwithstanding subsections (a) to (c), inclusive, this section shall not apply during a 
188 public health emergency or state of emergency declared by the governor relevant to the actions 
189 of the hospital. 
190  (e) The department shall promulgate regulations to implement this section.

197  SECTION 8. Said chapter 111 is hereby further amended by adding the following 3 
198 sections:- 
199  Section 250. (a) The department, in consultation with the executive office of aging and 
200 independence, the advisory council on Alzheimer’s disease research and treatment established 
201 pursuant to subsection (b) of section 16AA of chapter 6A and any additional stakeholders as 
202 determined by the department, shall develop a public awareness campaign on brain health, 
203 Alzheimer’s disease and other dementias. The department shall incorporate the public awareness 
204 campaign into existing and relevant public health outreach programs. 

11 of 16 205  (b)(1) The public awareness campaign on brain health, Alzheimer’s disease and other 
206 dementias shall: 
207  (i) educate health care providers on: (A) the importance of early detection and timely 
208 diagnosis of cognitive impairment; (B) validated cognitive assessment tools; (C) current and 
209 emerging treatment options; (D) the value of a Medicare Annual Wellness visit for cognitive 
210 health; and (E) the Medicare and Medicaid care planning billing codes for individuals with 
211 cognitive impairment;  
212  (ii) increase public understanding and awareness of: (A) early warning signs of 
213 Alzheimer’s disease and other types of dementia; (B) the value of early detection and diagnosis; 
214 and (C) how to reduce the risk of cognitive decline, particularly among persons in diverse 
215 communities who are at greater risk of developing Alzheimer’s disease and other types of 
216 dementia; and  
217  (iii) inform health care professionals and the general public of: (A) dementia care 
218 coordination services for those living with Alzheimer’s disease and other dementias; and (B) 
219 other resources and services available to individuals living with dementia and their families and 
220 caregivers. 
221  (2) For the materials for the general public, the department shall provide uniform and 
222 consistent guidance on Alzheimer’s disease and other dementia in nonclinical terms. The 
223 department shall: (i) ensure cultural relevancy and health literacy in developing materials; and 
224 (ii) provide the information to diverse populations who are at higher risk for developing 
225 dementia.  

12 of 16 226  (c) The department shall include the federal Centers for Disease Control and Prevention’s 
227 Healthy Aging Program’s modules on subjective cognitive decline and caregiving in the annual 
228 behavioral risk factor surveillance system survey on a rotating annual basis to collect prevalence 
229 data on Alzheimer’s disease and other dementias, track trends over time and analyze data to 
230 direct public health programs and resources. 
231  Section 251. As used in this section, “Healthy Brain Initiative Road Map” shall mean the 
232 federal Centers for Disease Control and Prevention’s collaborative approach to fully integrate 
233 cognitive health into public health practice and reduce the risk and impact of Alzheimer’s disease 
234 and other dementias. 
235  Biannually, the department shall report on the department’s work on the Healthy Brain 
236 Initiative Road Map. The department shall submit the report to the clerks of the house of 
237 representatives and the senate, the joint committee on public health and to the advisory council 
238 on Alzheimer’s disease research and treatment established pursuant to subsection (b) of section 
239 16AA of chapter 6A. 
240  Section 252. (a) Not less than every 5 years, each hospital licensed pursuant to section 51 
241 shall complete an operational plan for the recognition and management of patients with dementia 
242 or delirium in acute-care settings and submit the plan to the department for approval. Not later 
243 than 90 days of receipt, the department shall review the plan and approve the plan or offer 
244 amendments to the plan. Upon final approval of the plan by the department, the hospital shall 
245 implement the plan.  
246  (b) The operational plan shall include: (i) education and training of clinical and non-
247 clinical staff; (ii) providing a dementia- and delirium-appropriate environment; (iii) recognition 

13 of 16 248 of dementia and delirium; (iv) patient management and treatment, including, but not limited to, 
249 how to manage symptoms, treatment protocols and side effect management; (v) transition 
250 planning to improve and provide safe admissions, transfers and discharges, including, but not 
251 limited to, protocols to ensure that patients living with dementia are safe and have a staff 
252 member or caregiver present during discharge or transfer; (vi) advance care planning 
253 information; (vii) caregiver communication and coordination, including, but not limited to, 
254 protocols to ensure that contact to a caregiver has been attempted upon arrival and prior to 
255 discharge if the patient agrees; and (viii) additional recommendations made by the Alzheimer’s 
256 and related dementias acute care advisory committee established pursuant to section 1 of chapter 
257 228 of the acts of 2014 and any additional guidance issued by the Massachusetts Health and 
258 Hospital Association, Inc. or the department. 
259  (c) Each hospital may update the operational plan more frequently as needed. 
260  (d) Each hospital shall provide a copy of its operational plan to the advisory council on 
261 Alzheimer’s disease research and treatment established pursuant to subsection (b) of section 
262 16AA of chapter 6A upon approval. 
263  (e) Each hospital shall provide an electronic copy of its operational plan to each 
264 employee upon approval by the department. Upon request by an employee the hospital shall 
265 provide a printed copy of the plan to its employees, 
266  (f) Each hospital shall keep a copy of the operational plan on file and shall make it 
267 available for review by the public by posting it to the hospital’s website.

300  Section 9D 1/2. (a) As used in this section, the term “dementia care coordination” shall

302 caregiver. 
303  (b) The division shall require all senior care options plans and one care plans to provide 
304 coverage for dementia care coordination services for senior care options and one care members

314 including, but not limited to, symptom management strategies, communication techniques,

316 appropriate community support services. 
317  (e) Senior care options plans and one care plans may contract with community partners or 
318 directly provide dementia care coordination services to their members.
