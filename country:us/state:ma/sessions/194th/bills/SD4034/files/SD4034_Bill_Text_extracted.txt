Pursuant to subsection (e) of Section 2EEEEEE of Chapter 29 of the Massachusetts General
Laws, as inserted by Chapter 214 of the Acts of 2024, An Act to Provide for Competitiveness and
Infrastructure Investment in Massachusetts (the “Act”) and with Section 3 of the Act, I write to
report on the activities of and expenditures from the Commonwealth Federal Match and Debt
Reduction Fund (the “Fund”) for debt defeasance, technical assistance, and matching funds for
federal programs as of June 2026.
State Match
The Act aims to enhance the competitiveness of Massachusetts in winning federal funds and to
provide for the infrastructure needs of the Commonwealth. It makes up to $750 million available
in the form of matching funds and technical assistance for Massachusetts entities competing for
federal funds. It also provides tools to help reduce the Commonwealth’s indebtedness.
The Executive Office for Administration and Finance (“A&F”) has effectively leveraged this
historic legislation to support critical investments in Massachusetts. To date, we have made
commitments to or advanced the review of applicants to the Fund that further key state policy
goals. This activity includes, but is not limited to, more than $336 million toward economic
development projects, more than $188 million toward transportation projects, and more than
$135 million toward climate-focused projects.

Additionally, $128 million of the total funds we have committed or advanced in review are
dedicated to municipalities across the state to support their efforts to make our roads safer, our
firefighters better equipped to respond to emergencies, and our schools more energy efficient,
among other priorities.

Reducing the Commonwealth’s Indebtedness
The Act also authorizes expenditures from the Fund for reducing indebtedness through varied
means, including the defeasance of outstanding bonds. A&F, working closely with the MBTA
and the Office of the State Treasurer and Receiver-General, strategically leveraged this
authorization to pay off approximately $386 million in outstanding debt, resulting in recurring
operating budget savings for the MBTA and the Commonwealth. Commonwealth general
obligation bonds are used to fund various capital projects throughout Massachusetts and
Commonwealth-guaranteed general transportation system (GTS) bonds were legacy debt, issued
by the MBTA in an earlier era. GTS bonds are no longer issued by the MBTA. In addition to
generating budgetary savings, these bond defeasances also reduced the Commonwealth’s overall
long-term liabilities, which rating agencies and investors consider when analyzing the state’s
credit.
