55 SECTION 16. Said chapter 114 is hereby amended by striking out section 43M, as so
56 appearing, and inserting in place thereof the following section:-
57 Section 43M. Except as otherwise provided by law, or in case of a dead body being
58 rightfully carried through or removed from the commonwealth for the purpose of burial or
59 disposition elsewhere, every dead body of a human being dying within the commonwealth, and
60 the remains of any body after dissection therein, shall be decently buried, entombed in a
61 mausoleum, vault or tomb or disposed of by cremation or natural organic reduction within a
62 reasonable time after death. The permanent disposition of such bodies or remains shall be by
63 interment in the earth or deposit in a chamber, vault or tomb of a cemetery owned, maintained
64 and operated in accordance with the laws of this commonwealth, by deposit in a crypt of a
65 mausoleum, or by cremation or natural organic reduction. The remains of a human body after
66 cremation or natural organic reduction may be deposited in a niche of a columbarium or a crypt
67 of a mausoleum, buried or disposed of in any manner not contrary to law. Notwithstanding any
68 general or special law to the contrary, a funeral establishment in possession of the remains of a
69 human body which is not claimed by a next-of-kin or duly authorized representative within 12
70 months after the date of cremation or natural organic reduction may have the remains interred or
71 placed in a common grave, niche or crypt in a cemetery, or scattered in an area of the cemetery
72 designated for that purpose; provided, however, that if the deceased is a veteran of the United
73 States Armed Forces the deceased shall be interred at a veterans' cemetery. Each cemetery and
74 funeral establishment shall maintain permanent records of such disposition. There shall be no
4 of 7

75 liability for a funeral establishment, cemetery or corporation described in section 6, or any
76 employee or agent thereof that disposes of unclaimed remains in accordance with this section.
77 Each municipality or cemetery corporation shall maintain records which identify the name, if
78 known, of the dead human body or remains in each burial lot, tomb or vault under its control. No
79 deposit of the bodies or remains of the human dead shall be made in a single chamber, vault or
80 tomb wholly or partly above the natural surface of the ground unless the part thereof below such
81 surface is of a permanent character, constructed of materials capable of withstanding extreme
82 climatic conditions, waterproof and air tight, and capable of being sealed permanently to prevent
83 all escape of effluvia, and unless the part thereof above the natural surface of the ground is
84 constructed of natural stone of a standard not less than that required by the United States
85 government for monuments erected in national cemeteries, of durability sufficient to withstand
86 all conditions of weather.
87 Notwithstanding any general or special law to the contrary and for the purposes of this
88 section, a board of health may serve as the duly authorized representative for the purpose of
89 requesting cremation, or natural organic reduction of unclaimed remains by signing a form under
90 the following circumstances: (i) the unclaimed remains shall be in a location that is within the
91 jurisdiction of the board of health; (ii) the board of health has received notice from a licensed
92 funeral director that either no person has come forward to claim the remains or that no person
93 may legally claim the remains; provided, however, that the board of health shall wait 30 days
94 after such notification under this clause prior to signing the form. The unclaimed remains shall
95 then be viewed by a medical examiner or forensic investigator designated by the chief medical
96 examiner pursuant to section 14 of chapter 38, who shall authorize the cremation or natural
97 organic reduction only when no further examination or judicial inquiry concerning the death is

98 necessary. The office of the chief medical examiner may waive the fee set forth in said section
99 14 of said chapter 38 for cremation authorizations pursuant to this section. There shall be no
100 liability for a board of health or an employee, agent, or licensee thereof that authorizes the
101 disposal of unclaimed remains in accordance with this section. Nothing in this section shall
102 supersede the obligations of the office of the chief medical examiner as set forth in this chapter
103 and chapter 38.
104 SECTION 17. Said chapter 114 is hereby further amended by striking out section 44 and
105 inserting in place thereof the following section:-
106 Section 44. Cremation or natural organic reduction shall not be conducted upon the body
107 of a deceased person within 48 hours after the person’s decease, unless the person died of a
108 contagious or infectious disease. If the death occurred within the commonwealth, cremation or
109 natural organic reduction shall not be conducted upon the body by any corporation described in
110 section 6 until the corporation’s officers have received (i) the certificate or burial permit required
111 by law before burial; and (ii) a certificate from a medical examiner or similarly authorized
112 person who has viewed the body and made personal inquiry into the cause and manner of death
113 and is of opinion that no further examination or judicial inquiry concerning the same is
114 necessary. If the death occurs without the commonwealth, the medical examiner's certificate may
115 be provided by a medical examiner or similarly authorized person in whose jurisdiction the death
116 occurred or the reception and cremation or natural organic reduction of the body of a deceased
117 person shall be governed by a by-law or regulation made or approved by the department of
118 public health as provided in section 9.
