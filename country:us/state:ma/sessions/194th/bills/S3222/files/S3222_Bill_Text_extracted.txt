2  "SECTION 109B. The General Laws, as appearing in the 2024 Official Edition, are 
3 hereby amended by inserting after chapter 112A the following new chapter:-  
4   Chapter 112C. Physical Therapy Licensure Compact   
5  Section 1. The purpose of this compact is to facilitate interstate practice of physical 
6 therapy with the goal of improving public access to physical therapy services. The practice of 
7 physical therapy occurs in the state where the client is located at the time of the client encounter. 
8 The compact preserves the regulatory authority of states to protect public health and safety 
9 through the current system of state licensure.  
10  This compact is designed to achieve the following objectives: (a) increase public access 
11 to physical therapy services by providing for the mutual recognition of other member state 
12 licenses; (b) enhance the states’ ability to protect the public’s health and safety; (c) encourage the 
13 cooperation of member states in regulating multi-state physical therapy practice; (d) 
14 support  spouses of relocating military members; (e) enhance the exchange of licensure, 

2 of 21 15 investigative and disciplinary information between member states; and (f) allow a remote state to 
16 hold a provider of services with a compact privilege in that state accountable to that state’s 
17 practice standards.  
18  Section 2. As used in this chapter, the following words shall have the following meanings 
19 unless the context requires otherwise:  
20  “Active-Duty Military”, full-time duty status in the active uniformed service of the 
21 United States, including members of the National Guard and Reserve on active duty orders 
22 pursuant to 10 U.S.C. Section 1209 and 1211.  
23  “Adverse Action”, disciplinary action taken by a physical therapy licensing board based 
24 upon misconduct, unacceptable performance or a combination of both.  
25   “Alternative Program”, a non-disciplinary monitoring or practice remediation process 
26 approved by a physical therapy licensing board. This includes, but is not limited to, substance 
27 abuse issues.  
28  “Compact privilege”, the authorization granted by a remote state to allow a licensee from 
29 another member state to practice as a physical therapist or work as a physical therapist assistant 
30 in the remote state under its laws and rules. The practice of physical therapy occurs in the 
31 member state where the client is located at the time of the patient/client encounter.  
32  “Continuing competence”, a requirement, as a condition of license renewal, to provide 
33 evidence of participation inor completion of, educational and professional activities relevant to 
34 practice or area of work.  

3 of 21 35  “Data system”, a repository of information about licensees, including examination, 
36 licensure, investigative, compact privilege and adverse action.  
37  “Encumbered license”, a license that a physical therapy licensing board has limited in any 
38 way.  
39  “Executive Board”, a group of directors elected or appointed to act on behalf of, and 
40 within the powers granted to them by, the commission.  
41  “Home state”, the member state that is the licensee’s primary state of residence.  
42  “Investigative information” information, records and documents received or generated by 
43 a physical therapy licensing board pursuant to an investigation.  
44  “Jurisprudence Requirement”, the assessment of an individual’s knowledge of the laws 
45 and rules governing the practice of physical therapy in a state.   
46  “Licensee”, an individual who currently holds an authorization from the state to practice 
47 as a physical therapist or to work as a physical therapist assistant.  
48  “Member state”, a state that has enacted the compact.  
49  “Party state”, any member state in which a licensee holds a current license or compact 
50 privilege or is applying for a license or compact privilege.  
51  “Physical therapist”, an individual who is licensed by a state to practice physical therapy.  
52  “Physical therapist assistant”, an individual who is licensed by a state and who assists the 
53 physical therapist in selected components of physical therapy.  

4 of 21 54  “Physical therapy”, the care and services provided by or under the direction and 
55 supervision of a licensed physical therapist.  
56  “Physical Therapy Compact Commission” or “Commission”, the national administrative 
57 body whose membership consists of all states that have enacted the compact.  
58  “Physical therapy licensing board” or “licensing board”, the agency of a state that is 
59 responsible for the licensing and regulation of physical therapists and physical therapist 
60 assistants.    
61  “Remote State”, a member state other than the home state, where a licensee is exercising 
62 or seeking to exercise the compact privilege.  
63  “Rule”, a regulation, principle or directive promulgated by the commission that has the 
64 force of law.  
65  “State”, any state, commonwealth, district or territory of the United States that regulates 
66 the practice of physical therapy.  
67  Section 3. (a) To participate in the compact, a state must: (i) participate fully in the 
68 commission’s data system, including using the commission’s unique identifier as defined in 
69 rules; (ii) have a mechanism in place for receiving and investigating complaints about licensees; 
70 (iii) notify the commission, in compliance with the terms of the compact and rules, of any 
71 adverse action or the availability of investigative information regarding a licensee; (iv) fully 
72 implement a criminal background check requirement, within a time frame established by rule, by 
73 receiving the results of the Federal Bureau of Investigation record search on criminal background 
74 checks and use the results in making licensure decisions in accordance with section 3; (v) 

5 of 21 75 comply with the rules of the commission; (vi) utilize a recognized national examination as a 
76 requirement for licensure pursuant to the rules of the commission; and (vii) have continuing 
77 competence requirements as a condition for license renewal.  
78  (b) Upon adoption of this statute, the member state shall have the authority to obtain 
79 biometric-based information from each physical therapy licensure applicant and submit this 
80 information to the Federal Bureau of Investigation for a criminal background check in 
81 accordance with 28 U.S.C. §534 and 42 U.S.C. §14616.  
82  (c) A member state shall grant the compact privilege to a licensee holding a valid 
83 unencumbered license in another member state in accordance with the terms of the compact and 
84 rules.  
85  (d) Member states may charge a fee for granting a compact privilege  
86    Section 4. (a) To exercise the compact privilege under the terms and provisions of 
87 the compact, the licensee shall: (i) hold a license in the home state; (ii) have no encumbrance on 
88 any state license; (iii) be eligible for a compact privilege in any member state in accordance with 
89 subsections (d), (g) and (h); (iv) not received any adverse action against any license or compact 
90 privilege within the previous 2 years; (v) notify the commission that the licensee is seeking the 
91 compact privilege within a remote state or states; (vi) pay any applicable fees, including any state 
92 fee, for the compact privilege; (vii) meet any jurisprudence requirements established by the 
93 remote state or states in which the licensee is seeking a compact privilege; and (viii) report to the 
94 commission adverse action taken by any non-member state within 30 days from the date the 
95 adverse action is taken.  

6 of 21 96  (b) The compact privilege is valid until the expiration date of the home state license. The 
97 licensee must comply with the requirements of this section to maintain the compact privilege in 
98 the remote state.  
99    (c) A licensee providing physical therapy in a remote state under the compact 
100 privilege shall function within the laws and regulations of the remote state.  
101    (d) A licensee providing physical therapy in a remote state is subject to that state’s 
102 regulatory authority. A remote state may, in accordance with due process and that state’s laws, 
103 remove a licensee’s compact privilege in the remote state for a specific period of time, impose 
104 fines, or take any other necessary actions to protect the health and safety of its citizens. The 
105 licensee is not eligible for a compact privilege in any state until the specific time for removal has 
106 passed and all fines are paid.   
107  (e) If a home state license is encumbered, the licensee shall lose the compact privilege in 
108 any remote state until the following occur: (1) the home state license is no longer encumbered; 
109 and (2) 2 years have elapsed from the date of the adverse action.  
110    (f) Once an encumbered license in the home state is restored to good standing, the 
111 licensee must meet the requirements of subsection (a) to obtain a compact privilege in any 
112 remote state.  
113  (g) If a licensee’s compact privilege in any remote state is removed, the individual shall 
114 lose the compact privilege in any remote state until the following occur: (A) the specific period 
115 of time for which the compact privilege was removed has ended; (B) all fines have been paid; 
116 and (C) 2 years have elapsed from the date of the adverse action. Once the requirements of this 

7 of 21 117 paragraph have been met, the license must meet the requirements in subsection (a) to obtain a 
118 compact privilege in a remote state.
