5 CHARTER OF THE CITY OF LYNN
6 PREAMBLE
7 We the people of the city of Lynn, under the constitution and laws of the Commonwealth
8 of Massachusetts, in order to secure the benefits of local self-government and to provide for an
9 honest and accountable local government, do hereby adopt this charter and confer upon the city
10 the powers contained herein, subject to the following restrictions, and prescribed by the
11 following procedures and governmental structure. By this action, we the people secure the
12 benefits of home rule and affirm the values of representative democracy, encourage the passage
13 of knowledge between administrations which will aid in strong political leadership, public
14 engagement, diversity, inclusiveness and regional cooperation.

15 ARTICLE 1
16 INCORPORATION, FORM OF GOVERNMENT AND POWERS OF THE CITY
17 Section 1-1 Incorporation
18 The inhabitants of the city of Lynn within the territorial limits established by law shall
19 continue to be a body corporate and politic under the name "City of Lynn. "
20 Section 1-2 Short Title
21 This instrument shall be known and may be cited as the Lynn Special Act Charter.
22 Section 1-3 Division of Powers
23 The administration of the fiscal, prudential and municipal affairs of the city, with the
24 government thereof, shall be vested in an executive branch, which shall consist of the mayor, and
25 a legislative branch, which shall consist of the city council. The executive branch shall never
26 exercise any legislative power and the legislative branch shall never exercise any executive
27 power.
28 Section 1-4 Powers of the City
29 Subject only to express limitations on the exercise of any power or function by a city in
30 the constitution or laws of the commonwealth, it shall be the intent and purpose of the voters of
31 the city of Lynn, through the adoption of this charter, to secure for the city all powers it is
32 possible to secure under the constitution and statutes of the commonwealth, as fully and as
33 completely as though each such power were specifically and individually enumerated herein.
34 Section 1-5 Construction
2 of 46

35 The powers of the city under the charter are to be construed liberally in favor of the city,
36 and the specific mention of particular powers is not intended to limit in any way the general
37 powers of the city as stated in Section 1-4.
38 Section 1-6 Intergovernmental Relations
39 Subject only to express limitations in the constitution or statutes of the commonwealth,
40 the city may exercise any of its powers or perform any function, and may participate in the
41 financing thereof, jointly or in cooperation, by contract or otherwise, with the commonwealth or
42 any political subdivision, or agency thereof or the United States government or agency thereof.
43 Section 1-7 Definitions
44 As used in this charter, the following words shall have the following meanings unless the
45 context clearly requires otherwise:
46 (a) “Charter” , this charter and any amendment to it hereafter adopted.
47 (b) “City”, the city of Lynn.
48 (c) “City agency”, a board, commission, committee or other multiple-member body,
49 department, division or office of the city.
50 (d) “City officer”, a person in charge of a department of the city who in the exercise
51 of the duties of his office exercises some portion of the sovereign power, whether great or small.
52 (e) “City website”, a set of related web pages located under a single domain name
53 that is produced by the body corporate and politic of the city of Lynn.
3 of 46

54 (f) “Emergency”, a sudden, unexpected, unforeseen happening, occurrence or
55 condition which necessitates immediate action.
56 (g) “Full council”, the entire authorized complement of the city council
57 notwithstanding any vacancies which may exist.
58 (h) “General laws”, — general laws with all lower case letters shall mean laws
59 enacted by the general court which apply alike to all cities and towns, to all cities or to a class of
60 cities and towns of which the city of Lynn is a member.
61 (i) “General Laws", with initial letter of each word in capital letters, the General
62 Laws of the Commonwealth of Massachusetts, a codification and revision of statutes enacted on
63 December 22, 1920 and all amendments thereto.
64 (j) “Initiative measure”, a measure proposed by initiative procedures under this
65 charter.
66 (k) “Local newspaper”, a newspaper of general circulation within the city of Lynn.
67 (l) “Majority vote”, a majority of the full complement of the city council, school
68 committee or other multiple-member body.
69 (m) “Measure”, an ordinance adopted or to be adopted by the city council or an order,
70 resolution, vote or other proceeding adopted or to be adopted by the city council or the school
71 committee.
72 (n) “Multiple-member body”, any body consisting of at least 2 members, whether
73 elected, appointed or otherwise constituted; provided, however, that “multiple-member body”
74 shall not include the city council or school committee.
4 of 46

75 (o) “Referendum measure”, a measure adopted by the city council or an order,
76 resolution, vote or other proceeding adopted or to be adopted by the city council or the school
77 committee that is to be protested by the referendum procedures under this charter.
78 (p) “Voters”, the registered voters of the city of Lynn.
79 ARTICLE 2
80 LEGISLATIVE BRANCH
81 Section 2-1 Composition; Eligibility; Election and Term
82 (a) Composition— There shall be a city council consisting of 11 members. The city
83 council shall exercise the legislative powers of the city. Four members of the council, who shall
84 be councilors at-large, shall be nominated and elected by and from the voters of the entire city.
85 Seven members of the council, who shall be ward councilors, shall be nominated and elected by
86 and from the voters of 7 city wards; provided, however, that 1 ward councilor shall be elected
87 from each ward.
88 (b) Eligibility — Any voter shall be eligible to hold the office of councilor. A ward
89 councilor, notwithstanding the ward councilor’s removal from 1 ward in the city to another
90 during the term for which they were elected, may continue to serve and to perform the duties of
91 the office until the expiration of the term for which that ward councilor was elected.
92 (c) Election and term — The term of office for councilors shall be for 2 years, which
93 shall begin on the first business day of January following the election and until their successors
94 are qualified.
95 Section 2-2 Presiding Officer
5 of 46

96 The city council shall, annually on the first business day in January, meet for the purpose
97 of organization. The council shall be called together by the city clerk or, in the absence of the
98 city clerk, by the senior member present, as determined by years of service, who shall preside.
99 The city council shall then elect from among its members, by separate roll call votes, a president
100 and vice president. A majority vote of the full council shall be necessary for such election. No
101 other business shall be in order until such officers are elected. The president shall preside at all
102 meetings of the city council, regulate the proceedings thereof and decide all questions of order.
103 They shall have such other powers and duties as may be provided by the charter, by ordinance or
104 by vote of the city council. They shall have the same right to vote as any other city councilor. In
105 the event of the absence of the president, the vice president shall act as president.
106 Section 2-3 Compensation
107 The city council shall, by ordinance, establish an annual salary for its members. No
108 ordinance increasing the salary shall be effective unless it shall have been adopted during the
109 first 18 months of the term for which the councilors are elected and it provides that such salary
110 shall take effect at the commencement of the term of office of the next city council to be elected.
111 Section 2-4 Prohibitions
112 (a) Appointment after expiration of term — A city councilor shall not hold any
113 compensated appointive city office or city employment during the councilor’s term and until 1
114 year following the expiration of the term for which the councilor was elected unless waived by a
115 2/3 vote of the full city council. Nothing in this subsection shall prohibit a city officer or city
116 employee who has taken a leave of absence from such office or employment to serve on the city
117 council from resuming the duties of their office following service as a city councilor.
6 of 46

118 (b) Interference in administration — Except as otherwise provided in this charter, no
119 member of the city council and no committee of the city council shall directly participate in the
120 conduct of the administrative business of the city.
121 Section 2-5 Exercise of Powers; Quorum; Rules of Procedure
122 (a) Exercise of powers — Except as otherwise provided by law or this charter, the
123 legislative powers of the city council may be exercised in such a manner as it shall determine.
124 (b) Quorum — A majority of the city council shall constitute a quorum. The
125 affirmative vote of a majority of the full council shall be necessary to adopt any motion,
126 resolution, order or other measure, except as otherwise provided by law or this charter.
127 (c) Rules of procedure — The city council shall establish rules for its proceedings,
128 including:
129 (i) scheduling regular meetings of the council which shall be held at such times and
130 places as set by ordinance; and
131 (ii) scheduling special meetings of the council which shall be held at the call of the mayor
132 as provided in section 3-5, at the call of the president of the council or at the call of any 3
133 members; provided, however, that notice of a special meeting shall be by written notice delivered
134 via electronic mail at least 48 hours in advance of the time set and shall include the subjects to be
135 acted upon.
136 (d) Except as otherwise provided by law, all sessions of the city council and all sessions
137 of any committees of the city council shall be open to the public.
7 of 46

138 (e) Each issue which comes before the city council requiring planning or the selection of
139 a course of action shall be determined by a vote of the city council, the results of which shall be
140 recorded.
141 (f) A full, accurate, up-to-date record of the proceedings of the city council shall be kept
142 and shall be open for inspection by the public. It shall include a record of each roll call vote.
143 Section 2-6 Council Access to Information
144 (a) In general — The city council may make investigations into the affairs of the city
145 and into the conduct and performance of any city agency.
146 (b) City officers, members of city agencies, employees — The city council may
147 require any city officer, member of a city agency or city employee to appear before it to give any
148 information that the city council may require in relation to the municipal services, functions,
149 powers or duties which are within the scope of responsibility of that person. This section shall
150 not apply to school officials or employees.
151 (c) Mayor — The city council may require the mayor to provide specific information
152 to it on any matter within the jurisdiction of the city council. The city council may require the
153 mayor to appear before it, in person, to provide specific information on the conduct of any aspect
154 of the business of the city. The mayor may bring to such a meeting any city officer or employee
155 that the mayor deems necessary to assist in responding to the questions posed by the city council.
156 (d) Notice — The city council shall provide at least 7 days' notice to any person
157 whom it may require to appear before it under this section and such notice shall include specific
158 questions on which the city council seeks information. No person called to appear before the
8 of 46

159 city council under this section shall be required to respond to any question not relevant or related
160 to those presented in advance and in writing.
161 Section 2-7 Appointments by the City Council
162 (a) City clerk — The city council shall, by a majority vote, appoint a city clerk who
163 shall serve for a 5-year term. Any vacancy in the office of city clerk shall be filled in like manner
164 for the remainder of any unexpired term. The city clerk shall receive such compensation as the
165 city council may determine. The city clerk shall keep full and accurate records of the proceedings
166 of the city council and perform such other duties as prescribed by the General Laws, by this
167 charter or by ordinance. In the absence of the city clerk, the assistant city clerk shall perform the
168 duties of the city clerk and shall perform such other duties as prescribed by the city clerk or by
169 ordinance.
170 (b) Clerk of committees — The city council shall, biennially in January of the year
171 following a municipal election, by a majority vote, elect a clerk of committees who shall hold
172 office for 2 years, such term beginning not later than the first day of March in the year of the
173 clerk of committees election and ending upon the election of a successor, unless sooner removed.
174 The clerk of committees shall receive such compensation as the city council may determine. The
175 clerk of committees shall keep full and accurate records of the proceedings of the several
176 committees of the city council and shall perform such other duties as may be required of the
177 clerk of committees by the city council.
178 (c) Other personnel — Subject to appropriation, the city council may employ such
179 other personnel as it deems necessary, which may include an attorney on the staff of the city
180 solicitor's office.
9 of 46

181 Section 2-8 Appointments to City Offices
182 The mayor shall submit to the city council the names of all persons the mayor desires to
183 appoint to any city office as a department head or as a member of a multiple-member body;
184 provided, however, that this section shall not apply to any position which is subject to civil
185 service law. The city council shall refer all such names submitted to a standing committee which
186 shall investigate the candidates for confirmation and make a report with recommendations to the
187 full city council not less than 7 and not more than 21 days following such referral. If the city
188 council takes no other action, the appointments shall become effective 30 days after the date the
189 name was received by the city council.
190 Section 2-9 Ordinances and Other Measures
191 (a) Measures — No measure, except resolutions and other votes constituting
192 ordinary, routine matters, shall be passed finally on the date on which they are introduced, except
193 in the case of an emergency. Except as otherwise provided by this charter, each adopted measure
194 shall take effect 30 days after adoption or at such other date as specified in the measure. No
195 ordinance shall be amended or repealed except by another ordinance adopted in accordance with
196 the charter or as provided in the initiative and referendum procedures.
197 (b) Emergency measures — An emergency measure shall be introduced in the form
198 and manner prescribed for measures generally except that it shall be plainly designated as an
199 emergency measure and shall contain statements after the enacting clause declaring that an
200 emergency exists and describing its scope and nature in clear and specific terms. A preamble
201 which declares and defines the emergency shall be separately voted on and shall require the
202 affirmative vote of 2/3 of the full city council. An emergency measure may be passed with or
10 of 46

203 without amendment or rejected at the meeting at which it is introduced. No measure making a
204 grant, renewal, or extension, of whatever kind or nature, or a franchise or special privilege shall
205 be passed as an emergency measure and, except as otherwise provided by the laws of the
206 commonwealth, no such grant, renewal or extension shall be made otherwise than by ordinance.
207 An emergency measure shall become effective upon adoption or at such later time as it may
208 specify.
209 Section 2-10 Filling of Vacancies
210 If a vacancy occurs in the office of city councilor, whether by failure to elect or otherwise
211 and there is no regular city election scheduled to be held within 90 days following the date of the
212 vacancy, the remaining councilors shall, within 30 days following the date of such vacancy, act
213 to fill it. The city council shall fill the vacancy for the remainder of the unexpired term by
214 choosing the defeated candidate receiving the most votes for the office of city councilor in the
215 last regular city election if that defeated candidate received at least 20 per cent of the total ballots
216 cast for the that position; provided, however, that for the purpose of this section, the 20 per cent
217 minimum threshold shall be calculated as a ratio of the total number of ballots cast for the office
218 of city councilor to the total number of ballots cast for the defeated candidate. If no defeated
219 candidate received at least 20 per cent of the total ballots cast, the city council may, at its
220 discretion, appoint a person, who may be the defeated candidate, from among the qualified
221 voters of the city to serve for the remainder of the unexpired term. If the city council appoints a
222 person to fill the vacancy under this section, the city clerk shall post notice of the vacancy at
223 least 14 days prior to the meeting at which the council shall act to fill the vacancy. A person
224 chosen to fill the vacancy shall take the oath of office and commence serving forthwith. A person
11 of 46

225 filling a vacancy in the office of councilor under this section shall not be entitled to have the
226 words "candidate for re-election" printed against their name on the election ballot.
227 ARTICLE 3
228 EXECUTIVE BRANCH
229 Section 3-1 Mayor - Qualifications; Term of Office; Compensation
230 (a) Mayor, qualifications — The mayor shall be the chief executive officer of the city
231 who shall be elected by and from the qualified voters of the city. Any registered voter may hold
232 the office of mayor. The mayor shall devote full time to the duties of the mayor’s office, shall not
233 hold any other elective public office and shall not actively engage in any other business,
234 occupation or profession while serving as mayor.
235 (b) Term of office — The term of office of the mayor shall be 4 years which shall
236 begin on the first business day of January following the election and shall continue until a
237 successor is elected and qualified.
238 (c) Compensation — The city council shall by ordinance establish an annual salary
239 for the mayor. No ordinance increasing the salary of the mayor shall be effective unless it was
240 adopted in the first 18 months of the term for which councilors are elected and provides that such
241 salary shall be effective at the commencement of the next term of office of the mayor.
242 Section 3-2 Executive Powers; Enforcement of Ordinances
243 The executive powers of the city shall be vested solely in the mayor and shall be
244 exercised by the mayor personally or through the several city agencies under the mayor’s general
245 supervision and control. The mayor shall cause the charter and the laws, ordinances and orders
12 of 46

246 for the government of the city to be enforced and shall cause a record of all the mayor’s official
247 acts to be kept.
248 The mayor shall exercise general supervision and direction over all city agencies unless
249 otherwise provided by law. Each city agency shall furnish to the mayor, forthwith upon the
250 mayor’s request, any information, materials or otherwise as the needs of the mayor and the
251 interest of the city may require.
252 Section 3-3 Appointments by the Mayor
253 The mayor shall appoint all city officers, department heads and members of multiple-
254 member bodies for whom no other method of appointment is provided by this charter. This
255 section shall not apply to persons serving under the school committee and the city clerk, persons
256 appointed by state officials and persons serving under the city council. All appointments made
257 by the mayor shall be subject to review by the city council as provided in section 2-8.
258 Section 3-4 Temporary Appointments to City Offices
259 If a vacancy in a city office occurs and the needs of the city require that such office be
260 filled, the mayor may designate a person to perform the duties of the office on a temporary basis
261 for not more than 120 days; provided, however, that such temporary appointment may be
262 extended for 30 days upon a majority vote of the full city council.
263 Section 3-5 Special Meetings
264 The mayor may at any time and for any purpose call a special meeting of the city council
265 by causing a notice of such meeting, including the purpose for which the meeting is necessary, to
266 be delivered by electronic mail to each member of the city council. The notice shall be delivered
13 of 46

267 at least 48 hours in advance of the time set or in a lesser period time if the mayor determines that
268 an emergency exists.
269 Section 3-6 Approval of Mayor, Exception; Veto
270 Each order, ordinance or vote adopted or passed by the city council relative to the affairs
271 of the city shall be presented to the mayor for approval, except resolutions, the selection of city
272 officers or employees by the city council and any matters relating to the internal operations of the
273 city council. If the mayor approves of the measure, the mayor shall sign it within 10 days. If the
274 mayor disapproves of the measure, the mayor shall return the measure within 10 days, with the
275 specific reasons for such disapproval attached to it, in writing, to the city council. The city
276 council shall enter the objections of the mayor on its records and not sooner than 10 days but not
277 later than 30 days from the date of its return to the city council, shall reconsider the same
278 measure. If the city council, notwithstanding the disapproval by the mayor, shall again pass the
279 order, ordinance or vote by a 2/3 vote of the full council, it shall then be deemed in force
280 notwithstanding the failure of the mayor to approve it. If the mayor has neither signed a measure
281 nor returned it to the city council within 10 days following the date it was presented to the
282 mayor, the measure shall be deemed approved and in force.
283 Section 3-7 Temporary Absence of the Mayor
284 (a) Acting mayor — Whenever the mayor shall be unable to perform the duties of the
285 office by reason of incapacitation or other cause as determined by a 4/5 vote of the full city
286 council, the president of the city council shall be the acting mayor. Should the president of the
287 city council be unable or unwilling to serve, the city council shall forthwith elect 1 of its
288 members to serve as acting mayor.
14 of 46

289 (b) Powers of acting mayor — The acting mayor shall have only those powers of the
290 mayor that are essential to conduct the business of the city in an orderly and efficient manner and
291 on which immediate action is required. The acting mayor shall not make any permanent
292 appointments or remove any person from city service unless the acting mayor has served in that
293 capacity for more than 60 days. The acting mayor shall not approve or disapprove any measure
294 adopted by the city council unless the time within which the mayor is required to act expires
295 before the return of the mayor. During any period in which any member of the city council is
296 serving as acting mayor, that councilor shall not vote as a member of the city council.
297 Section 3-8 Vacancy in Office of Mayor
298 (a) Whenever a permanent vacancy occurs in the office of mayor, the process for filling
299 of the vacancy shall be as provided in this section.
300 (b) If a vacancy occurs within the initial 36 months of the mayor’s term, the city council
301 shall call a special election to be held within 90 days following the date of the vacancy; provided,
302 however, that if the vacancy occurs in a regular municipal election year, the city council may
303 request that the city clerk determine if the election calendar would allow for the filling of the
304 vacancy at the next regular municipal election following such vacancy. Upon receiving the
305 clerk's determination, the city council may delay the filling of the vacancy until such election
306 notwithstanding that the date of the election may exceed 90 days from the date of the vacancy.
307 The president of the city council shall serve as acting mayor until the vacancy is filled. If the city
308 council president is unwilling or unable to serve, the city council shall elect a councilor to serve
309 as the acting mayor from among its membership. The resulting vacancy on the council shall be
310 filled in the manner provided in section 2-10. Any person serving as the acting mayor under this
15 of 46

311 section shall receive the compensation then in effect for the position of mayor and shall not vote
312 as a member of the city council. The person elected as mayor shall take office immediately upon
313 certification of the vote and serve for the remainder of the unexpired term.
314 (c) If a vacancy occurs in the final 12 months of the term, the office of mayor shall be
315 filled as provided in this section; provided, however, that if the councilor serving as the acting
316 mayor under this section chooses to be a candidate for mayor, such councilor shall not be entitled
317 to have the words "candidate for reelection" printed with that councilor's name on the regular
318 municipal election ballot. The resulting vacancy on the council shall be filled in the manner
319 provided in section 2-10. The person elected as mayor at the regular municipal election shall take
320 office immediately upon certification of the vote and shall serve for the remainder of the
321 unexpired term.
322 ARTICLE 4
323 SCHOOL COMMITTEE
324 Section 4-1 Composition; Eligibility; Term of Office
325 (a) Composition — The school committee shall consist of the mayor, who shall serve
326 as chair, and 6 members who shall be elected at large.
327 (b) Eligibility — Any registered voter may hold the office of school committee
328 member.
329 (c) Term of office — School committee members shall serve for 2 year-terms
330 beginning on the first business day in January following the election and continuing until a
331 successor is qualified.
16 of 46

332 Section 4-2 Officers of the School Committee
333 The school committee shall organize biennially on the first business day in January and
334 shall elect 1 of its members as vice chair. The vice chair shall preside in the absence of the chair.
335 Section 4-3 Powers and Duties
336 (a) The school committee shall have all powers which are conferred on school
337 committees by the General Laws and such additional powers and duties as may be provided by
338 the charter, by ordinance and as otherwise consistent with the General Laws. The school
339 committee shall also have the specific powers and duties as provided in this section.
340 (b) The school committee shall elect a superintendent of schools who shall be charged
341 with the administration of the school system, subject only to policy guidelines and directives
342 adopted by the school committee and, upon the recommendation of the superintendent, to
343 establish and appoint assistant or associate superintendents as provided by law.
344 (c) The school committee shall make all reasonable rules and regulations for the
345 management of the public school system and for conducting the business of the school
346 committee as it deems necessary or desirable.
347 (d) The school committee shall oversee the administration of the annual operating budget
348 for the school department, subject to appropriation by the city council. The school committee
349 shall have general charge of all school buildings and grounds and shall furnish all school
350 buildings with proper fixtures, furniture and equipment. The school committee shall provide
351 ordinary maintenance of all school buildings and grounds unless a central municipal maintenance
352 department, which may include maintenance of school buildings and grounds, is established in
17 of 46

353 accordance with law. Whenever the school committee determines that additional classrooms are
354 necessary to meet the educational needs of the community, at least 1 member of the school
355 committee, or a designee thereof, shall serve on the agency, council or committee to which the
356 planning or construction of the new, remodeled or renovated school building has been delegated.
357 Section 4-4 Prohibitions
358 (a) Appointment — No member of the school committee, except the mayor, shall
359 hold any compensated appointive city or school office or employment during their term until 1
360 year following the expiration of their term unless waived by a 2/3 vote of the full school
361 committee. This subsection shall not preclude a city officer or employee who has taken a leave
362 of absence from such office or employment from resuming to their duties as such following their
363 service as a school committee member.
364 (b) Interference in administration — Except as may be otherwise authorized by the
365 charter, no member of the school committee or any subcommittee thereof shall directly
366 participate in the conduct of the administrative business of the schools.
367 Section 4-5 Exercise of Powers
368 (a) In general — Unless otherwise provided by General Laws or this charter, the
369 duties of the school committee shall be exercised in the manner as it determines appropriate.
370 (b) Quorum — The presence of 4 members of the school committee shall constitute a
371 quorum. The affirmative votes of 4 members shall be necessary to adopt any order, resolution or
372 other formal vote.
18 of 46

373 (c) Meetings to be public — All meetings of the school committee and each
374 subcommittee thereof shall at open to the public unless otherwise provided by law.
375 Section 4-6 Filling of Vacancies
376 (a) If a vacancy occurs in the office of school committee, whether by failure to elect or
377 otherwise and there is no regular city election scheduled to be held within 90 days following the
378 date the vacancy, the remaining school committee members shall, within 30 days following the
379 date of the vacancy, act to fill it.
380 (b) The school committee shall fill the vacancy for the remainder of the unexpired term
381 by choosing the defeated candidate receiving the most votes for that office in the last regular city
382 election if the defeated candidate received at least 20 per cent of the total ballots cast for that
383 position. For the purpose of this section, the 20 per cent minimum threshold shall be calculated
384 as a ratio of the total number of ballots cast for the office of school committee member to the
385 total number of ballots cast for the defeated candidate.
386 (c) If a vacancy on the school committee cannot be filled pursuant to subsections (a) and
387 (b), the president of the city council shall, within 30 days after making such determination, call a
388 joint meeting of the city council and school committee to fill the vacancy. The city council and
389 school committee shall choose a person to fill the vacancy from among the qualified voters of the
390 city entitled to vote for that office. Any person chosen to fill the vacancy under this subsection
391 shall serve until the next regular city election when the office shall be filled by the voters. The
392 person selected to fill the vacancy shall take the oath of office and commence serving forthwith
393 and shall continue to serve until a successor is qualified. Persons serving as school committee
19 of 46

394 members under this section shall not be entitled to have the words "candidate for reelection"
395 printed with that person's name on the election ballot.
396 ARTICLE 5
397 FINANCIAL PROCEDURES
398 Section 5-1 Fiscal Year
399 The fiscal year of the city shall begin on the first day of July and shall end on the last day
400 of June unless another period is required by the General Law.
401 Section 5-2 Annual Budget Policy
402 The mayor shall call a joint meeting of the city council and school committee, including
403 the superintendent of schools, before the commencement of the budget process to review the
404 financial condition of the city, revenue and expenditure forecasts and other relevant information
405 prepared by the mayor to develop a coordinated budget.
406 Section 5-3 Submission of Operating Budget; Budget Message
407 At least 45 days before the beginning of the fiscal year, the mayor shall submit to the city
408 council a proposed operating budget for all city agencies, which shall include the school
409 department, for the ensuing fiscal year with an accompanying budget message and supporting
410 documents. The budget message submitted by the mayor shall explain the operating budget in
411 fiscal terms and in terms of work programs for all city agencies. It shall outline the proposed
412 fiscal policies of the city for the ensuing fiscal year, describe important features of the proposed
413 operating budget and include any major variations from the current operating budget, fiscal
414 policies, revenues and expenditures and the reasons for such changes. The proposed operating
20 of 46

415 budget shall provide a complete fiscal plan of all city funds and activities and shall be in such
416 form as the mayor deems desirable. The school budget, as adopted by the school committee,
417 shall be submitted to the mayor at least 30 days before the submission of the proposed operating
418 budget to the city council. The mayor shall notify the school committee of the date by which the
419 proposed budget of the school committee shall be submitted to the mayor. The mayor and the
420 superintendent of schools shall coordinate the dates and times of the school committee's budget
421 process under the General Laws.
422 Section 5-4 Action on the Operating Budget
423 (a) Public hearing — The city council shall publish in at least 1 local newspaper and
424 on the city's website a notice of the proposed operating budget as submitted by the mayor. The
425 notice shall state: (i) the times and places where copies of the entire proposed operating budget
426 will be available for inspection by the public; and (ii) the date, time and place, not less than 14
427 days after its publication, when a public hearing on the proposed operating budget shall be held
428 by the city council.
429 (b) Adoption of the budget — The city council shall adopt the proposed operating

859 Section 9-12 Enforcement of Charter Provisions
860 It shall be the duty of the mayor to see that the charter is faithfully followed and complied
861 with by all city agencies and city employees. If it shall appear to the mayor that a city agency or
862 city employee has failed to follow this charter, the mayor shall cause written notice to be given to
863 such agency or employee directing compliance with the charter. If it shall appear to the city
864 council that the mayor personally has failed to follow this charter, the city council shall, by
865 resolution, direct the attention of the mayor to those areas in which it believes there was a failure
866 to comply with this charter. Actions under chapter 231A of the General Laws may be
867 commenced to determine the rights, duties, status or other legal relations arising under this
868 charter, including any question of construction or validity which may be involved in such
869 determination.

872 Section 10-1 Continuation of Existing Laws
873 All special acts of the general court and all ordinances, rules, regulations and resolutions
874 of the city which are in force on the effective date of this charter which are consistent with this
875 charter shall continue in full force and effect until amended, revised or repealed as provided by

877 Section 10-2 Continuation of Government

878 All city agencies shall continue to perform the duties of the office until reappointed or
879 reelected or until any successors to their respective duties are duly appointed or elected or until
880 their duties have been transferred and assumed by another city agency.
881 Section 10-3 Continuation of Administrative Personnel
882 Any person holding a city office or a position in the administrative service of the city
883 shall retain such office or position and shall continue to perform the duties thereof until
884 provisions have been made for the performance of those duties by another person or agency;
885 provided, however, that no person in the permanent full-time service of the city shall forfeit pay
886 grade or time in service. All such persons shall be retained in a capacity as similar to their former
887 capacity as is practicable and any reductions in the personnel needs of the city shall be
888 accomplished through attrition unless specific provision is otherwise made in this article.
889 Section 10-4 Transfer of Records and Property
890 All records, property and equipment of a city agency or part thereof, the powers and
891 duties of which have been transferred or assigned to another city agency under this charter shall
892 be transferred forthwith to the city agency to which such powers and duties have transferred or
893 assigned.
894 Section 10-5 Effect on Obligations, Taxes, etc.
895 All official bonds, recognizances, obligations, contracts and other instruments entered
896 into or executed by or to the city before the adoption of this charter and all taxes, assessments,
897 fines, penalties and forfeitures incurred or imposed, due or owing to the city shall be enforced
898 and collected. All writs, prosecutions, actions and causes of action, except as otherwise provided
