Overview of the Massachusetts Women Veterans Network
In recognition of the unique needs of women veterans, Congress established the Center for Women
Veterans within the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) in 1994. This initiative aimed to
ensure that female veterans would receive equal benefits and services comparable to their male
counterparts. In 1997, the Commonwealth of Massachusetts created the Women Veterans Network
(WVN) to provide additional support to women veterans through programming, connections to
resources, and advocacy. Under the state’s Executive Office of Veterans Services, the WVN
continues to collaborate with service organizations, veteran advocates, the United States
Department of Veterans Affairs, and other organizations to raise awareness about women veteran
issues in Massachusetts.
The mission of WVN is to empower women veterans by ensuring their voices are heard, their
contributions acknowledged, and their well-being enhanced through education, advocacy, and
collaboration. The vision is to foster an environment that promotes the success and well-being of
women veterans in all aspects of their lives, including career development, personal growth, and
overall fulfillment. The WVN’s values are Advocacy, Support, Progressiveness, Inclusion,
Respect, and Equity (ASPIRE).
The WVN offers programming to women veterans that includes an annual Women Veterans
Network Conference, Women Veterans Recognition Day Luncheon, the Deborah Sampson Award
Ceremony, a financial education and well-being series, family events, and co-sponsored
community events such as film screenings and social gatherings. These events allow women
veterans to build a sense of camaraderie with fellow women veterans as well as with community
service providers.
Additionally, the WVN visits communities across the state to identify women veterans and share
resources and support services with veterans and their loved ones. Outreach is not possible without
continued partnerships with federal, state, and local government stakeholders, as well as
community organizations. These partnerships and direct outreach efforts inform WVN and EOVS
and have resulted in improved advocacy for resources and expanded programming.
The Women Veterans Network Collaborative, an evolution of the former Women Veterans
Network Steering Committee, continued to meet quarterly, with special meetings for those
assisting in planning WVN’s major events. Membership increased from 15 in 2024 to 25 in 2025
and is diverse across race/ethnicity, age, military branch, and conflict era. In addition to providing
advocacy and guidance, members volunteered at the annual Women Veterans Network
Conference, the Women Veterans Recognition Luncheon, and the Deborah Sampson Award
Ceremony.

Massachusetts Women Veteran Demographics
The U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) estimates that 22,870 female veterans reside in
Massachusetts, representing approximately 8% of the state’s total veteran population. Women
veterans are overwhelmingly white, comprising 81% of the female veteran population. The three
counties with the highest concentrations of women veterans are Middlesex, Worcester, and
Hampden, respectively. 1

Events and Programming Highlights
Since October 2024, the Women Veterans Network (WVN) has hosted nine events, with some
cancellations prompting the launch of a Program Interest Survey to better understand women
veterans’ social and educational needs. The survey, conducted from July to August 2025,
measured location, availability, and program interests. Results from the survey informed more
strategic event planning for FY26.

Executive Director of the VA Center for Women Veterans; and a keynote delivered by Elizabeth
Estabrooks, U.S. Army Veteran and speaker.

Tabling and Outreach Highlights
The WVN also performed outreach (tabling) at 46 events. During tabling, the WVN provides
women veterans with information about programs and services through direct engagement,
conversations, and brochures. These events allowed the WVN to connect with a record number
of women veterans. The WVN also participates in several statewide collaboratives that share
information and advocate for veteran issues.
A total of 210 new WVN Newsletter subscribers were added in FY25, representing a 6.86%
increase from FY24. The newsletter achieved an average open rate of 54% and a 4% click rate.
Overall membership in the WVN grew to 3,297, reflecting an increase from 3,154 in the
previous year. Additionally, a resource document was developed to support LGBTQ+ veterans
and their loved ones.
As part of its broader collaborative efforts, the WVN contributes to the work of the Advisory
Committee on Women Veterans (WVAC). The Committee advises the Executive Office of
Veterans Services on issues affecting women veterans, focusing on improving access to benefits,
health care, housing, and employment opportunities. In FY25, WVAC published its first annual
report highlighting key priorities, including addressing barriers to care, increasing visibility of
women veterans, and enhancing statewide coordination among veteran-serving organizations.
Through active participation with WVAC, the WVN ensures that insights gained through its
outreach and engagement efforts inform policy recommendations, strengthen advocacy, and
advance equity for women veterans across the Commonwealth.

Women Veterans Benefits Documents
The WVN continues to engage women veterans throughout the Commonwealth. Multiple WVN
documents have been created to outline benefits, increase knowledge of services, and offer
opportunities to join the WVN network. Below is a summary of the documents developed by the
WVN, with corresponding examples included in the addendums.
The WVN produces a monthly electronic newsletter of upcoming events and resources that is
emailed to all WVN members. Archives of past newsletters can be found on the WVN website
(https://www.mass.gov/orgs/massachusetts-women-veterans-network).
The WVN produced a brochure that shares the mission, vision, and history of the WVN, as well
as information about how to join the network. The brochure provides contact information such as
a phone number, email, QR code, and website (Addendum A).
The WVN offers an online application to join the network. This application is open to all women
veterans and allies and is accessible on the WVN website (Addendum B).
The WVN offers an online application to join the Women Veterans Network Collaborative. This
collaborative is an advisory council of women veterans that provides guidance and leadership for
the WVN. The application is available online for women veterans interested in serving a one-year
term (Addendum C). A renewal application is also available for those who decide to return the
following year (Addendum D).
The WVN also manages the Deborah Sampson Award application. This application supports the
annual Deborah Sampson Award Ceremony, an event honoring an exemplary woman veteran, held
each June. The application is accessible online through the WVN website (Addendum E).
The Massachusetts Women Veterans Network website is rich with information for women
veterans, state and federal agencies, community partners, and other stakeholders. In February
2024, the WVN website was redesigned to include a robust resources section and to improve the
display of upcoming events, updates, and WVN newsletter archives.

Upcoming In-person Events
March 2026: Annual Women Veterans Recognition Luncheon
This event provides a heartwarming tribute to the brave women of Massachusetts who have served,
and includes a keynote speaker and opening ceremony.
May 2026: WVN Annual Conference
The annual conference provides women veterans with an opportunity to meet, network, and learn
about updates from the Executive Office of Veterans Services. A typical conference includes an
opening ceremony, afternoon guest speakers, multiple breakout sessions, and opportunities for
women veterans to build community. The conference often hosts numerous exhibitors from the
veteran community, including the United States Department of Veterans Affairs, various state
programs, nonprofit organizations, and veteran community groups. It is staffed by EOVS
employees, supported by a contingent of volunteers, and accessible to all women veterans with a
virtual option.
June 2026: Deborah Sampson Award Ceremony
Hosted in Memorial Hall at the Massachusetts State House, this annual award ceremony pays
homage to those who exemplify the spirit of Deborah Sampson. Named after the Revolutionary
War hero who disguised herself as a man to fight for her country, the ceremony celebrates
individuals who embody her indomitable spirit, resilience, and dedication to service. The recipient
of this esteemed award is recognized for extraordinary contributions and selfless acts of service.
From military heroes to community champions, these individuals inspire others with their
unwavering commitment to making a difference. The ceremony includes the presentation of the
Deborah Sampson Award, captivating stories of bravery and sacrifice, and the commemoration of
one of America's first female soldiers.
