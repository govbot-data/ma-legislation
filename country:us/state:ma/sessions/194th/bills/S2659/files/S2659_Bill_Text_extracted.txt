227  Section 1. As used in this chapter, the following words shall have the following meaning 
228 unless the context clearly requires otherwise:  
229  “Child advocate”, the child advocate appointed under section 3.  
230  “Child-serving individual,” an individual who, in their professional role, has consistent 
231 access to children and are often alone with children and/or are responsible for their care. A 
232 child–serving individual may be a staff member, a contractor, or temporary employee at a public 
233 or private organization.  
234  “Child-Serving Volunteer,” an individual who, without compensation or expectation of 
235 compensation, provides services or supports in a capacity that involves direct contact with, 

12 of 52 236 supervision of, or responsibility for the care, education, treatment, or welfare of children under 
237 the age of eighteen at a public or private organization. This includes, but is not limited to, 
238 volunteers in schools, childcare programs, youth-serving organizations, recreational or athletic 
239 programs, faith-based programs, mentoring or tutoring services, and residential or congregate 
240 care settings. 
241  “Critical Incident”, (i) a fatality, near fatality, or serious bodily, or emotional injury, of a 
242 child, or the indecent assault and battery of a child in accordance with M.G.L. c. 265 §§ 13B- 
243 13B ¾ and §§ 23-23B, who is in the custody of or receiving services from an executive agency 
244 or a constituent agency; or (ii) circumstances which result in a reasonable belief that an executive 
245 agency or a constituent agency failed in its duty to protect or adequately serve a child and, as a 
246 result, the child was at imminent risk of, or suffered serious bodily or emotional injury or death. 
247  “Department”, the department of children and families.  
248  “Emotional Injury”, occurs when a child of any age witnesses the fatality or life-
249 threatening incident of an individual related to an unexpected medical event, overdose, violent 
250 act, or suicide. 
251  “Executive agency”, a state agency within the office of the governor, including that 
252 provides services to children, including services through contracted providers and through 
253 entities licensed by the state agency.  Executive agencies include the executive office of 
254 education, the executive office of health and human services, and their constituent agencies, the 
255 Massachusetts interagency council on housing and homelessness and the executive office of 
256 housing and livable communities.  

13 of 52 257  “Near fatality”, is an injury that is accidental, the result of a medical condition, or the 
258 result of abuse and neglect and is dependent on verbal certification by a physician that the child’s 
259 condition is considered to be life threatening. 
260  “Office”, the office of the child advocate.  
261  “Sudden unexplained death in pediatrics”, the sudden and unexpected death of an infant 
262 or child, under the age of three, where a cause is not immediately known before investigation.  
263  “Serious bodily injury”, an injury which involves a substantial risk of death, extreme 
264 physical pain, protracted and obvious disfigurement or protracted loss or impairment of the 
265 function of a bodily member, organ or mental faculty or emotional distress.
