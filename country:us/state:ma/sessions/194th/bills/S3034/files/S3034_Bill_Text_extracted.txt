119  Section 25.  Sludge management; master plan 
120  (a) The department shall investigate and study the sludge disposal needs of the 
121 commonwealth. In doing so, the department may incorporate the scientific research and technical 
122 findings included in, but not limited to, the following reports:  

7 of 36 123  (1) “MassDEP PFAS Testing Study for NPDES POTWs (PRF77),” published on June 
124 30, 2025, which documented PFAS contamination in influent, effluent and sewage sludge 
125 generated at every publicly owned treatment works in the commonwealth, with sludge 
126 consistently containing the highest concentrations of PFAS measured anywhere in the 
127 wastewater treatment system; and 
128  (2) “Current and Near-Term Management of Massachusetts Wastewater Sludge,” 
129 published on June 28, 2024, which assessed sludge generation, existing treatment and hauling 
130 capacity, available disposal outlets, regional infrastructure constraints and current technological 
131 limitations that prevent wastewater treatment facilities from removing PFAS or reducing sludge 
132 toxicity under current systems.  
133  (b) Having already established through said reports that PFAS contamination of sewage 
134 sludge is universal, persistent and technologically unavoidable with existing wastewater 
135 treatment processes, the department’s investigation under this section shall focus on the 
136 outstanding economic and operational questions related to sewage sludge management not 
137 addressed in prior studies. For the purposes of this section, toxicity shall include any and all 
138 pathogens, chemicals or other agents or molecules regulated by the commonwealth, and any 
139 substance known to cause harm to human health. Said study shall identify, to the maximum 
140 practicable extent: 
141  (1) For each wastewater treatment plan in the commonwealth:  
142  (i) The current arrangements for the collection, transfer, processing, treatment and 
143 disposal of sewage sludge;  

8 of 36 144  (ii) The current costs for the collection, transfer, processing, treatment and disposal of 
145 sewage sludge, including, but not limited to:  
146  (iii) The costs to the wastewater treatment plant to haul and dispose of sewage sludge 
147 from the wastewater treatment plant;   
148  (iv) The total payments to outside vendors to haul, dispose of or process sewage sludge 
149 generated at the wastewater treatment plant; and  
150  (v) The final location of sewage sludge, including, but not limited to, whether the sewage 
151 sludge is transported to: 
152  (A) an in-state landfill;  
153  (B) an out-of-state landfill;  
154  (C) a monofill landfill;  
155  (D) an incinerator;  
156  (E) a composting or co-composting facility; or  
157  (F) any other processing site; and 
158  (G) Appropriate technologies and practices for reducing the volume and toxicity of 
159 sewage sludge and improving wastewater treatment design and operation, delineated by cost, 
160 target wastewater treatment plant size and capacity and geographic location.  
161  (c) The department, after not less than 3 public hearings, held in geographically diverse 
162 areas of the commonwealth, and a public comment period, shall develop and maintain a 

9 of 36 163 comprehensive statewide master plan for sludge processing and disposal, including any 
164 necessary provisions to meet eligibility requirements under any federal program for financial aid 
165 in sludge management. Said plan shall describe, to the maximum practicable extent, a short and 
166 long-range program for reduction and management of sludge throughout the commonwealth, and 
167 the funding for the development of such systems, infrastructure and facilities which the 
168 department finds to be reasonably necessary. 
169  The plan shall be updated at a frequency to be determined by the commissioner, but not 
170 less frequently than every 10 years. Any amendment, alterations or changes to said master plan 
171 shall be adopted only after a public hearing.  
172  Without limitation of the foregoing, said plan shall include, but not be limited to: 
173  (1) A program for providing technical assistance and funding to entities that own a 
174 publicly-owned treatment works, hereafter referred to as POTWs, as defined in section 12B of 
175 chapter 132 A, to develop local sludge management plans; 
176  (2) A template local sludge management plan which entities that own and operate a 
177 POTW shall adopt and may alter; provided that any alterations are certified by the department as 
178 being compliance with the goals of this section; and provided further that no such entity shall be 
179 required to adopt a plan without having received technical assistance or funding from the 
180 department; 
181  (3) Goals and measurable benchmarks for the reduction, processing, toxicity and disposal 
182 of sludge in the commonwealth, as well as detailed methods and solutions to achieve said goals 
183 and benchmarks, including, but not limited to: 

10 of 36 184  (i) the creation and maintenance of specialized landfills for sludge disposal; 
185  (ii) the implementation of methods for reducing the volume and toxicity of sludge, 
186 including by, but not limited to, facilitating source reduction, remediation and gasification;  
187  (iii) the development of new or improved technologies and methods for reducing the 
188 volume and toxicity of sludge;  
189  (iv) the remediation of toxic chemicals and substances in wastewater and in sludge; and  
190  (v) viable research investments the commonwealth could make to further the goals laid 
191 out in said plan;  
192  (4) Criteria that would trigger updates to the plan, including, but not limited to, 
193 advancements in the treatment of sludge and procedures for determining if and how sludge could 
194 possibly be safely applied to land in the commonwealth; and 
195  (5) Estimates of the expected financial resources, and any regulatory or statutory changes, 
196 needed to implement said plan. 
197  The plan shall prioritize providing technical assistance and funding to entities that own a 
198 POTW; provided that the plan and any included funding formula or grant application criteria 
199 shall also prioritize geographic equity and environmental justice principles, as defined in section 
200 62 of chapter 30.  
201  The statewide master plan for sludge processing and disposal shall be posted online on 
202 the department’s website. All data, comments and reporting used by the department in drafting 
203 the plan shall be made easily available to the public on the department’s website. 

11 of 36 204  Section 25A. Sludge management grant program
