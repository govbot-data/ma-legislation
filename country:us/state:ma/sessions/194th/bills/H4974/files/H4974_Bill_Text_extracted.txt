An Act Preparing Apprentices through Training, Hiring, and Skills in Massachusetts.
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives in General Court assembled, and by the authority
of the same, as follows:
1 SECTION 1. For purposes of sections 5 and 6, the following words shall, unless the
2 context clearly requires otherwise, have the following meanings:—
3 “Bid”, any bid, proposal, or quote submitted by a contractor at any tier to a public agency or to
4 another contractor for the performance of work on a public works project.
5 “Construction contract”, any public works contract with an estimated construction cost in excess
6 of $10,000,000.
7 “Public agency”, any department, agency, board, commission, authority, or other instrumentality
8 of the commonwealth or of a political subdivision thereof, including any person contracting or
9 subcontracting for public works.
10 “Public works”, the construction, reconstruction, alteration, remodeling, repair, or demolition of
11 any public building or other public work by a public agency.

46 SECTION 5. (a) Notwithstanding any general or special law to the contrary, a public agency
47 shall require, as a condition of bidding on a construction contract for public works, that all
48 construction managers, general contractors, and subcontractors at any tier maintain or participate
49 in a bona fide apprentice training program for each apprenticeable trade or occupation
50 represented in their workforce.
51 Such apprentice training programs shall be approved by the division of apprentice standards
52 within the executive office of labor and workforce development pursuant to sections 11H and 11I
53 of chapter 23 of the General Laws. Contractors and subcontractors shall register all apprentices
54 with the division and shall comply with applicable apprentice-to-journeyperson ratios established
55 by a trades licensing board or by the division.
56 A public agency may grant a waiver of the requirements of this subsection solely upon a written
57 determination that, after good-faith solicitation, no qualified and responsible bidder maintaining
58 or participating in a bona fide apprentice training program is reasonably available for a specific
59 trade or scope of work.
60 Such waiver shall:
3 of 6

61 be limited to the specific trade or scope of work for which no qualified bidder is available;
62 not apply to the entire project unless the public agency makes a separate written finding for each
63 affected trade;
64 be project-specific and non-transferable;
65 be retained as part of the public record for the contract; and
66 be reported by the public agency to the executive office of labor and workforce development, in
67 a form and manner prescribed by the office, within 30 days of issuance.
68 Nothing in this section shall be construed to require an apprenticeship program to qualify as an
69 employee welfare benefit plan under the federal Employee Retirement Income Security Act of
70 1974, 29 U.S.C. §§1001–1461.
71 (b) Any bidder for a public works project subject to this section who fails to maintain or
72 participate in a bona fide apprentice training program, and who has not been granted a waiver
73 pursuant to subsection (a), shall not be deemed a responsible and eligible bidder.
74 SECTION 6. (a) The eligible industry sectors within the Department of Elementary and
75 Secondary Education’s Innovation Career Pathways program shall be expanded to include
76 Building and Transportation.
77 (b) Within said sector, a Building and Transportation Innovation Pathway shall be established to
78 prepare high school students for high-demand careers across the built environment, including but
79 not limited to housing production and modern building systems; transportation networks;
80 infrastructure maintenance and public works; clean energy–driven technologies; and construction
81 management, project coordination, and civil engineering fields.
82 (c) The department shall develop a statewide program template that includes academic and
83 industry integration, STEM-aligned coursework, partnerships with contractors and public
4 of 6

84 agencies, including MassDOT and the MBTA, and a minimum of 100 hours of work-based
85 learning. The pathway shall meet all additional Innovation Career Pathways criteria and shall be
86 made available to all designated schools and districts.

94 SECTION 8. There shall be established a special commission on apprenticeships in the
95 commonwealth to study, evaluate, and make recommendations concerning the development,
96 accessibility, quality, funding, and utilization of apprenticeship and pre-apprenticeship programs
97 across all industry sectors, with particular attention to public works, construction, transportation,
98 infrastructure, and emerging industries.
99 The commission shall consist of the following members or their designees:
100 (i) the secretary of labor and workforce development, who shall serve as chair;
101 (ii) the commissioner of the division of apprentice standards;
102 (iii) the secretary of housing and economic development;
103 (iv) the secretary of transportation;
104 (v) the commissioner of elementary and secondary education;
105 (vi) the president of the Massachusetts AFL-CIO, or a designee;
106 (vii) a representative of union apprenticeship programs, appointed by the senate president;
5 of 6

107 (viii) a representative of open-shop or non-union apprenticeship programs, appointed by the
108 speaker of the house of representatives;
109 (ix) a representative of construction contractors, appointed by the senate president;
110 (x) a representative of minority- or women-owned businesses engaged in apprenticeable trades,
111 appointed by the speaker of the house; and
112 (xi) one member with expertise in workforce development or labor economics, appointed jointly
113 by the speaker of the house and the senate president.
114 The commission shall meet not less than twice annually and may convene additional meetings or
115 public hearings as it deems necessary. The commission shall study, including but not limited to:
116 (a) the adequacy of existing apprenticeship and pre-apprenticeship programs to meet current and
117 projected workforce needs;
118 (b) barriers to participation for workers, employers, and sponsors;
119 (c) alignment of apprenticeship programs with secondary and post-secondary education
120 pathways;
121 (d) funding mechanisms and appropriation strategies to expand and strengthen apprenticeship
122 programs; and
123 (e) statutory or regulatory changes necessary to improve apprenticeship utilization and outcomes.
124 Not later than December 31 of each year, the commission shall file a report of its findings and
125 any recommendations for legislation or appropriations with the clerks of the senate and house of
126 representatives, who shall forward the same to the house and senate chairs of the committees on
127 Ways and Means and the joint committee on Labor and Workforce Development.
128 The commission shall be supported administratively by the executive office of labor and
129 workforce development and shall exist until dissolved by act of the general court.
