To the Honorable Senate and House of Representatives,
I am filing for your consideration a bill entitled An Act Making Appropriations for Fiscal
Year 2025 (FY25) to Provide for Supplementing Certain Existing Appropriations and for Certain
Other Activities and Projects.
This legislation appropriates $2.45 billion gross/$947 million net to meet critical budget
needs and close Massachusetts’s Fiscal Year 2025 (FY25) on balance. Our proposal also
strengthens the state’s ability to weather economic uncertainty by building our reserves,
maximizing revenues from the voter-approved Fair Share surtax, and creating a new fund
dedicated to responding to federal actions.
Taken together, this budget proposal addresses costs incurred in FY25, which ended on
June 30, and other critical needs that will allow our administration to advance key priorities. The
MassHealth program accounts for the largest of these deficiencies, due to rising caseloads,
patient health acuity and unforeseen increases in the costs to provide care. This budget
recommends $2.046 billion gross to pay for services provided through MassHealth in FY25, at a
net cost to the state of $539.7 million after federal reimbursements. We recognize the importance
of continuing to manage spending in this critical safety net program and adjusting to the
changing Medicaid landscape. This budget also allocates:
• $162.7 million for a reserve to cover costs accrued by Massachusetts Sheriffs
• $75 million for housing preservation and stabilization
1 of 4

• $60.7 million for snow and ice removal
• $18.5 million for public health hospital operations
• $12 million for Universal School Meals
• $7.2 million for Department of Correction (DOC) Facilities operations
• $3.5 million for State Police operations
• $700,000 for the Suffolk County District Attorney
• $20 million for the Massachusetts Life Science Center
• $20 million to support costs associated with hosting the 2026 World Cup
• $6.8 million for the Clean Water Trust Fund
• $5 million for direct supports to reproductive health providers at risk of losing
federal funding
Earlier this summer, I filed a $130 million supplemental budget bill for FY26 that would
create new flexible resources and expand our administration’s toolkit for responding to budget
pressures created by federal actions in the fiscal year ahead. The bill I'm filing today continues to
strengthen Massachusetts’s financial footing so that we can thrive in an uncertain future by
building our reserves, including flexible funds that can be easily accessed to mitigate negative
impacts on state programming. We propose a $125 million deposit into the state’s Stabilization
Fund to continue to build our most important bulwark against an economic downturn, bringing
the balance to $8.23 billion. We are also recommending the creation of a new Economic
Resiliency and Federal Response Fund and directing a portion of excess capital gains and any
consolidated net surplus from FY25 to this new fund. This fund will provide an estimated $250
million in uncommitted resources to ensure that the Commonwealth remains resilient against the
negative impacts of federal budgetary and policy decisions.
Additionally, we are proposing to transfer $150 million of excess FY25 Fair Share surtax
revenues into the Student Opportunity Act Investment Fund to support our administration’s
commitment to providing world-class education in every community. As of July 21, the
Department of Revenue has certified receipt of $2.98 billion in surtax collections in FY25, which
is $1.7 billion above our spending threshold. By transferring a portion of these excess collections
to the SOA Investment Fund, we are supporting implementation of the SOA in Massachusetts
communities and continuing to meet our commitment to our students.
I am also filing several outside sections in this legislation, including provisions that will
increase government transparency, support public health, and streamline policy implementation.
Among these outside sections is a proposal that gives the Massachusetts Department of Public
2 of 4

Health the authority to decouple immunization recommendations and requirements in
Massachusetts from federal standards if necessary to maintain public health. This legislation is
one of several actions our administration has taken in recent months to protect Massachusetts’s
ability to provide necessary medical care despite actions at the federal level. With thanks to the
Legislature, this includes the recently signed provision ensuring the Department of Public Health
is able to distribute important prescription drugs.
Another important time-sensitive provision that I have filed relates to the preservation of
federal support for our Medicaid program. This proposal updates the current MCO assessment to
meet new federal requirements. I am also filing language that aligns with critical
recommendations made by the Special Commission on State Institutions, including a proposal to
make state institutional records over 75 years of age public. The goal of these provisions is to
make institutional records easier for loved ones and historians to access while respecting patient
privacy concerns.
We are also proposing to return to the historical practice in place prior to enactment of
the Work and Family Mobility Act (WFMA) of allowing the RMV to make public its driving
records that detail a person’s history of motor vehicle violations, with new guardrails included to
protect privacy and prohibit broad non-driver specific requests.
Due to uncertainty surrounding federal permitting, the bill also proposes more flexibility
in the state’s timeline for offshore wind procurements in response to changing market conditions.
The supplemental budget extends the deadline for the state to contract for 5,600 MW of offshore
wind from 2027 to 2029. Additionally, the bill removes the requirement that a solicitation be
conducted every 24 months.
Another outside section in this bill would set Sept. 1, 2026 as the statewide primary
election day, ensuring that the Secretary of State can meet all state and federal ballot deadlines.
I also want to reiterate my support for bills recently filed by our administration and
currently pending before the Legislature. As mentioned, the FY26 supplemental budget bill that I
filed in tandem with signing the FY26 budget earlier this summer provides critical resources to
help Massachusetts manage the unknown in the coming months. There are also proposals filed in
separate vehicles and still before the Legislature, such as increasing construction thresholds and
providing funding to support ongoing celebrations of our country’s 250th anniversary, of which I
would appreciate continued consideration.
I respectfully urge you to support my administration’s efforts to close the books for Fiscal
Year 2025 in a timely fashion by enacting this legislation promptly. Sufficient revenues are
available to finance the appropriations and other spending we have proposed in this bill, and we
are eager to keep Massachusetts strong and financially solid as we transition into the next fiscal
year.
