Section 2YYYY  of Chapter 29 of the Massachusetts General Laws requires the  Executive 
Office of Health and Human Services (EOHHS) to submit to the House and Senate 
Committees on Ways and Means and the Joint Committee on Mental Health and 
Substance Use and Recovery a quarterly report on expenditures from the  Substance Use 
Disorder Federal Reinvestment Trust Fund  (SUD Trust Fund)  that support the 
components of the roadmap for behavioral health reform .  
 
This report satisfies this requirement , including: (i) information on which components of 
the roadmap such funds are allocated to support; and (ii) a breakdown of the progress and 
status of any such components of the roadmap.  
 
SUD Trust  Fund expenditures  that support components of the Behavioral Health 
Roadmap comply  with the below categories defined in General Laws Part I, Title III, 
Chapter 29, Section 2YYYY : 
 
• Reduce stigma, expand access, support implementation,  and increase 
competencies for medications for substance use disorder (Medication for 
Addiction Treatment (MAT) ) 

   
 
   
 • Support access to evidence -based recovery services through peer and 
paraprofessional services (Peer supports)  
• Ensure appropriate assessment for levels of care utilizing American Society of 
Addiction Medicine or other evidence -based modalities and to support integration 
of physical health, mental health and substance use disorder care across all 
provider settings ( American Society of Addiction Medicine criteria (ASAM) )

Behavioral Health Roadmap: Initiatives Implemented to Date  
The accomplishments and activities for implementation of the Roadmap in fiscal  year 
2023.  
 
Launch in January 2023:  
• Community Behavioral Health Center (CBHC) providers began offering services, 
including outpatient mental health and SUD treatment services, Adult and Youth  
Community Crisis Stabilization  services  and Adult and Youth Mobile Crisis 
Intervention services .  These services provide both Mental Health and Substance 
Use Disorder treatment, including MAT, assessment,  and ongoing treatment.  
• Certified Peer Specialists services were launch ed in CBHCs and Mental Health 
Centers to ensure integration and whole person care for those i n need of recovery 
support.   
 
Impact of CBHCs  by the numbers  – through June 2024  
• 770,997 outpatient clinic visits , including for MAT,  provided  to 58,379 
MassHealth members since January 2023.   
• CBHC Mobile Crisis Intervention teams have provided 31,427 crisis evaluations 
to 17,035 adult  MassHealth  utilizers and 16,073 crisis evaluations to 10,971 youth  
MassHealth  utilizers since operational launch.  
o In 2024, 93% of adult and youth crisis evaluations, on average, were 
started within 60 minutes of member readiness  to engage in services . 99% 
of adults and 100% of youth, on average, were offered in -person, mobile 
evaluations.  
• 5,900 admissions to community crisis stabilization programs since the expansion 
of Adult CCS services and launch of Youth CCS services in January 2023.  
• 507 MassHealth members  have been able to avoid  the ED  by being transported  by 
police directly to a Community Behavioral Health Center for a BH Crisis 
Evaluation.  
• 229 MassHealth members have been directly  admitted to inpatient psychiatr ic 
units  from CBHCs, avoiding the ED for “medical  clearance”.

Behavioral Health Roadmap: Community Behavioral Health Centers  
In January 2023,  25 CBHCs , which expanded access to routine, urgent, and crisis 
treatment for mental health conditions and substance use disorders , enrolled with 
MassHealth and began offering services . In Summer 2023, an additional CBHC was 
added for the catchment area of Martha’s Vineyard.  In January 2024, a final CBHC site 
in Malden opened , for a total of 27 CBHC sites statewide.  
 
CBHCs  serve all ages and all communities across the Commonwealth.  
 
More information can be found at: https://www.mass.gov/info -details/community -
behavioral -health -centers   
 
 
 
 
Behavioral Health Roadmap: Behavioral Health Urgent Care   
 
MassHealth Community Mental Health Center  (CMHC)  providers who meet certain criteria may 
be designated as Behavioral Health Urgent Care  (BHUC) providers . Designated BHUCs are 
reimbursed above the CMHC rate for certain outpatient services.  
 
MassHealth  Community Mental Health Center providers who seek designation as a BHUC must 
attest to being able to provide all CMHC services as well as meeting the following requirements:  
• Appointments:  
o Appointments for diagnostic evaluation for new clients  are available on the same 
or next day of clinical operation, when clinically indicated based on initial intake;  
o Appointments for all existing clients  with an urgent behavioral health need are 
available on the same or next day of clinic operation ; 
o Urgent psychopharmacology and MAT evaluation available within 72 hours  of 
initial diagnostic evaluation and based on psychosocial assessment; All o ther 


   
 
   
 treatment appointments including follow -up appointments are available within 14 
calendar days.  
• Hours:  
o Each center designated as a BHUC must offer e xtended availability on Mondays 
through Fridays outside of the hours of 9am -5pm. At a minimum, BHUCs must  
offer  at least 8 hours  of extended availability per week during weekdays  and a t 
least two 4 -hour blocks of availability on weekends per month.  
  
Implementation Update:  
• The submission deadline for initial  BHUC  attestations  was June 30, 2022 . 
• As of Q 4 2023, there were  60 attested providers . By the end of Q3 2024, 14 additional 
providers attested for a total of 74.  Additional attestations are approved on a quarterly 
basis.  
• Approved ARPA funds will be utilized through a grant program to increase  culturally 
relevant care with an emphasis on servicing underserved communities and populations 
with underserved language needs, including individuals who are deaf or heard of hearing. 
Funds will be distributed during CY24 .
