Re: Facility Inspection – Essex County Correctional Facility, Middleton  
Dear Sheriff Coppinger:  
The Massachusetts Department of Public Health (Department) Division of Environmental Health Regulations and 
Standards (EHRS) conducted an inspection of the Essex County Correctional Facility on March 18, 2025 accompanied by 
Lieutenant Steven Wilkins and Ca ptain Jotham Garbin, in accordance with Department regulations 105 CMR 451.000: 
Minimum Health and Sanitation Standards and Inspection Procedures for Correctional Facilities.  
The inspection identified 259  total deficiencies: 103 deficiencies under the Required Standards (.100 and .200 series), 53 
repeat deficiencies under the Required Standards, 76 deficiencies under the Recommended Standards (.300 series), and 
27 repeat deficiencies under the Recommended Standards.  
Overview  
Section 1  provides details of all deficiencies, including repeat deficiencies, found during the inspection. These 
are categorized by Required Standards, Recommended Standards, or additional applicable regulatory standards.  
Section 2  provides information on areas that EHRS found to be compliant.  
Section 3  documents the areas that EHRS did not inspect.  
Section 4 provides information on submitting a Plan of Correction for the identified deficiencies. 
Section 5  outlines observations and recommendations related to the inspection.  The Commonwealth of Massachusetts

SECTION 4: Observations and Recommendations  
 
1. The inmate population was 758 at the time of inspection.  
2. During the Kitchen Inspection:  
a. EHRS noted detergent with no sanitizing solution within sanitizing buckets while the kitchen was actively 
in use. It was determined that soap was continuing to be incorrectly placed in sanitizing buckets. 
Sanitizing solution was provided , however, EHRS strongly recommended:  
i. Additional kitchen staff be trained on how to properly mix the sanitizing solution;  
ii. Kitchen staff develop a sanitizing distribution procedure to ensure sanitizing solutions of the 
proper concentration are distributed within the sanitizing buckets throughout the kitchen; and  
iii. Kitchen staff develop a procedure to test the sanitizing solution when made as well as 
throughout each shift to ensure the proper concentrations are maintained.  
b. The warewash machine was out -of-order and being serviced at the time of inspection. As an interim 
measure, the facility stated they would utilize Styrofoam trays and manually wash, rinse, and sanitize 
additional dishware until repairs are made.
