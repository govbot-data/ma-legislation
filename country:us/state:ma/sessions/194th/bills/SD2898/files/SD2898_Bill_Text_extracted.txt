LETTER FROM THE COMMISSIONER
Dear Honorable Members of the Committees on Ways and Means and the Joint Committee on Higher
Education:
I am pleased to submit this legislative report on the MassReconnect program, as we advance through
the second implementation year.
The report outlines the metrics being used to measure the MassReconnect program’s success, while
comparing the Fall 2024 cohort’s outcomes with those of the Fall 2023 cohort and the baseline of fall
2022. It also includes a first look at enrollment for FY2024, the first complete fiscal year since the
implementation of MassReconnect. As required by the FY25 GAA, this report has been developed in
consultation with the Massachusetts Association of Community Colleges (MACC).
This report indicates that MassReconnect is decisively driving enrollment upward among adults at our
fifteen (15) community colleges, with further increases seen in the second year of MassReconnect
across every subgroup examined herein.
In FY2025, we also have seen a further increase in the FAFSA completion rate among students in the
MassReconnect-eligible population. FAFSA completion ensures that students access all available aid,
maximizes federal funding, and keeps students’ costs and potential debt as low as possible—all of
which are outcomes worth celebrating.
On behalf of our Massachusetts community college sector and the thousands of students who benefit
from MassReconnect, I thank you for the continued support for the program. Questions about this
report may be directed to Alex Demou, Director of Constituent and Legislative Affairs, at
ademou@dhe.mass.edu.
Sincerely,
Noe Ortega

PROGRAM OVERVIEW
Now in its second year and with a $4 million increase in its annual appropriation,
MassReconnect provides a pathway to affordable higher education for the
nearly two million Massachusetts residents over the age of 25 who do not hold
a college credential.
Since the implementation of MassReconnect in Fall 2023, community colleges have experienced sharp
increases in enrollment. In the first Fall of MassReconnect, enrollment of community college students
25 years and older increased +12.0% (+2,638 students) over the prior fall. This was followed by an
increase of +26.8% (+6,594 students) this fall, making for a combined increase of +42.0% (+9,232
students) from fall 2022 to fall 2024. Particularly in this second fall of implementation, the demand for
free community college among adult learners remains high.
As a “last dollar” financial aid program, MassReconnect covers any remaining financial need for adults
age 25 or over without degrees who enroll in community colleges. After the application of other
federal and state aid for which each student qualifies, MassReconnect funding ensures that their
tuition and fees are fully covered, and it also provides an allowance for books, supplies, and other
approved costs.
To be eligible for a MassReconnect grant, students must:
• be at least 25 years of age or older as of the first day of classes;
• have physically resided in Massachusetts for at least one year, as of the start of the enrolled
term, with an intent to remain in Massachusetts consistent with Board of Higher Education
(BHE) policy;
• have not previously received an associate or bachelor's degree or the equivalent;
• complete the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) or the Massachusetts
Application for State Financial Aid (MASFA) as applicable for eligible students under the
Massachusetts Tuition Equity Law annually;
• be a U.S. Citizen, lawful permanent resident, or non-citizen eligible under Title IV regulations;
or have an approved eligibility status designated by the BHE pursuant to the Massachusetts
Tuition Equity Law;
• be enrolled in at least six credits per semester; and
• meet satisfactory academic progress according to the institution's requirements and federal
standards.
Awards are made directly by the community colleges at the point of enrollment throughout the
academic year. The Massachusetts Department of Higher Education continues to partner with the
community colleges and the Massachusetts Association of Community Colleges to streamline the
process of delivering assistance to those students.

METRIC 1: ENROLLMENT OF ADULT STUDENTS &
NUMBER OF GRANT RECIPIENTS
As a “last dollar” financial aid program, MassReconnect covers any remaining financial need for tuition
and fees for adults without degrees who enroll in community colleges, after the application of other
federal and state aid for which each student qualifies. It also functions as a messaging platform to
encourage adults to enroll or re-enroll in college, including some who will find their costs fully covered
by Pell Grants and/or other aid. Therefore, all of our metrics examine adults age 25 or over and
whether they received a MassReconnect grant, as well as MassReconnect grant recipients specifically.
This metric tracks enrollment by student type (various types of new students, as well as continuing).
Metric
Enrollment of adults age 25 or over in community colleges and number of MassReconnect grant
recipients, overall and among new student types.
Fall 2024 Summary
• From Fall 2023 to Fall 2024, overall enrollment of adults age 25 and over increased +26.8%
(+6,594). Over the two-year period from Fall 2022 to Fall 2024, overall enrollment of adults age 25
and over increased +42.0% (+9,232).
• From Fall 2023 to Fall 2024, among new students (new first-time, new transfer, and readmitted /
reactivated students) age 25 or over, enrollment increased +39.6% (+3,327).
• First-time freshman enrollment among adults age 25 and over increased +65.3% (+1,688). First-
time freshmen are students who are enrolling in college for the first time ever.
• New transfer enrollment among adults age 25 and over increased +39.4% (+977). New transfers
are students who have previously attended college elsewhere.
• Readmitted / reactivated enrollment among adults age 25 and over increased +19.8% (+662).
Readmitted or reactivated students were previously degree-seeking students at the same
institution and have reapplied after a lengthy period of non-enrollment, as defined by the
institution’s policies.
• Comparing the population of 7,835 MassReconnect grant recipients and the overall population of
students age 25 or over, the grant recipients were more likely than the overall population to be
first-time freshmen (16.6% and 13.7% respectively) or readmitted/reactivated students (15.2% and
12.9% respectively). Grant recipients and the overall population of students age 25 or over were
just as likely to be transfer students (11.4% and 11.1% respectively).

METRIC 2: RACE/ETHNICITY OF ADULT STUDENTS &
GRANT RECIPIENTS
This metric, like all others, looks at the entire group of students who are eligible for MassReconnect
to better measure the impact that the message “free community college” is having on enrollment of
adult learner subgroups (by race/ethnicity in this case), as well as the distribution of the direct aid by
subgroup.
Metric
Enrollment of students age 25 or over in community colleges and number of students receiving
MassReconnect grants, disaggregated by race/ethnicity.
Fall 2024 Summary
• From Fall 2023 to Fall 2024, there was enrollment growth among every racial/ethnic group in the
overall population of adults age 25 or over, including an +30.6% increase (+1,696) in Hispanic or
Latino students, a +23.8% increase (+1,131) in Black or African American students, and a +25.9%
increase (+2,777) in White students.
• Over the two-year period from Fall 2022 to Fall 2024, Hispanic or Latino students experienced the
highest increase of +57.4% (+2,637), followed by Black or African American students with an
increase of +43.0% (+1,771), and White students with +33.3% (+3,371).
• From Fall 2023 to Fall 2024, among new students (new first-time, new transfer, and readmitted /
reactivated students) age 25 or over, there was enrollment growth within every racial/ethnic group,
including +31.8% (+524) for Black or African American students, +42.0% (+847) for Hispanic or
Latino students, and +40.0% (+1,463) for White students.
• Comparing the population of MassReconnect grant recipients and the overall population of
students age 25 or over (students of known race only), the racial/ethnic diversity is comparable,
but the grant recipients were more likely than the overall population to be Hispanic or Latino
(25.5% and 24.6% respectively) or White (49.0% and 45.9% respectively), and less likely to be Black
or African American (17.1% and 20.0% respectively).

METRIC 4: AGE GROUP OF ADULT STUDENTS &
GRANT RECIPIENTS
MassReconnect is intended to offer all adults who do not have college degrees the opportunity to
benefit from a college education. Whether they are just beginning their career or are entering or
returning to college later in life, benefits may include achievement of personal, academic, and career
goals along with societal benefits such as greater civic engagement. This metric indicates which age
groups are taking advantage of this opportunity and at what scale.
Metric
Enrollment of students age 25 or over in community colleges and number of students receiving
MassReconnect grants, disaggregated by age group.
Fall 2024 Summary
• From Fall 2023 to Fall 2024, among all students age 25 or over, there was enrollment growth
within every age group, including a +22.9% increase (+3,258) in the 25–34 group and a +31.5%
increase (+2,090) in the 35–44 age group.
• Over the two-year period from Fall 2022 to Fall 2024, there was an enrollment increase of +33.3%
(+4,373) in the 25–34 age group and +52.3% increase (+2,998) in the 35–44 group.
• From fall 2023 to fall 2024, among new students age 25 or over, there was enrollment growth
within every age group, including an +36.8% increase (+1,788) in the 25–34 group and a +42.8%
increase (+3,292) in the 35–44 group.
• Comparing the population of MassReconnect grant recipients and the overall population of
students age 25 or over, both were predominantly ages 25–34 (56.7% of grant recipients and
56.1% of the overall population).

METRIC 5: FAFSA OR MASFA COMPLETION RATE
AMONG ADULT STUDENTS
Of all MassReconnect grant criteria, the requirement to complete the Free Application for Federal
Student Aid (FAFSA) or the new Massachusetts Application for State Financial Aid (MASFA) likely
presents the greatest barriers for students, as these forms are perceived as complex and stressful to
complete, particularly for families who are new to the college and financial aid application process. A
2023 paper from Brookings states:
The FAFSA has historically been a very difficult form to navigate. The process can be
especially challenging for low-income students, who tend to have fewer academic
supports and whose parents are less likely to have been through the college
application process. Financial challenges and anxieties associated with poverty can
further strain families’ ability to navigate complex processes.
Through MassReconnect, any Massachusetts resident age 25 or older without a prior degree
who enrolls at a community college for six or more credits per term is guaranteed aid to cover
tuition, fees, and an allowance for books and supplies—as long as they complete the FAFSA or
MASFA. This metric tells us what percentage of students who meet all other criteria for
MassReconnect have checked the final box of completing the FAFSA or MASFA and claimed all
aid available to them, up to and including a MassReconnect grant if needed. The Department
of Higher Education is working jointly with the Department of Elementary Secondary
Education to improve FAFSA completion rates. While there is more work to be done, the data
below indicate that the rates are improving year-over-year.
Metric
Percentage of adult students who completed the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) or
Massachusetts Application for State Financial Aid (MASFA), along with meeting all other primary
MassReconnect grant criteria (Massachusetts residency, no prior degree, enrolled for six or more
credits per term).
Note: With the very recent implementation of MASFA in January 2024, no completion data has yet been
published, but it will be provided in future reports.
Fall 2024 Summary
• In Fall 2024, among all students age 25 or over who met MassReconnect’s residency, credit and
prior education requirements, FAFSA completion was 88.3%, an increase of +4.7 percentage points
from Fall 2023.
• Over the two-year period from Fall 2022 to Fall 2024, the FAFSA completion rate increased among
this population by +11.0 percentage points.

14
• In Fall 2024, among new students age 25 or over who met MassReconnect’s residency, credit &
prior education requirements, FAFSA completion was 91.3%, an increase of +3.9 percentage points
from Fall 2023.
• Over the two-year period from fall 2022 to fall 2024, the FAFSA completion rate increased
among this population by +12.1 percentage points.
• Within the all-student subpopulation:
• From Fall 2023 to Fall 2024, there were increases in FAFSA completion rates among every
racial/ethnic group except for American Indian or Alaskan Native students (-3.4 percentage
points).
• White students had the largest increase of +6.2 percentage points, followed by Asian or Pacific
Islander students at +4.3 percentage points, students of two or more races at +4.0 percentage
points, Hispanic or Latino students at +3.4 percentage points, and Black or African American
students at +3.2 percentage points.
• Over the two-year period from Fall 2022 to Fall 2024, White students have had the largest increase
in FAFSA completion at +13.8 percentage points, followed by Asian or Pacific Islander students at
+12.3 percentage points.
• FAFSA completion rates among Asian or Pacific Islander and Black or African American students
within this population were lower than the overall rate, at 80.0% and 87.2% respectively. Rates
among Hispanic or Latino and White students were near the overall average at 90.1% and
89.7% respectively.
• There were also increases in these rates among every gender group dating back to Fall 2023,
including an increase of +7.7 percentage points for male students and +3.6 percentage points
for female students. The 83.9% rate for male students still trails the overall rate.
• Over the two-year period from Fall 2022 to Fall 2024, male students had an increase in FAFSA
completion of +17.6 percentage points.
• Within the new student subpopulation:
• From Fall 2023 to Fall 2024, there were increases in FAFSA completion rates among every
racial/ethnic group.
• Among the larger racial/ethnic groups, Asian or Pacific Islander students had the largest
increase of +4.9 percentage points, followed by Black or African American students at +4.4
percentage points, White students at +3.8 percentage points, and Hispanic or Latino students
at +2.8 percentage points over 2023. Nonetheless, FAFSA completion rates among Asian or
Pacific Islander and Black or African American students within this population were lower than
the overall rate, at 85.9% and 90.1% respectively.
• Over the two-year period from Fall 2022 to Fall 2024, Asian or Pacific Islander students have had
the largest increase in FAFSA completion at +18.5 percentage points, followed by White students
at +14.0 percentage points.

• Among the smaller racial/ethnic groups, American Indian or Alaskan Native student had the
largest increase of +7.3 percentage points, followed by students of two or more races at +6.4
percentage points.
• There were also increases in these rates among every gender group since Fall 2023, including
an increase of +6.1 percentage points for male students and +2.9 percentage points for female
students. The 87.9% rate for male students still trails the overall rate.
• Over the two-year period from fall 2022 to fall 2024, male students had an increase in

• While these improvements in FAFSA completion rates among the target population are significant
, according to this analysis, there were 2,398 enrolled students who otherwise qualified for
MassReconnect but did not complete FAFSA. These included 557 Black or African American
students, 563 Hispanic or Latino students, and 999 White students or, by gender, 1,475 female
students and 1,125 male students. Additional emphasis on FAFSA completion efforts may improve
the rates and equitable access to this program.

METRIC 6: RETENTION OF ADULT STUDENTS &
GRANT RECIPIENTS
A key metric is the percentage of enrolled students who return for a second year at their initial
institution.
Metric
Percentage of students enrolling in community colleges in the fall term who return in the subsequent
year at their initial institution, for the overall population of students age 25 or over enrolled for six or
more credits, and for students receiving MassReconnect grants.
Fall 2023 Summary
• Comparing the Fall 2022 cohort (pre-MassReconnect) to the Fall 2023 cohort (the first
MassReconnect cohort), among new students (new first-time, new transfer, and readmitted /
reactivated students) age 25 or over, retention rates were mostly stable.
• First-time freshman students age 25 and over enrolled full-time experienced a retention rate of
52.5%, a decrease of -6.9 percentage points over fall 2022. First-time freshman students enrolled
part-time experienced an increase of +2.6 percentage points (51.9% for the fall 2023 cohort
compared to 49.3% for the fall 2022 cohort).
• The retention rates of transfer students age 25 and over were essentially flat. The rate for transfer
students enrolled full-time was 56.2%, +0.3 percentage points higher than the Fall 2022 cohort.
Transfer students enrolled part-time had a retention rate of 51.4%, +0.3 percentage points higher
than the fall 2022 cohort.
• The retention rates of readmitted/reactivated students age 25 and over were also essentially
flat. Those enrolled full-time had a retention rate of 46.7%, +0.3 percentage points higher than
the fall 2022 cohort. Readmitted/reactivated students enrolled part-time had a retention rate
of 49.5%, -0.4 percentage points lower than the fall 2022 cohort.
• Comparing the fall 2022 cohort (pre-MassReconnect) to the fall 2023 cohort (the first
MassReconnect cohort), among continuing students age 25 or over, retention rates were mostly
stable. The retention rate of continuing students enrolled full-time was relatively flat (54.3% for the
fall 2022 cohort and 54.0% for the fall 2023 cohort). Continuing students enrolled part-time had a
retention rate of 53.5%, a decrease of -1.9 percentage points from the fall 2022 cohort.

METRIC 7: ANNUAL UNDUPLICATED HEADCOUNT
OF ADULT STUDENTS (SNAPSHOT)
For this report, we primarily use data on students enrolled in the Fall semester, as it is the most recent
data available at the time of drafting and provides the most detailed information on student
characteristics, such as student type and demographics. To complement this data, we include annual
enrollment data based on the fiscal year as a secondary, lagging metric. Fiscal year data becomes

FY2024 Summary
• From FY2023 to FY2024, overall enrollment of adults age 25 and over increased 19.0% (+6,001).
• There was enrollment growth within every racial/ethnic group in the overall population of adults
age 25 or over, including +29.0% (+1,810) for Hispanic or Latino students, +18.5% (+1,073) for
Black or African American students, and +15.1% (+2,128) for White students.
• Overall enrollment of male adults age 25 or over increased +25.6% (+2,235) followed by female
adults at +16.2% (+3,642).
• The 25–34 age group, which is double the size of the next largest age group, increased enrollment
by +14.8% (+2,755). The 35–44 age group increased +23.5% (+1,952).

PLANNING FOR ADDITIONAL METRICS &
ASSESSMENT
• Other Barriers to Enrollment: The metrics outlined in this document only examine the enrolled
population. Metrics that measure barriers leading up to enrollment are difficult to define. Going
forward, we expect to engage in qualitative analysis to gain a better understanding of why
potential participants are not applying or enrolling, with a particular emphasis on ensuring the
program is being accessed equitably by different subgroups.
• Longer-Term Success Metrics: After more time has elapsed, we will be able to report on longer-
term success metrics. This will include ongoing evaluation to establish whether receiving a
MassReconnect grant or any associated interventions has a positive impact on student retention
and completion. It should however be noted that MassReconnect was designed as an access tool,
not a completion tool. The community college segment has identified the SUCCESS program,
funded through a separate appropriation, as a tool to help with completion.
