75 Section 70. Local Option Rent Board for Manufactured Housing Communities
76 (a) Notwithstanding chapter 40P, a city by ordinance or a town by by-law may establish a
77 rent board for the purpose of regulating rents and minimum standards for the use or occupancy of
78 manufactured homes, as defined in section 32Q of chapter 140, and the eviction of tenants

79 therefrom. A rent board established by a city or town may regulate such rents, standards and
80 evictions so as to remove hardships or correct inequities for both the owners and tenants of
81 manufactured housing communities. A rent board shall have all the powers necessary or
82 convenient to perform its functions and may make rules and regulations, require the registration,
83 by owners of manufactured housing communities under penalty of perjury, of information
84 relating to manufactured housing accommodations, sue and be sued, compel the attendance of
85 persons and the production of papers and information and issue appropriate orders that shall be
86 binding on both the owners and tenants of manufactured homes. Violations of any ordinance or
87 by-law adopted pursuant to this act or any order of a rent board shall be punishable by a fine of
88 not more than $1,000. Manufactured housing community owners holding license in a city or
89 town that has established a board shall not increase the rent of manufactured home residents
90 during any 12-month period, even if new lease agreements are signed or other rental agreements
91 are entered into, by an amount of more than the most recent Chained Consumer Price Index for
92 All Urban Consumers. For this section, the term “Chained Consumer Price Index for All Urban
93 Consumers” or “C-CPI-U”, shall mean the consumer price index for all urban consumers,
94 defined in 26 U.S.C. § 1(f)(6) and published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics of the Department
95 of Labor. Owners may increase the manufactured housing community residents’ rent by an
96 amount of more than the C-CPI-U upon a finding of the board that such increase will yield a fair
97 net operating income pursuant to this section.
98 (b) In regulating the rents for such manufactured homes, a rent board may make rent
99 adjustments, either upward or downward, as may be necessary to assure that rents for the
100 manufactured homes in the city or town are established at levels which yield to owners a fair net
101 operating income for such units. The following factors, among other relevant factors, which the
5 of 20

102 board may define by regulation, shall be considered in determining whether manufactured
103 housing community accommodations yield a fair net operating income:
104 (1) increases or decreases in property taxes;
105 (2) unavoidable increases or any decreases in operating and maintenance expenses;

107 ordinary repair, replacement, and maintenance;
108 (4) increases or decreases in space, services, equipment, or other similar factors;
109 (5) substantial deterioration of the manufactured housing community other than as a
110 result of ordinary wear and tear;
111 (6) failure to perform ordinary repair, replacement, and maintenance;
112 (7) Notwithstanding any other provision of this section, the board may not issue any
113 order that violates this chapter or any other federal, state, or local statutes, regulations,
114 ordinances, or any other laws.

314 Section 32T. Manufactured Housing Dispute Resolution Program
15 of 20

315 (a) There shall be a manufactured housing dispute resolution program to assist
316 manufactured housing communities with resolving disputes. A resident, owner or operator of a
317 manufactured housing community may file a complaint with the attorney general alleging a
318 violation of sections 32A to 32S, inclusive, regulations promulgated by the attorney general
319 pursuant to said sections 32A to 32S, inclusive, or manufactured housing community rules
320 approved pursuant to paragraph (5) of section 32L. Complaints filed pursuant to this section shall
321 be in writing and shall include an acknowledgement signed by the party making the complaint
322 that the party is aware of the dispute resolution program created by this section.
323 (b) The attorney general shall review all complaints filed pursuant to this section. The
324 attorney general may take action upon such complaints or refer the complaints to the
325 manufactured housing commission for resolution.
326 (c) Upon receipt of a complaint from the attorney general, the commission shall assign 1
327 of its members to conduct a mediation between the parties involved in the dispute; provided,
328 however, that commissioners or alternate commissioners appointed pursuant to section 108 of
329 chapter 6 as residents or owners of a manufactured housing community shall not serve as
330 mediators. The mediator may gather information that the mediator deems necessary to determine
331 whether a violation has occurred. After reviewing information from the parties involved in the
332 dispute, the mediator shall provide recommendations for the resolution of the dispute. If the
333 parties accept the mediator’s proposal, or subsequently negotiate a settlement, the mediator shall
334 notify both parties in writing of the terms agreed to by the parties involved in the dispute. If the
335 parties fail to agree to a resolution of the dispute during the mediation process, 1 or both parties
336 may request that the commission hear and issue a decision on the dispute pursuant to subsection
337 (d). The request shall be made in writing not later than 30 days after the close of the mediation.
16 of 20

338 All requests for a hearing shall include a fee of $25 paid by the party requesting a hearing,
339 subject to adjustment pursuant to section 3B of chapter 7, which the commission shall deposit
340 into the Manufactured Housing Trust Fund established by section 2GGGGGGG of chapter 29.
341 (d) A hearing held pursuant to subsection (c) shall be conducted by 3 members of the
342 manufactured housing commission. One of the 3 members shall be the member who is the
343 resident of a manufactured housing community and 1 shall be the member who is the owner of a
344 manufactured housing community. Commission members appointed by the governor pursuant to
345 section 108 of chapter 6 as either owners or residents, and that own or reside in the manufactured
346 housing community that is the subject of the complaint, shall be prohibited from participating in
347 hearings involving that manufactured housing community. When a commission member is so
348 excluded from hearing a complaint, the corresponding alternate member of the commission shall
349 replace the affected member and hear the complaint. The third member who hears the complaint
350 shall not be the member who conducted the mediation of the complaint. Not later than 45 days
351 after the hearing, the commission shall issue a written decision as to whether a violation has
352 occurred and shall order appropriate action, if any, to be taken by the owner, resident, operator or
353 any of the parties.
354 (e) Costs incurred under this section including, but not limited to, costs to file or defend a
