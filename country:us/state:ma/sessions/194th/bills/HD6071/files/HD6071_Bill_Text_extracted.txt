Introduction
The Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (Department) respectfully
submits this Report to the House and Senate Committees on Ways and Means on rural
school aid pursuant to Chapter 9 of the Acts of 2025, line-item 7061-9813. The line-item
states, in relevant part:
“For rural school aid to eligible towns and regional school districts, excluding
vocational schools, independent agricultural, technical schools and charter
schools; provided, that a school district shall be eligible for rural school aid if a
school district has a student density of not more than 35 students per square mile
and an average annual per capita income of not more than the average annual
per capita income for the commonwealth for the same period; provided further,
that rural school aid shall be allocated equitably in the following priority order: (i)
school districts serving less than 11 students per square mile; (ii) school districts
serving not more than 21 students per square mile; and (iii) school districts
serving not more than 35 students per square mile; provided further, that not later
than December 1, 2025, the department of elementary and secondary education
shall submit a report to the house and senate committees on ways and means
detailing: (a) its recommendations for additional adjustments to the rural school
aid calculation for fiscal year 2027 to improve the accuracy and equity of the
student density component and the per capita income component; and (b) the
calculation and planned distribution of funds to school districts; provided further,
that not later than January 30, 2026, any district receiving funds under this item
shall submit a plan to the department of elementary and secondary education
outlining steps the district will take to increase regional collaboration,
consolidation or other efficiencies over the next 3 fiscal years;...”
Rural school aid (line-item 7061-9813) was initially funded in fiscal year 2019 at a level
of $1.5 million. Under the initial appropriation, 33 school districts (districts) were eligible
for funding. The program was subsequently funded at $3.5 million in fiscal year 2020,
$3.0 million in fiscal year 2021, $4.0 million in fiscal year 2022, $5.5 million in fiscal year
2023, and $15 million in fiscal year 2024, and $16 million in fiscal year 2025. The fiscal
year 2026 budget authorized $12 million for rural school aid.
Rural school aid recipients
Eligibility for rural school aid is currently based on two criteria per the line-item: per capita
income and student density. School districts were eligible for rural school aid in fiscal year
2026 if they had a student density of not more than 35 students per square mile and a per
capita income less than the state average of $57,834 based on Massachusetts Department
of Revenue data.1 After determining income eligibility, the Department of Elementary and

Secondary Education calculates student densities (current foundation enrollment divided
by district square mileage) and assigns districts that meet the density criteria to priority
groups. In fiscal year 2026, there were three priority groups:
• Priority 1 included districts that serve not more than 11 students per square mile.
• Priority 2 included districts that serve more than 11 and not more than 21 students
per square mile.
• Priority 3 included districts that serve more than 21 and not more than 35 students
per square mile.
Consistent with the line-item language, the Department set aside more of the available
FY2026 funding for Priority 1 districts, leaving the remaining balance for Priority 2 and 3
districts. The rural school aid that the districts in each priority group receive is based on a
prorated share of their FY2026 Chapter 70 target aid. The Department allocated $6 million
of available funding for Priority 1 districts, $3.6 million for Priority 2 districts, and $2.4
million for Priority 3 districts. The total FY2026 line-item amount has been divided across
each priority group in the same manner as FY2025. For all three groups, the Department
calculated the target aid amounts for each district based on current Chapter 70 aid,
summed these amounts, and gave the districts in each priority group a prorated share of
their target aid totals based on available funding. Priority 1 districts received a higher
percentage of aid than Priority 2 districts, and Priority 1 and 2 districts received a higher
percentage of aid than Priority 3 districts. Sixty-five districts will receive rural school aid in
fiscal year 2026. Awards ranged from $13,518 for Warwick to $1,164,818 for Central
Berkshire Regional School District; see Table 1 for the funding distributions for the last five
years.

Recommendations
The Department respectfully reiterates some recommendations made in its fiscal year
2025 report and makes additional recommendations for consideration for future
adjustments to the rural school aid line-item.
Require that a portion of rural school aid be used for efforts that increase
regional collaboration, consolidation, or other efficiencies.
While the current legislation requires that rural school aid recipients submit a plan
indicating the steps they will take to increase regional collaboration, consolidation, or
other efficiencies, the use of these funds is not directly linked to these efforts. We
recommend that the legislature mandate that a portion of rural school aid be used directly
in support of these efforts, consistent with the Commonwealth’s longstanding
commitment to promoting consolidation, greater efficiencies, and collaboration in school
district operations.
9

Require districts to submit a short survey at the end of each fiscal year
instead of a three-year plan in January.
The legislature should streamline reporting requirements for districts that receive rural
school aid to reduce administrative burden and standardize the information that the
Department collects. We recommend that the legislature eliminate the requirement that
districts submit three-year plans and instead direct the Department to collect a short
survey from participating districts 60 days after the close of each fiscal year to:
• Report on how rural school aid was used;
• Describe efforts to improve efficiencies and promote shared services,
consolidation, and regionalization; and
• Identify steps the state can take to assist with these efforts.
Allow the Department to submit one comprehensive report to the
legislature following the annual survey collection.
The current budget language requires the Department to submit two reports to the
legislature each year, one report two months before rural school aid recipients are required
to submit their three-year plans via an annual survey, and one report two months after
plans are submitted. Consistent with the previous recommendation, we recommend that
the Department submit one legislative report each year in October following an annual
survey of recipient districts conducted after the close of the fiscal year. This approach will
simplify report requirements and give the legislature the information that it needs regarding
districts’ use of funds and efforts at collaboration, consolidation, and other efficiencies to
assist the legislature in planning for the upcoming fiscal year.
Use three years of data to determine income eligibility and student
density to minimize the impact of year-over-year changes.
We recommend that the legislature allows districts to qualify for rural school aid based on
income and student density data from the most recent three years to address the impact
that small changes in a district’s income or enrollment can have on their eligibility to
receive rural school aid from year to year.
Allow districts that receive rural school aid to carry over unexpended
funds for one fiscal year.
Currently, districts that receive rural school aid are required to expend the funds in the
same fiscal year that the funds are awarded. We recommend that the legislature allow
districts that receive rural school aid to carry over unexpended funds for one fiscal year
following the fiscal year in which the funds are awarded.
10

Allow a portion of rural school aid to be used by DESE to support
emerging regionalization efforts.
Consistent with recommendations made by recipients, we respectfully recommend that
the Department be provided with the discretion to use a portion of rural school aid to
support emerging regionalization studies and efforts.
