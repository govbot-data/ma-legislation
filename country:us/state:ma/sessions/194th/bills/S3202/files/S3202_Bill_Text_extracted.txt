2  “SECTION XX. Chapter 32A of the General Laws is hereby amended by inserting after 
3 section 34 the following section:-  
4  Section 35. (a) For the purpose of this section, the term “doula services” shall have the 
5 following meaning:  
6  “Doula Services” are physical, emotional, and informational support, but not medical 
7 care, provided by trained doulas to individuals and families from conception until twelve months 
8 after pregnancy, labor, childbirth, adoption, miscarriage, stillbirth or abortion. Doula services 
9 include but are not limited to:  
10  (1) continuous labor and delivery support, inclusive of all outcomes;  
11  (2) support for pregnancy loss or infant loss, including bereavement home or in-person 
12 visits;  
13  (3) accompanying individuals to health care and social services appointments;  

2 of 14 14  (4) connecting individuals to community-based and state- and federally-funded resources, 
15 including those which address social determinants of health;  
16  (5) being on-call around the time of birth, adoption, loss or abortion, as well as providing 
17 on-call support in-person or via telehealth for individuals’ questions or concerns;  
18  (6) support for other individuals providing care for a birthing or adoptive parent, 
19 including spouses, partners, and other family members.  
20  (7) educational and informational support including but not limited to childbirth 
21 preparation, newborn care, perinatal mental health, and postpartum recovery.  
22  (8) empowering individuals to advocate for their preferences, needs and rights during 
23 pregnancy, labor and delivery, and postpartum.  
24  (b) Any coverage offered by the commission to an active or retired employee of the 
25 commonwealth and their dependents insured under the group insurance commission (hereinafter 
26 “policy”) shall provide coverage for all doula services. Coverage provided for doula services 
27 shall not be subject to any deductible, coinsurance, copayment or any other cost-sharing 
28 requirement. Provided, however, that deductibles shall be required if the applicable plan is 
29 governed by the Federal Internal Revenue Code and would lose its tax-exempt status as a result 
30 of the prohibition on such for these services.  
31  (c) No policy shall require a referral for doula services by any other health care provider 
32 as a condition of reimbursement.  
33  (d) Doulas shall be reimbursed for their services at a rate at least equivalent to 
34 MassHealth’s reimbursement rate for doula services.  

3 of 14 35  (e) A policy shall cover, without prior authorization, a minimum of twenty hours of 
36 prenatal and postpartum doula services per pregnancy, and continuous support throughout labor 
37 and delivery, inclusive of all pregnancy outcomes. Within the authorized number of doula hours, 
38 policies shall not impose limitations on the amount of time a doula can bill per patient per day.  
39  (f) Policies must establish a process to approve coverage of additional hours of doula 
40 services in cases where the patient has heightened risk or need.  
41  (g) Policies shall follow the doula credentialing requirements developed by the 
42 Massachusetts Department of Public Health, and may not impose any additional credentialing 
43 requirements. Additionally, any doula approved as a MassHealth Doula Provider shall be 
44 deemed as meeting all credentialing requirements.

228  SECTION XX. Doula Advisory Committee: There is hereby created a Doula Advisory 
229 Committee.  
230  (1) The committee shall consist of 10-12 members to be appointed by the Governor, or 
231 designee.  
232  All but 2 of the members shall be practicing doulas from the community representing a 
233 range of experience levels and a diversity of lived experience; the remaining 2 members shall be 
234 individuals from the community who have experienced pregnancy as a MassHealth member and 
235 are not practicing doulas.  
236  The members of the committee shall represent an equitable geographic distribution from 
237 across the Commonwealth, including representation from areas within the Commonwealth where 
238 maternal and infant outcomes are worse than the state average, as evidenced by the MA 
239 Department of Public Health’s most current perinatal data available at the time the member is 
240 appointed.  
241  (2) The committee shall be convened within six months of passage of this law.  

13 of 14 242  (3) Of the initial appointments to the Doula Advisory Committee, half shall be appointed 
243 to a term of 2 years and half shall be appointed to a term of 18 months. Thereafter, all terms shall 
244 be 2 years. The Governor, or designee, shall fill vacancies as soon as practicable.  
245  (4) At least once every 8 weeks, the Division of Medical Assistance shall meet with the 
246 Doula Advisory Committee to consult about MassHealth’s coverage of doula services, including 
247 but not limited to the following:  
248  (a) the standards and processes around billing for and prompt reimbursement of doula 
249 services;  
250  (b) establishing grievance procedures for doulas, MassHealth members, and health care 
251 providers about MassHealth’s coverage of doula services, the provision of doula services to 
252 MassHealth members, and bias that doulas face as they try to integrate into birth teams;  
253  (c) maintaining a reimbursement rate for doula services that incentivizes and supports a 
254 diverse workforce representative of the communities served, and establishing a recurring 
255 timeframe to review that rate in light of inflation and changing costs of living in the 
256 commonwealth;  
257  (5) Each year, the Doula Advisory Committee must, by a majority vote of a quorum of its 
258 members, select an individual to serve as its chairperson for a one year term. The Doula 
259 Advisory Committee may replace the chairperson in the same manner mid-term.  
260  (6) The Doula Advisory Committee may, by a majority vote of a quorum of its members, 
261 reduce the frequency of meetings with MassHealth to less than once every 8 weeks.  

14 of 14 262  (7) The Division of Medical Assistance shall seek resources to offer reasonable 
263 compensation to members of the Doula Advisory Committee for fulfilling their duties, and shall 
264 reimburse members for actual and necessary expenses incurred while fulfilling their duties.
