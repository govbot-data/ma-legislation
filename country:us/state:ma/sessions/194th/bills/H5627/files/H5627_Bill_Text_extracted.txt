1  SECTION 1. Chapter 6A of the General Laws is hereby amended by inserting after 
2 section 16GG the following 4 sections:- 
3  Section 16HH. For the purposes of this section and sections 16II to 16KK, inclusive, the 
4 following words shall, unless the context clearly requires otherwise, have the following 
5 meanings: 
6  “Abusive treatment”, physical, verbal or mental abuse, including, but not limited to, 
7 bullying, either directed at or committed by individual home care consumers, surrogates, family 
8 members, home care workers, other caregivers and any other individuals present in the home 
9 care consumers’ household when the home care worker is providing home care services. 
10  “Executive office”, the executive office of health and human services. 
11  “Home care agency”, any business, including a sole proprietorship, nonprofit 
12 organization or other entity that: (i) provides home care services to consumers in the consumers’ 

2 of 18 13 residences for compensation; (ii) represents itself as a home care agency by name, advertising or 
14 communications to the public, or uses the terms “home care agency” or “home care” in the 
15 entity’s name; or (iii) procures, offers, refers, provides or attempts to provide home care services 
16 by matching individuals seeking home care services to entities that provide home care services; 
17 provided, however, that “home care agency” shall not include: (A) an entity operated by either 
18 the federal government or the commonwealth that provides home care services; (B) an entity that 
19 limits its business to the provision of house cleaning services; (C) an entity designated as an 
20 aging services access point pursuant to section 4B of chapter 19A; (D) a hospice program 
21 licensed under section 57D of chapter 111; or (E) a home health agency as defined in section 
22 51K of chapter 111. 
23  “Home care consumer”, an individual receiving home care services or any legal 
24 representative of such individual. 
25  “Home care services”, supportive services provided to an individual in their residence to 
26 enable such individual to remain in their residence safely and comfortably, including, but not 
27 limited to, assistance with eating, toileting, dressing, bathing, transferring, ambulation and other 
28 activities of daily living, housekeeping, personal laundry and companionship; provided, 
29 however, that “home care services” shall not include: (i) hospice services provided by an entity 
30 subject to licensure under section 57D of chapter 111; (ii) home health services provided by an 
31 entity subject to licensure under section 51K of chapter 111; (iii) services provided by a personal 
32 care attendant in the commonwealth’s personal care attendant program as defined in 130 CMR 
33 422.000; or (iv) services provided under the MassHealth adult foster care program as defined in 
34 130 CMR 408.402. 

3 of 18 35  “Home care worker”, an individual employed by a home care agency who provides home 
36 care services to a consumer in the consumer’s residence. 
37  “PCA quality home care workforce council”, the council established in section 71 of 
38 chapter 118E.  
39  “Personal care attendant”, as defined in section 70 of chapter 118E. 
40  “Surrogates”, as defined in section 70 of chapter 118E. 
41  "Violent act", any act involving: (i) an assault or other infliction or threat of infliction of 
42 death or bodily harm; or (ii) damage to, or destruction of, real or personal property committed by 
43 individual home care consumers, surrogates, family members, home care workers, other 
44 caregivers and any other individuals present in the home care consumers’ household when the 
45 home care worker is providing home care services. 
46  Section 16II. (a) No person or entity shall establish, maintain, operate or hold itself out as 
47 a home care agency, provide home care services or use the words “home care” to describe its 
48 services without a home care agency license issued pursuant to subsection (b); provided, 
49 however, that home care agency licensure shall be required for home care agencies that directly 
50 employ home care workers and home care agencies that contract with entities that employ home 
51 care workers. 
52  (b)(1) The executive office shall issue for a term of 3 years, and may renew for like 
53 terms, a home care agency license to any person or entity that the executive office deems 
54 responsible and suitable to establish or maintain a home care agency and which meets the 

4 of 18 55 requirements of the rules and regulations promulgated pursuant to subsection (e). A license 
56 issued under this subsection shall not be transferable or assignable. 
57  (2) The executive office may suspend, revoke or refuse to renew for cause a home care 
58 agency license in accordance with regulations promulgated by the executive office pursuant to 
59 subsection (e); provided, however, that the office shall provide home care agencies an 
60 opportunity for appeal of such suspension, revocation or refusal of renewal. 
61  (3) License application and renewal fees shall be established pursuant to section 3B of 
62 chapter 7. 
63  (c) If the executive office determines a licensee has failed or refused to comply with 
64 requirements established under this section or the regulations promulgated thereunder, the 
65 executive office may: (i) deny an application for licensure; (ii) modify, suspend or revoke a 
66 license; or (iii) issue a fine of not more than $500 for each day of such failure or refusal to 
67 comply.  
68  (d) The executive office, in collaboration with the executive office of aging and 
69 independence and the department of public health, may conduct surveys and investigations to 
70 monitor and enforce compliance with this section. 
71  (e)(1) The executive office shall, in consultation with the executive office of aging and 
72 independence, the department of public health and the home care oversight advisory council 
73 established in section 16KK, promulgate rules and regulations for the licensing and conduct of a 
74 home care agency. 

5 of 18 75  (2) The executive office shall ensure that home care agency licensure, reporting and 
76 oversight requirements align with and avoid the duplication of licensure, reporting and oversight 
77 requirements across the long-term care services and support system, including home health 
78 agency licensure and provider monitoring conducted by aging services access points.  
79  (3) The regulations shall include, but shall not be limited to: 
80  (i) background checks for all home care workers which shall include, but shall not be 
81 limited to: (A) Massachusetts criminal background checks; (B) state or county criminal history 
82 checks for each location outside of the commonwealth in which the home care worker is known 
83 to have lived or worked during the previous 5 years; (C) a review of the  United States Office of 
84 Inspector General List of Excluded Individuals/Entities; (D) a review of the nurse aide registry; 
85 (E) if applicable, a review of professional certifications or other credentials; and (F) for all home 
86 care workers who will transport home care consumers, verification of licensure, auto insurance 
87 and driving records for not less than the previous 5 years; 
88  (ii) minimum standards for consumer-specific service plans and contracts that home care 
89 agencies shall develop with home care consumers; provided, however, that the plans shall 
90 include: (A) a detailed description of services, including services the home care agency is 
91 subcontracting to another agency or provider; (B) a written unit rate and total cost of services, 
92 inclusive of any additional fees or deposits; and (C) home care agency contact information for 
93 consumer questions; 
94  (iii) minimum coverage requirements for workers’ compensation insurance and liability 
95 insurance for the home care agency; 

6 of 18 96  (iv) maintenance of a payroll process that follows all state and federal labor and wage 
97 laws for the home care agency; 
98  (v) annual and ongoing training and competency requirements for home care workers and 
99 home care agency staff that interact with home care consumers; provided, however, that 
100 minimum training and competency requirements shall include: (A) confidentiality and privacy 
101 rights of home care consumers; (B) infection control and communicable diseases; (C) handling 
102 of emergencies, including safety and falls prevention; (D) observing, reporting and documenting 
103 changes in home care consumer needs and environment; (E) identifying and reporting suspected 
104 abuse, neglect or misappropriation of property; (F) understanding Alzheimer’s disease and 
105 dementia, including person-centered care, activities of daily living, safety and dementia-related 
106 behaviors and communication; and (G) employee rights and remedies under the 
107 commonwealth’s wage and hour laws; provided further, that the trainings shall be culturally and 
108 linguistically competent for the trainee; 
109  (vi) policies and procedures to ensure home care workers have safe working conditions, 
110 adequate safety training and a process for submitting complaints; 
111  (vii) development and maintenance of an emergency preparedness plan; 
112  (viii) quality metrics and standards for services provided to home care consumers, 
113 including reporting mechanisms for tracking performance of home care agencies; 
114  (ix) annual reporting by home care agencies on quality and any significant changes 
115 related to home care agency finances; 

7 of 18 116  (x) requirements and written processes for the timely reporting of home care consumer 
117 complaints; and  
118  (xi) requirements for home care agencies to provide adequate equipment and supplies for 
119 home care workers. 
120  (f) The executive office shall: 
121  (i) review and process licensure applications, including a suitability review that ensures 
122 the applicant shall meet the obligations and conditions of licensure, including, but not limited to: 
123  (A) a review of the state debarment list maintained by the operational services division 
124 and federal Office of Inspector General List of Excluded Individuals/Entities;  
125  (B) a competency review where all individuals with not less than a 5 per cent ownership 
126 stake in the home care agency shall submit the individuals’ names, contact information, 
127 companies where they have not less than a 5 per cent ownership interest, any civil or criminal 
128 findings against said individual and a completed background check; and 
129  (C) a review to ensure that applicants have the financial capacity to provide ongoing 
130 home care services while maintaining compliance with labor and employment laws; 
131  (ii) establish a process for a change of ownership that includes advanced notice to home 
132 care consumers and home care workers and a review by the executive office to determine if the

134  (iii) work with other state agencies to investigate and resolve complaints against the 
135 licensee; and

137  Section 16JJ. (a) The executive office, in consultation with the home care worker and 
138 consumer abuse stakeholder advisory committee established in subsection (b), MassHealth, the 
139 executive office of aging and independence, the department of public health and the PCA quality 
140 home care workforce council, shall adopt minimum standards and procedures for addressing 
141 abusive treatment of home care workers, personal care attendants and home care consumers in 
142 the commonwealth. The standards and procedures shall: (i) address abusive treatment, including 
143 the connection between abusive behavior by home care consumers and disruptive behavioral 
144 disorders; (ii) recommend appropriate training and support for home care workers, personal care 
145 attendants and home care consumers; (iii) ensure a safe and healthy work environment for home 
146 care workers and personal care attendants; (iv) recognize the privacy rights of home care 
147 consumers; and (v) recognize the responsibilities of the consumer-employers of personal care 
148 attendants.

150 committee within the executive office. The advisory committee shall advise the executive office 
151 on addressing abusive treatment of home care workers, personal care attendants and home care 
152 consumers and shall periodically conduct studies and issue reports containing its findings and 
153 recommendations.

168  (d) The advisory committee shall advise the executive office on addressing abusive 
169 treatment of home care workers, personal care attendants and home care consumers and shall 
170 periodically conduct studies and issue reports containing its findings and recommendations, 
171 which shall include, but shall not be limited to best practices and recommendations on: 
172  (i) reporting abusive treatment and post-incident debriefing of abusive treatment, 
173 including best practices for standards and procedures for reporting abuse to the home care 
174 workers’ employers or appropriate state entities, including, in the case of personal care 
175 attendants, the PCA quality home care workforce council or MassHealth, without retaliation and

178 affected home care workers and home care consumers following abusive treatment and violent

10 of 18 180  (ii) tracking and retaining records of abusive treatment, including best practices for 
181 standards and procedures for the executive office to track and monitor reports of both home care 
182 worker abuse and reports of home care consumer abuse or neglect; 
183  (iii) informing home care workers and personal care attendants of potentially unsafe 
184 working environments, including best practices for standards and procedures requiring 
185 employers of home care workers or other appropriate state entities, including, in the case of 
186 personal care attendants, the PCA quality home care workforce council or MassHealth, to 
187 regularly inform home care workers of patterns of abusive treatment by home care consumers 
188 that may indicate a potentially unsafe working environment; provided, however, that the 
189 standards and procedures shall balance respect for home care consumer privacy with the need to 
190 protect home care worker safety and ensure continuity of care; 
191  (iv) employee training, including best practices for standards and procedures for home 
192 care consumer orientations and home care worker trainings on recognizing, preventing and 
193 addressing abuse including: (A) abusive treatment in the home care setting; (B) escalation cycles 
194 and effective de-escalation techniques; and (C) culturally competent and peer-to-peer trainings 
195 and strategies to prevent physical harm with hands-on practice or role play; and 
196  (v) employer internal policies, including best practices for standards and procedures to: 
197 (A) prevent abusive treatment among employees; (B) offer resources to employees for coping 
198 with the effects of violence; and (C) develop labor-management workplace safety committees.
