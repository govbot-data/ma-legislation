To the Honorable Senate and House of Representatives,
I am filing for your consideration a bill entitled “An Act Making Appropriations for
Fiscal Year 2027 (FY27) to Provide for Supplementing Certain Existing Appropriations and for
Certain Other Activities and Projects.”
This supplemental budget would make an additional $100 million available in FY27 to
augment municipal school budgets and provide immediate, one-time relief for all school districts
across Massachusetts. I am filing this legislation now with the goal of making the funding
available before the start of the new school year in order to continue to provide high-quality
public education and to help avoid layoffs and program cuts, which can have negative impacts on
student learning and mental health.
The FY27 budget that I recently signed made substantial investments in K-12 education
and local aid, including full funding for the final-year implementation of the Student Opportunity
Act and support for the continuation of free school meals. Together, our administration and the
Legislature also increased the state’s financial support of special education, school transportation
reimbursement, rural school aid, charter school reimbursement, student mental health services
and English language learner programming.
However, we also know that municipal budgets continue to be severely strained. This
new funding - proposed to be taken from excess, unbudgeted Fair Share surtax revenue in FY27
– would provide a one-time bridge for cities and towns to mitigate harmful cuts in the new
school year while they prepare for a more sustainable future moving forward. Since its inception,
Fair Share has been a robust revenue source to support education and transportation across our
budget, and I am confident that we will have sufficient resources to cover this relief package.
1 of 2

This bill proposes to distribute the $100 million in additional Fair Share funding by
formula based on FY27 enrollment, ensuring that every one of Massachusetts’ 319 operating
school districts receives a portion of the additional funding. This amounts to approximately $112
per student on top of the $160 million in minimum per pupil aid included in the FY27 budget as
part of the final-year phase-in of the Student Opportunity Act.
School districts can use this flexible one-time funding to meet local needs, which may
include paying for teachers, paraprofessionals and mental health counselors, as well as evidence-
based early literacy reading curriculum, instructional materials and implementation of our
administration’s recommended high school graduation requirements.
Given the cost pressures on our schools, I believe this funding is immediately necessary
and will work in conjunction with FY27 budgeted aid and the reconvening of the Foundation
Budget Review Commission to evaluate how best the state can best support public education.
I look forward to partnering with the Legislature to deliver this urgently needed
additional school aid to districts promptly so that our partners in municipal government have
time to plan and prepare how best to meet their needs.
Respectfully submitted,
Maura T. Healey,
Governor
