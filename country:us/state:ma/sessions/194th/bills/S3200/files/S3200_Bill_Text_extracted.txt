49  Section 22. (a) As used in this section, the following words shall have the following 
50 meanings unless the context clearly requires otherwise: 
51  “Legislative record”, any of the following final records made, received, maintained or 
52 kept by the general court or any branch, committee, member, officer, caucus or employee 
53 thereof; provided, however, that “legislative record” shall not include records the disclosure of 
54 which would endanger the safety or security of persons or property, including, but not limited to, 
55 building blueprints, floor plans, security protocols and building access control information: 

4 of 12 56  (i) all: (A) legislation; (B) resolutions; (C) bill summaries; (D) fiscal notes; and (E) 
57 amendments filed with the clerks of the senate or house of representatives, including the names 
58 of the primary sponsors and a list of the names of all petitioners; 
59  (ii) published notices of committee hearings, the record of attendance of members at such 
60 hearings in accordance with joint rules of the senate and house of representatives and the 
61 recording and transcript of any hearing, when available;  
62  (iii) written testimony solicited and received by a committee, subject to committee rules 
63 regarding the disclosure of sensitive information or information that may jeopardize the health, 
64 wellness or safety of the testifier or others; 
65  (iv) all recorded votes of a committee, including roll calls or electronic polls of each 
66 member; 
67  (v) committee reports on bills including favorable and adverse reports and extension and 
68 study orders; 
69  (vi) the rules and joint rules of the senate and house of representatives, and any orders to 
70 adopt or amend such rules;  
71  (vii) daily legislative session calendars and orders of the day; 
72  (viii) the journals of the senate and house of representatives and all recorded votes, 
73 including roll calls taken in a legislative session;  
74  (ix) finalized senate and house of representatives leadership assignments, committee 
75 assignments, office assignments and legislator contact information;  

5 of 12 76  (x) published manuals or formal written policies of the senate or the house of 
77 representatives regarding employment, training and administrative operations prepared by the 
78 office of human resources of the senate or the house of representatives; 
79  (xi) communications to the clerks of the senate or house of representatives regarding the 
80 official appointment of a legislative appointee to, or resignation or removal from, a commission, 
81 board or task force;  
82  (xii) reports required by law to be submitted to the general court, any committee thereof 
83 or the clerks of the senate or house of representatives;  
84  (xiii) financial records provided to the office of the comptroller, including, but not limited 
85 to, financial records relating to salaries of members, officers, temporary employees and 
86 employees, operating expenditures and expenditures to vendors;  
87  (xiv) the final report of any audit conducted pursuant to the rules of the senate or house of 
88 representatives;  
89  (xv) written disclosures submitted by a member of the senate or house of representatives 
90 to the clerks of the senate or house of representatives required under chapter 268A; 
91  (xvi) names, public office addresses, job titles and salaries of members, officers and 
92 employees, as maintained by the office of human resources of the senate or house of 
93 representatives; 
94  (xvii) procurements and contracts for goods and services, unless such procurement or 
95 contract is specifically or by necessary implication exempted from disclosure by law or rule or 
96 the disclosure of which would unduly impair present or future contract awards;  

6 of 12 97  (xviii) a list of news outlets and legislative reporters approved to use state house rooms 
98 and facilities as members of the Massachusetts State House Press Association, Inc.; and 
99  (xix) electoral district maps approved by the general court and the associated geospatial 
100 files in industry standard formats supported by the geographic information system used to create 
101 the approved electoral district maps for the most recent redistricting cycle. 
102  (b)(1) The senate and the house of representatives shall each appoint a legislative records 
103 access officer for their respective branch to provide access to legislative records pursuant to this 
104 section. Each legislative records access officer shall coordinate the respective branch’s response 
105 to requests for access to legislative records and shall facilitate the resolution of such requests by 
106 the timely and thorough production of legislative records. Each legislative records access officer 
107 shall:  
108  (i) assist persons seeking legislative records to identify the records sought;  
109  (ii) assist the respective branch in preserving legislative records in accordance with this 
110 section and rules of the respective branch; 
111  (iii) prepare guidelines that enable a person seeking access to legislative records to make 
112 informed requests regarding the availability of such legislative records electronically or 
113 otherwise; provided, however, that such guidelines shall: (A) be updated periodically; (B) 
114 include the categorical list of legislative records, as specified in subsection (a); and (C) be posted 
115 on the official website of the general court; and 
116  (iv) prepare guidelines for the identification and processing of requests that are frivolous, 
117 designed to harass or seek records that may contain confidential information not suitable for 

7 of 12 118 dissemination; provided, however, that such guidelines shall describe the criteria by which such 
119 requests shall be identified, the procedures and timeframe by which they shall be reviewed and 
120 any steps taken to protect personal information prior to production; provided further, that such 
121 guidelines shall be updated periodically and shall be posted on the official website of the general 
122 court.  
123  (2) The clerks of the senate and house of representatives shall post in a conspicuous 
124 location at their offices and on the official website of the general court the name, title, business 
125 address, business telephone number and business email address of the legislative records access 
126 officer for the respective branch. The legislative records access officers shall provide legislative 
127 records to the public in accordance with this section. 
128  (c) The legislative records access officer shall provide the legislative records to a 
129 requestor by electronic means unless the record is not available in electronic form or the 
130 requestor does not have the ability to receive or access the records in a usable electronic form, in 
131 which case the legislative records access officer shall, to the extent feasible, provide the 
132 legislative record in the requestor’s preferred format or, in the absence of a preferred format, in a 
133 searchable, machine-readable format. A public record that is not also a legislative record shall 
134 not be provided by a legislative records access officer. The legislative records access officer shall 
135 not be required to create a new legislative record in order to comply with a request; provided, 
136 however, that furnishing a segregable portion of a legislative record shall not be deemed to be 
137 creation of a new record. If the legislative record requested is available on a public website, the 
138 legislative records access officer may furnish the legislative record by providing reasonable 
139 assistance in locating the requested record on the public website.  

8 of 12 140  (d)(1) A legislative records access officer appointed pursuant to subsection (b), or a 
141 designee, shall at reasonable times and without unreasonable delay permit inspection or furnish a 
142 copy of any legislative record, or any segregable portion of a legislative record, not later than 10 
143 business days following the receipt of the written request; provided, however, that the request 
144 shall reasonably describe the legislative record sought. A written request for legislative records 
145 may be delivered to the legislative records access officers by hand, via first class mail or 
146 electronic mail or by any additional means authorized by the legislative records access officer. 
147  (2) If a legislative records access officer does not intend to permit inspection or furnish a 
148 copy of a requested record, or if the magnitude or difficulty of the request, including requests 
149 subject to the guidelines prepared pursuant to clause (iv) of paragraph (1) of subsection (b), or of 
150 multiple requests from the same requestor, unduly burdens their responsibilities such that they 
151 are unable to permit inspection or furnish a copy of a requested record within the timeframe 
152 under paragraph (1), the legislative records access officer shall inform the requestor in writing 
153 not later than 10 business days after the initial receipt of the request for legislative records. The 
154 written response shall be made via first class or electronic mail and shall, as applicable: 
155  (i) confirm receipt of the request; 
156  (ii) identify any legislative records sought that are not within their possession, custody or 
157 control of their respective branch; 
158  (iii) identify any other legislative records access officer that may be in possession, 
159 custody or control of the legislative records sought, if known; 
160  (iv) identify any legislative records that the legislative records access officer intends to 
161 withhold pursuant to paragraph (1) of subsection (e) and provide the specific reasons for such 

9 of 12 162 withholding; provided, however, that nothing in the written response shall limit the legislative 
163 records access officer’s ability to redact or withhold written testimony in accordance with this 
164 section; 
165  (v) identify any legislative records that the legislative records access officer intends to 
166 produce and provide a detailed statement describing why the magnitude or difficulty of the 
167 request unduly burdens the responsibilities of the legislative records access officer and therefore 
168 requires additional time to produce the legislative records sought; 
169  (vi) identify a reasonable timeframe in which the legislative records access officer shall 
170 produce the legislative records sought; provided, however, that the timeframe shall not exceed 30 
171 business days following the initial receipt of the request for records; provided further, that the 
172 requestor may voluntarily agree to a response date beyond this timeframe; 
173  (vii) suggest a reasonable modification of the scope of the request or offer to assist the 
174 requestor to modify the scope of the request if doing so would enable the legislative records 
175 access officer to produce the legislative records sought more efficiently; and 
176  (viii) include a statement informing the requestor of the right to file a request for 
177 reconsideration pursuant to paragraph (2) of subsection (e). 
178  (e)(1) A legislative records access officer may withhold records, in whole or in part, if: (i) 
179 the record sought does not constitute a legislative record as defined in subsection (a); (ii) 
180 disclosure would be inconsistent with Article XXI of the Declaration of Rights of the 
181 Constitution of the Commonwealth; or (iii) denial of document production is otherwise 
182 consistent with the provisions of this section. A legislative records access officer may further 
183 withhold or redact any portion of a legislative record that falls within the exemptions established 

10 of 12 184 in clause Twenty-sixth of section 7 of chapter 4. A written denial shall be provided to the 
185 requestor and shall state with specificity the ground or grounds upon which the denial is based. 
186  (2) A requestor aggrieved by a denial of access to legislative records under this section 
187 may, within 30 days of receipt of the written denial, file a request for reconsideration with the 
188 legislative records access officer of the relevant branch. The request for reconsideration shall set 
189 forth, with specificity, the records sought, the grounds upon which the requestor disputes the 
190 denial and any reasons why the requestor believes the denial is inconsistent with this section. 
191  (3) Upon receipt of a request for reconsideration, the legislative records access officer 
192 shall, within 10 business days, provide a written final determination to the requestor, including a 
193 statement of the reasons for their determination. A copy of the final determination shall be 
194 transmitted to the committee on rules of the relevant branch. 
195  (4) The committee on rules of each branch may, on its own initiative, review a final 
196 determination of the legislative records access officer and, by majority vote of the committee, 
197 reverse or modify such determination; provided, however, that if the record pertains to a joint 
198 committee or a joint document of the general court, the joint committee on rules may review the 
199 matter jointly. The committee on rules of each branch and the joint committee on rules may, in 
200 connection with any such review, solicit or accept written submissions from the requestor and 
201 from the legislative records access officer. If the committee reverses or modifies a final 
202 determination, the legislative records access officer shall provide the legislative record or records 
203 in accordance with the decision by the committee. If the committee on rules fails to act within 30 
204 business days of receipt of a final determination, or if within 30 business days issues a decision 
205 confirming the final determination of the legislative records access officer, the determination of 

11 of 12 206 the legislative records access officer shall be deemed final for the purposes of initiating judicial 
207 review pursuant to paragraph (5).  
208  (5) A requestor aggrieved by a determination that has become final pursuant to paragraph 
209 (4) may obtain judicial review of such determination, but only as provided in this paragraph; 
210 provided, however, that any such action shall be brought only in Suffolk superior court where: 
211  (i) the aggrieved requestor shall file a petition for review in the nature of certiorari in 
212 Suffolk superior court within 60 days of written notice from the legislative records access officer 
213 that the determination has become final; 
214  (ii) the petition shall name the legislative records access officer who issued the 
215 determination as the sole defendant, in the officer’s official capacity, and the petition shall be 
216 served upon either the counsel to the senate or the counsel to the house of representatives, 
217 depending on which chamber employs the legislative records access officer; provided, however, 
218 that such service shall be only by electronic, registered or certified mail at the counsel’s business 
219 address; 
220  (iii) the requestor’s petition shall allege with specificity the alleged substantial errors of 
221 law contained in the final determination; 
222  (iv) the legislative records access officer shall, by way of answer, file an administrative 
223 record consisting of copies of the request, the initial response, the request for reconsideration, the 
224 final determination, any written submissions solicited or accepted by the committee on rules and 
225 any determination issued by the committee on rules;  

12 of 12 226  (v) review in the nature of certiorari shall be conducted by the court without a jury and 
227 shall be confined to the administrative record; provided, however, that no discovery shall be 
228 permitted; provided further, that the court may examine a withheld record in camera and any 
229 record so examined shall be impounded and shall not, by reason of such examination, be made 
230 available to the requestor or the public;  
231  (vi) the court shall have jurisdiction only to find that the final determination did, or did 
232 not, contain substantial errors of law; provided, however, that the court shall not have 
233 jurisdiction to issue injunctive or declaratory relief or other equitable relief, or relief in the nature 
234 of mandamus, nor award costs, fees or damages against the legislative records access officer, 
235 except pursuant to section 6F of chapter 231. 
236  (6) Notwithstanding clause Twenty-Sixth of section 7 of chapter 4 or any general or 
237 special law to the contrary, the procedure set forth in this section shall constitute the sole and 
238 exclusive remedy for obtaining access to records of the general court and no other provision of 
239 this chapter or any other law providing for public access to public records shall apply to the 
240 general court or a branch, committee, member, officer, caucus or employee thereof. 
241  SECTION 8. This act shall take effect on January 1, 2027 and apply to records made or 
242 received on or after January 6, 2027. Any record made or received before January 6, 2027 shall 
243 not be considered a legislative record under section 22 of chapter 66 of the General Laws; 
244 provided, however, that a legislative records access officer shall not be prohibited from assisting 
245 a requestor in accessing a record made or received before January 6, 2027 that is readily 
246 available and consistent with the records available under section 22 of chapter 66.
