81 SECTION 6. Said Chapter 40A of the General Laws is hereby amended by inserting after
82 section 3B the following three sections:-
83 Section 3C. Housing on land owned by a religious sect or denomination
84 (a) For the purposes of this section: (1) “religious sect or denomination” shall mean,
85 without limitation, any organization organized predominantly for religious purposes, whether
86 incorporated or unincorporated, including without limitation an organization recognized by the
87 Internal Revenue Service as exempt from taxation under section 501(c)(3) of the Internal
88 Revenue Code of 1986, as amended, based on its status as a religious or apostolic association or
89 corporation, or an organization recognized by the department of revenue as a religious
90 organization exempt from taxation pursuant to section 5 of chapter 59, as amended, or any other
91 organization that would qualify as a religious sect or denomination for purposes of section 3 of
92 chapter 40A, or any affiliated organization; and (2) “land owned by a religious sect or
93 denomination” shall mean land, buildings or structures owned by or held in trust for the use of a
94 religious sect or denomination for at least 3 years prior to the issuance of a building permit for
95 multifamily housing allowed under this section.
96 (b) No zoning ordinance or by-law shall prohibit, unreasonably restrict or require a
97 special permit or other discretionary zoning approval for the use of land owned by a religious
98 sect or denomination for multi-family housing; provided, that 20 per cent of housing units built
99 under this section shall be used as affordable housing, as defined in section 1 of chapter 60.
100 (c) Multi-family housing shall be allowed as of right on land owned by a religious sect or
101 denomination; provided, that the density permitted shall be constrained only by section 40 of
102 chapter 131, title 5 of the state environmental code established pursuant to section 13 of chapter
5 of 15

103 21A and the state sanitary code; provided further, that the maximum setback required shall not
104 be greater than 15 feet.
105 (d) All multi-family housing developed on land owned by a religious sect or
106 denomination under this section shall comply with state water resources regulations and
107 standards established by the department of environmental protection.
108 (e) No local occupancy preference shall be permitted in excess of 20 per cent of the
109 market rate multifamily housing units developed on land owned by a religious sect or
110 denomination under this section. Any local preference shall comply with all applicable federal
111 and state fair housing laws, and shall include current residents, employees of the municipality
112 and local businesses, including persons hired to work in the municipality, and households with
113 children attending public schools in the municipality or public schools within the school district
114 in which the municipality is located.
115 (f) Up to 2 housing units or 5 per cent of the total multi-family housing units developed
116 under this section, whichever is less, may be set aside for occupancy by employees of the
117 religious sect or denomination owning the land.
118 (g) Multi-family housing constructed pursuant to this section shall not be subject to any
119 municipal ordinances, bylaws or regulations, or other municipal development standards or
120 conditions of approval, that exceed applicable requirements of state law or regulation.
121 Section 3D. Local Option Property Tax Exemption
122 A city or town that accepts this section may grant an exemption of up to the full amount
123 of the taxable valuation of multi-family housing developed on land owned by a religious sect or
6 of 15

124 denomination under Section 3C. The city or town that accepts this section shall adopt an
125 ordinance or by-law specifying the method for negotiating and approving exemptions under this
126 section. This section shall take effect in any city or town only upon its acceptance by such city
127 or town.
128 Section 3E. Housing production plans; zoning alignment
129 (a) Each municipality in the commonwealth shall, every 5 years, establish and submit to
130 the executive office of housing and livable communities a housing production plan that sets forth
131 the municipality’s strategy to meet its affordable housing needs in a manner consistent with
132 chapter 40B and related regulations. Said housing production plan shall indicate whether current
133 zoning ordinances or bylaws in the municipality permit the construction or development of
134 affordable housing to meet the municipality’s needs under such plan; provided, that if current
135 zoning ordinances or bylaws do not permit such affordable housing, the municipality’s housing
136 production plan must include proposed zoning changes that would permit such construction or
137 development.
138 (b) The executive office shall approve or deny a municipality’s housing production plan
139 submitted under this section within 120 days of submission. A municipality must resubmit an
140 amended housing production plan within the time frame provided by the executive office when
141 the executive office denies a plan under this section.
142 (b) If the municipality’s zoning ordinances or bylaws do not permit the construction or
143 development of affordable housing to meet the municipality’s needs, within 2 years of receiving
144 an approved housing production plan under this section, each municipality shall adopt or update
145 zoning ordinances and bylaws in order to comply with the approved housing production plan.
