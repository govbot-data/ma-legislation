An Act to close the achievement gap by addressing disparities in children's vision. 
 
 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives in General Court assembled, and by the authority 
of the same, as follows: 
 
1  SECTION 1. Chapter 111 of the General Laws is hereby amended by adding after section 
2 248 the following 2 sections:-  
3 Section 249. There shall be a children’s vision and eye health advisory council within 
4 the department.  
5 The council shall be appointed by the commissioner, who shall serve as chair, and shall serve  
6 with 14 additional members, including the commissioner of the Department of Elementary and  
7 Secondary Education, or a designee; the Commissioner of Early Education and Care, or a  
8 designee; the Assistant Secretary for MassHealth, or a designee; 1 member of the Massachusetts  
9 School Nurse Organization; 1 member of the Massachusetts Society of Optometrists; 1 member  
10 of the Massachusetts Society of Eye Physicians and Surgeons; 1 member of the Massachusetts  
11 Chapter of the American Academy of Pediatrics; 1 member of the Massachusetts Academy of  
12 Family Physicians; 1 representative from the Massachusetts League of Community Health  
13 Centers; 1 member of the Massachusetts Federation for Children with Special Needs; 1 member  
14 of the Children’s Vision Massachusetts Coalition; 3 parents to be appointed by the  

2 of 4 15 commissioner, one of whom shall be a recipient of MassHealth and one of whom shall reside in a  
16 gateway city. The commissioner may appoint additional members that the commissioner deems  
17 appropriate.  
18 Council members shall receive no compensation for their services but shall be allowed actual and  
19 necessary expenses in the performance of their council duties.  
20 The council shall consult with and advise the department on matters related to the establishment,  
21 maintenance, operation and evaluation of children’s vision and eye health in the Commonwealth,  
22 including but not limited to the universal children’s vision surveillance registry, public health  
23 campaigns designed to encourage improved children’s eye health, and expansion of screening  
24 systems to other age groups. The council shall establish recommendations for improved  
25 children’s vision and eye health to facilitate short and long-term vision and eye health goals.  
26 The advisory council shall report findings and recommendations annually to the Joint Committee  
27 on Public Health and Joint Committee on Ways and Means.  
28 SECTION 2. Chapter 111 of the General Laws is further amended by adding after section 238  
29 the following section:-  
30 Section 249AA. The department shall establish, maintain, and operate a computerized registry of  
31 children’s vision screening and eye care. The registry shall record the results of vision and eye  
32 health screenings and follow-up eye care and shall include appropriate controls to protect the  
33 security of the system and the privacy of the information.  
34 The department shall promulgate rules and regulations to implement children’s vision registry.  
35 All licensed healthcare providers practicing who administer vision and eye health screenings and  
36 eye exams shall report to the registry such data related to screenings as the department  
37 determines is necessary to ensure adequate and equitable eye care.  

3 of 4 38 Information in the registry shall only be released from the registry to the following individuals  
39 and agencies without further express consent of the individual or the individual’s parent or  
40 guardian if the individual is a minor, unless the individual or the parent or guardian objects to  
41 such disclosure: (1) licensed health care providers providing direct care to the individual patient;  
42 (2) preschool, elementary and secondary school nurses; (3) staff of state agencies or state  
43 programs whose duties include education and outreach related to the improvement of children’s  
44 eye care amongst their clients.  
45 The department may designate appropriate users who shall have access only to the individually  
46 identifiable information for which access is authorized. Authorized users, including employees  
47 of the department, who in good faith disclose or refuse to disclose information to the  
48 immunization registry, shall not be liable in any cause of action arising from the disclosure or  
49 nondisclosure of such information. The department may revoke access privileges for just cause.  
50 Persons authorized by the commissioner may conduct research studies to further enhance  
51 understanding of children’s vision needs in the Commonwealth; provided, however, that the  
52 researcher shall submit a written request for information and shall execute a research agreement  
53 that protects the confidentiality of the information provided.  
54 Information contained in the children’s vision registry shall be confidential, shall not constitute a  
55 public record, and shall not otherwise be disclosed, except in accordance with this section. Such  
56 confidential information shall not be subject to subpoena or court order and shall not be  
57 admissible as evidence in any action of any kind before a court, tribunal, agency, board or  
58 person.  
59 The department shall establish procedures that allow for an individual, or if the individual is a  
60 minor, then the individual’s parent or guardian to amend incorrect information in the children’s  

4 of 4 61 vision registry and shall provide, upon request, a record of all individuals and agencies that have  
62 accessed an individual’s information.
