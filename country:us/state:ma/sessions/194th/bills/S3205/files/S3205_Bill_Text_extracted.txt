33  SECTION X. Section 4 of Chapter 151B of the General Laws is further amended by 
34 inserting the following subsection: - 

3 of 12 35  (1F) (a) For an employer, by itself or its agent, not to provide a reasonable 
36 accommodation to an employee or prospective employee who is a victim of abusive behavior, if 
37 the employee or prospective employee requests an accommodation related to the abusive 
38 behavior; provided, however, that an employer is not required to provide an accommodation if 
39 the employer can demonstrate that the accommodation would impose an undue hardship on the 
40 employer’s business.   
41  (b) For an employer, by itself or its agent, to (i) discharge, or in any other manner 
42 discriminate against, an employee for exercising the employee’s rights under this subsection, 
43 including requesting or using an accommodation under this subsection; (ii) refuse to hire, or in 
44 any other manner discriminate against, a prospective employee for exercising the prospective 
45 employee’s rights under this subsection, including requesting or using an accommodation under 
46 this subsection; (iii) require an employee or prospective employee who requests an 
47 accommodation under this subsection to accept an accommodation that the employee or 
48 prospective employee chooses not to accept if that accommodation is unnecessary to enable the 
49 employee to perform the essential functions of the job; (iv) require an employee to take leave if a 
50 reasonable accommodation may be provided that permits the employee to perform the essential 
51 functions of the job without undue hardship to the employer’s business; or (v) make 
52 preemployment inquiry of a prospective employee as to whether the prospective employee will 
53 require a reasonable accommodation under this subsection, unless the prospective employee first 
54 voluntarily discloses to the employer, or employer’s agent, a situation that would qualify the 
55 prospective employee for a reasonable accommodation under this section. 
56  (c) As used in this subsection, the following words shall have the following meanings 
57 unless the context clearly requires otherwise:  

4 of 12 58  “Accommodation” means a temporary or permanent adjustment to a job structure or 
59 schedule, workplace facility, or work requirement that enables an employee who is a victim of 
60 abusive behavior, or whose family member is a victim of abusive behavior, to be safe or to 
61 address issues directly related to the abusive behavior, including but not limited to (i) more 
62 frequent or longer paid or unpaid breaks; (ii) transfers, reassignments, or schedule changes; (iii) 
63 changing a work phone number, email address, or any other work contact, electronic or 
64 otherwise; (iv) installing or changing locks or work access mechanisms; (v) assisting with 
65 documentation of the abusive behavior; (vi) implementing safety procedures; or (vii) prohibiting 
66 the perpetrator of the abusive behavior from being on the workplace property or limiting the 
67 perpetrator’s access to the workplace property or portion thereof where the employee works; 
68 provided, however, that an employer shall not be required as part of providing a reasonable 
69 accommodation to discharge or transfer another employee or promote an employee who is not 
70 able to perform the essential functions of the job with or without a reasonable accommodation.  
71  “Undue hardship” shall mean an action requiring significant difficulty or expense; 
72 provided, however, that the employer shall have the burden of proving undue hardship; provided 
73 further, that in making a determination of undue hardship, the following factors shall be 
74 considered: (i) the nature and cost of the needed accommodation; (ii) the overall financial 
75 resources of the employer; (iii) the overall size of the business of the employer with respect to 
76 the number of employees and the number, type and location of its facilities; and (iv) any other 
77 impact of the reasonable accommodation on the employer’s business.  
78  (d)  Upon the employer’s receipt of a request from an employee or prospective employee 
79 for an accommodation under this subsection, the employee or prospective employee and the 

5 of 12 80 employer shall engage in a timely, good faith, and interactive process to determine an effective, 
81 reasonable accommodation. 
82  (e)  All employers will provide written notice to their employees of the right to be free 
83 from discrimination due to status as a victim of abusive behavior and of the right to a reasonable 
84 accommodation related to the abusive behavior.  Such notice may be provided in a handbook, 
85 pamphlet, or other means of notice regularly used by the employer.  In addition, all employers 
86 will provide such notice to (i) all new employees at the beginning of their employment and (ii) 
87 any employee or prospective employee who informs the employer that the employee, or  
88 prospective employee or family member of the employee or prospective employee, is a victim of 
89 abusive behavior not more than five days after the employer is so informed. 
90  (f)  Subject to appropriation, the commission shall develop courses of instruction and 
91 conduct public education efforts as necessary to inform employers, employees, and employment 
92 agencies, and, to the extent possible, the general population, about the rights and responsibilities 
93 established under this subsection not more than 180 days after the appropriation. Employers are 
94 encouraged to conduct an education and training program for new employees and members 
95 within one year of commencement of employment that includes information on the nature and 
96 extent of abusive behavior, issues of privacy and confidentiality, and the availability of 
97 reasonable accommodations. Employers are encouraged to conduct additional training for new 
98 supervisory and managerial employees within one ear of commencement of employment that 
99 includes information on the nature and extent of abusive behavior, issues of privacy and 
100 confidentiality, and the availability of reasonable accommodations.  

6 of 12 101  (g)  This subsection shall not be construed to preempt, limit, diminish, or otherwise affect 
102 any other law relating to leave or to abusive behavior, provided, however, that to the extent the 
103 reasonable accommodation requested pursuant to this section is a leave of absence, that leave 
104 need not exceed the amount of leave permitted under Section 52E of Chapter 149. 
105  (h) An employer may require any employee or prospective employee requesting an 
106 accommodation under this subsection to provide documentation evidencing that the employee, 
107 the prospective employee, or a family member of the employee or the prospective employee is a 
108 victim of abusive behavior; provided, however, that an employer may not require the employee 
109 or prospective employee to produce, but may consider, documentation concerning the reasonable 
110 accommodation itself, including its type, form, scope, or duration; provided further, that an 
111 employer shall not require the employee or prospective employee to show evidence of an arrest, 
112 conviction, or other law enforcement documentation for such abusive behavior.  The employee 
113 or prospective employee shall provide such documentation to the employer within a reasonable 
114 period after the employer requests such documentation, such reasonable period not to be shorter 
115 than 30 days.  The employer must provide a reasonable accommodation in a timely manner and 
116 may not delay providing reasonable accommodation pending receipt of the documentation; 
117 provided that if the employee or prospective employee requests that the accommodation begin 
118 before such documentation can be provided, the employee or prospective employee shall provide 
119 the documentation to the employer within 30 days of requesting the accommodation. The 
120 employer may periodically require the employee to provide documentation showing continued 
121 need for any temporary accommodation, but no more often than every six months or when the 
122 employer learns of a significant change in the situation that necessitated the accommodation. The 
123 provision of any documentation provided to an employer under this paragraph does not waive or 

7 of 12 124 diminish the confidential or privileged nature, if any, of communications between a victim of 
125 abusive behavior and any health care provider, clergy member, or other professional or counselor 
126 whose documentation is provided to the employer pursuant to this paragraph (h).   
127  The employer may maintain any documentation provided under this paragraph only in a 
128 separate, confidential file and only for as long as required for the employer to make a 
129 determination as to whether the employee is entitled to a reasonable accommodation under this 
130 subsection.   
131  The employee or prospective employee shall satisfy the documentation requirement by 
132 providing to the employer any one of the documents listed in Section 52E of Chapter 149, but an 
133 employer may not require the employee or prospective employee to provide a specific type of 
134 documentation from among the listed documents.  
135  (i) An employee or prospective employee requesting an accommodation under this 
136 subsection is not required to produce any documentation to, or discuss any information with, the 
137 employer that would in any way compromise the safety of the victim of the abusive behavior, 
138 and an employer is prohibited from requiring any such production or disclosure except to the 
139 extent such documentation or information is necessary to provide or effectuate the 
140 accommodation, to protect the safety of the employee or others employed at the workplace, or to 
141 enable the employer to satisfy legal obligations.  
142  (j) An employer shall maintain the confidentiality of all documentation and information 
143 provided by the employee or prospective employee related to the abusive behavior, including 
144 that the employee, prospective employee, or employee’s family member is a victim of abusive 
145 behavior or that the employee or prospective employee has requested or obtained a reasonable 

8 of 12 146 accommodation under this subsection, except under the following conditions: (i) the employee or 
147 prospective employee requests or consents in writing that such documentation or information be 
148 disclosed; (ii) the employer is ordered by a court of competent jurisdiction or administrative 
149 agency to disclose such documentation or information; (iii) disclosure is otherwise required by 
150 applicable federal or state law; (iv) disclosure is required in the course of an investigation 
151 authorized by law enforcement, including, but not limited to, an investigation by the attorney 
152 general; or (v) disclosure is necessary to protect the safety of the employee or others employed at 
153 the workplace or to provide or effectuate the accommodation. 
154  (k)  No employer shall (i) coerce, interfere with, restrain, or deny the exercise of, or any 
155 attempt to exercise, any rights provided under this subsection or (ii) make reasonable 
156 accommodations requested or taken hereunder contingent upon whether or not the employee, 
157 prospective employee, or victim of the abusive behavior  maintains contact with the alleged 
158 abuser.
