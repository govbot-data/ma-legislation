Introduction
The Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (Department) respectfully
submits this Report to the Legislature: Genocide Education Trust Fund: FY2023, pursuant
to Section 1(f) Chapter 98 of the Acts of 2021, which stipulates:
“There is hereby established and set up on the books of the commonwealth a separate
fund to be known as the Genocide Education Trust Fund for the purpose of educating
middle and high school students on the history of genocide…the commissioner of
elementary and secondary education shall report to the clerks of the house of
representatives and the senate, the joint committee on education and the house and
senate committees on ways and means on the fund’s activities.”
The Genocide Education Trust Fund is intended to support the mandate laid out in Chapter
98 which strengthens instruction on the history of genocide for middle and high school
students. More specifically, Chapter 98 requires that every school district, for the purpose
of educating middle and high school students, provide instruction on the history of
genocide consistent with the content standards articulated in the History/Social Science
Curriculum Framework.
In FY2023, the state legislature appropriated $1.5 million for the Genocide Education Trust
to strengthen instruction in the history and patterns of genocide. Per Chapter 98, the
Genocide Education Trust Fund may be used:
• to develop curricular materials detailing the underlying causes, international
reaction, progression and aftermath of genocide; and
• to provide professional development training, including the provision of trainings,
seminars, conferences and materials, for educators to use in the teaching of genocide.
The Trust Fund may also be used for other purposes related to instructing middle and high
school students on the history of genocide, as long as at least 50 percent of the funds
expended annually are allocated to public schools or school districts.
During FY2023, the Trust Fund was used to offer the inaugural Genocide Education Grant.
This grant provided 39 local education agencies with the funds to expand and deepen their
instruction in the history and patterns of genocide. Due to the timing of state
appropriations, we were unable to offer the grant until midway through the school year and
thus gave LEAs the option to divide their funds between FY2023 and FY2024 to provide
recipients with flexibility that would help them meet their goals. As such, the expenditures
noted in this report represent only a portion of the overall funds that were awarded.

Detailed Report on FY2023 Trust Fund Spending
FC215: Genocide Education Grant
Massachusetts local education agencies (LEAs) were eligible to apply for funding in FY2023,
with awardees having the option to divide their funds between FY2023 and FY2024. Those
recommended for funding most closely met the criteria outlined in the Funding
Opportunity RFP document. Thirty-seven LEAs or partnerships of LEAs were ultimately
funded through this grant opportunity, which benefitted a total of 39 LEAs. One proposal
was not funded due to not meeting the requirements of the RFP.
The grant was intended to support LEAs to strengthen instruction in the history and
patterns of genocide. Priority was given to LEAs with schools identified as requiring
assistance according to the state accountability system and/or where greater than 40
percent of the student population was designated as low-income; priority was also given to
proposals that came from LEAs that did not have a pre-existing genocide education
program. Grant applications were also prioritized based on:
1. Aligned, meaningful learning opportunities: Proposals that aligned materials,
curriculum, professional development, and/or experiential learning
opportunities to the History and Social Science Framework and the goals
outlined in Chapter 98 of the Acts of 2021, An act concerning genocide
education. Meaningful learning experiences that were well-integrated into
existing instruction were prioritized.
2. Equity: Proposals that increased equitable access to high-quality genocide
education for all students were preferred.
3. Sustainability: Proposals that led to sustainable, long-term impact were
preferred over “one-off” opportunities.
3

4. Partnership: Proposals were preferred that included opportunities to work in
partnership with relevant organizations and/or engage local community
members.
Ultimately, the funded projects included a variety of activities such as partnering with
external organizations to conduct professional development for teachers, supporting
teams of history teachers in incorporating high-quality instruction on genocide into their
existing curriculum, and taking field trips to relevant destinations. $1,193,400 in grants
were awarded in Fall 2022, with $399,321 of that total amount spent in FY2023. LEAs
utilized remaining funds in FY2024. A list of recipients can be found in Appendix A.
Looking Ahead: FY2024 Projects
Looking into FY2024, the Genocide Education Trust Fund will support a new landscape
review of genocide education across the state which will allow the Department to better
understand and respond to the needs of educators and students in developing supports
for genocide education. Additionally, we will continue to offer a significant amount of grant
funding directly to districts to support their strengthening of instruction in the history and
patterns of genocide.
Summary
Through its work in FY2023, the Department has leveraged the Genocide Education Trust
Fund to create new opportunities for teachers and students to learn vitally important
history that will support their development as ethical, informed young people with the
knowledge and frameworks to confront injustices and make moral choices.
