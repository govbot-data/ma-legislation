RE: Annual Data Equity Law Report  
 
Background  
The FY24 General Appropriations Act (GAA) included an outside section  requiring state 
government entities that already collect demographic data on race and ethnicity to do so 
at a standardized and more detailed level  (“Data Equity Law”).  The Data Equity Law , M.G.L. 
c. 6A s. 109,  require s the Executive Office for Administration & Finance (“A&F”) to establish 
regulations and guidelines on the collection of demographic data. Under subsection (f) (3) 
of section 109 of chapter 6A, A&F is required to submit an annual report on the progress of 
data collection under the Data Equity Law and provide a list of all waivers granted and the 
grounds for which the waiver was granted.  
On January 3, 2025, A&F issued regulations on the collection of demographic data ( 810 
CMR 8.00 ). The  Data Equity  Law and accompanying regulations  identifie d standardized 
demographic groups, mandated transparency and privacy, and established a waiver 
process. A&F, in partnership with the Executive Office of Technology Services & Security  
(“EOTSS”) , defined an implementation plan , completed a preliminary survey to identify 
programs subject to the Data Equity Law, and issued additional implementation guidance  
and a waiver application  to agencies in December  2025.  
This implementation  guidance provide d agencies with  more details o n requirements for 
multiple selections and custom entries, data privacy and confidentiality, and reporting and 
compliance. The guidance also provided standard disclosure language on voluntary 
participation , recommended interaction designs for digital experiences (supported by 
EOTSS Design System Team), and reporting format best practices and templates 
(supported by the EOTSS  Office of Data Innovation & Analytics).   
Program Implementation  
The implementation guidance set  September 30, 2026 as the date by which programs must 
be compliant with the Data Equity Law . Programs can achieve compliance through 
updat ing their programs to collect data in accordance with the Data Equity Law , integrating 
with the MyMassGov user profile,  or having an approved programmatic waiver in place.  

   
 
3 
 Through the implementation survey completed by executive branch agencies, we identified 
111 programs that are subject to the Data Equity Law. We anticipate 38 programs will be 
compliant by September 30, 2026  across 8 Secretariats .  
MyMassGov  
MyMassGov , the Commonwealth’s centralized online system for state services, was 
updated in June 2026 to meet the requirements of  the Data Equity Law. Constituents can 
now identify their race and ethnicity using the more granular options  outlined in the Data 
Equity Law and regulations. State programs that are integrated with the MyMassGov user 
profile are compliant with the Data Equity Law through this integration.  
Waiver Process  
A&F has completed its first round of waiver requests. Agencies could apply for waivers for 
programs th at could not comply by September 30, 202 6 due to  the following reasons:  
1) Contradictory obligations under another state or federal law  (“Contradictory Law ”) 
2) Information collected cannot be reported in a way that protects personally 
identifiable information (“PII”)  
3) Changes to demographic data collection require updates to technology system(s), 
and timeline for system updates does not align with implementation timeline of this 
law (“System Constraints”)  
4) The information cannot be collected without imposing a burden that significantly 
outweighs the benefits of collection  (“Excess Burden”)  
Agencies could select more than one reason for requiring a waiver. Agencies were required 
to provide justification (s) on need for each waiver reason selected and to provide a 
timeframe for ability to comply with the Data Equity Law, if feasible.  
Waiver Outcomes  
A&F granted 73 programmatic waivers across 8 Secretariats . Waivers were granted 
between 1 -5 years, with expiration dates on June 30 of that year.  See Appendix A for 
detailed information on approved waivers.  
The majority of  waivers were granted due to Contradictory Law or System Constraints. 
Waiver approval timeframes were generally shorter for those with system constraints as 
agencies often need additional time or  funding but will ultimately  comply  with the Data 
Equity Law . Waiver approval timeframes for those with contradictory laws were generally 
longer as the agency is required to collect and/or report data in a different format than the 
Data Equity Law.  These agencies will work over their identified time frames  to determine if 
alignment is feasible.   
Many EOHHS programs received waivers due to their current or planned alignment with the 
Quality Measure Alignment Taskforce (“QMAT”) requirements.  

   
 
4 
 Quality Measure Alignment Taskforce (“QMAT”)  
Since 2017, EOHHS, in collaboration with the Massachusetts Health Policy Commission 
(HPC) and the Center for Health Information Analysis (CHIA), has been conven ing the 
QMAT to build consensus on an aligned quality measure set for voluntary adoption by 
private and public payers and providers in global budget -based risk contracts. In 2022, 
EOHHS established a QMAT Health Equity Data Technical Advisory Group (HE Data T AG) to 
recommend aligned data standards for race, ethnicity, language, disability, sexual 
orientation, and gender identity. As part of the Advancing Health Equity in Massachusetts 
(AHEM) initiative, EOHHS expanded adoption of these standards across agencie s in 2024. 
The TAG was reconvened in 2025 to update the standards, which were approved and 
recommended by the Secretary of EOHHS in November 2025 for adoption across EOHHS 
and Massachusetts payers and providers by 2028.  
Beginning in January 2026, QMAT will serve as a Technical Advisory Group supporting the 
statutorily mandated Statewide Quality Advisory Committee (SQAC) (Chapter 343 Sec. 44, 
S.14(a)) in establishing data standards for an aligned quality measure set that w ill be 
mandatory for use in 2027 –2028 global budget -based contracts between health care 
payers and providers. The SQAC relies on the QMAT as a Technical Advisory Group to 
inform its aligned measure set; the race and ethnicity data standards are currently p art of 
the QMAT set.  
Next Steps  
A&F and EOTSS are providing ongoing  technical assistance and implementation support as 
agencies implement the Data Equity Law  by September 30, 2026.
