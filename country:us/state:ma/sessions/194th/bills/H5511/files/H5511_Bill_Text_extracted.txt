40 SECTION 3. Chapter 69 of the General Laws is hereby amended by inserting after
41 section 1E the following 2 sections:-
42 Section 1E½. (a) As used in this section and section 1E¾, the following words shall have
43 the following meanings unless the context clearly requires otherwise:
44 “District”, public education entities in the commonwealth including but not limited to
45 public school districts, school departments of a city or town, charter schools, commonwealth
46 virtual schools, regional school districts, independent vocational schools, vocational schools and
47 agricultural schools operated by a county.
48 “Evidence-based literacy instruction”, literacy instruction that: (i) is based on methods
49 backed by scientific research; and (ii) has a demonstrated record of success in increasing student
50 competency in reading ability, vocabulary, oral language and comprehension through well-
51 designed and well-implemented studies to produce measurable and positive effects on student
52 learning outcomes.
53 “Five research-based areas in reading instruction”, phonemic awareness, phonics,
54 fluency, vocabulary and comprehension; provided, however, that comprehension shall include
55 oral reading, oral communication skills and reading comprehension.

56 “High-quality curriculum”, a curriculum reviewed and approved by the department.
57 (b) A district shall select a high-quality curriculum to meet the curriculum frameworks
58 under section 1E for kindergarten through grade 3 literacy that: (i) includes instruction in the five
59 research-based areas in reading instruction and oral language development; and (ii) utilizes and
60 is based upon evidence-based literacy instruction. No curricula selected to meet a curriculum
61 framework pursuant to section 1E for kindergarten through grade 3 literacy shall include implicit
62 or incidental instruction in word reading, visual memorization of whole words or guessing from
63 context and picture cues, also known as MSV or three-cueing, as a substitute for identifying a
64 word.

66 use a kindergarten through grade 3 literacy instruction curriculum that does not appear on the
67 department’s list of high-quality curricula and that has not already been reviewed by the

69 based on evidence-based literacy instruction and meet quality standards determined by the
70 department.
71 (2) When applying for a waiver under this subsection, a district shall submit a report to
72 the department detailing: (i) the degree to which the curriculum’s instructional approach is

74 (iii) the duration of any such instruction using the curriculum; and (iv) any other considerations

76 determining whether to approve a district’s application for a waiver pursuant to this subsection,

174 Section 57B. (a) For the purposes of this section and section 57C, the term “screening
175 assessments” shall mean tools and assessments that can be used to identify students at risk for
176 poor academic outcomes as determined by the department.
177 (b) The commissioner, in consultation with the department of early education and care,
178 shall issue regulations requiring districts to adopt screening procedures and protocols for reading
179 ability and progress in literacy skills for all students in kindergarten through grade 3 using
180 screening assessments. The regulations shall include a threshold at which a student is determined
181 to be significantly below the grade level benchmark; provided, however, that said screening
10 of 14

182 procedures and protocols shall account for the needs of English language learners. The
183 commissioner shall annually make publicly available on the department’s website data on which
184 screening assessments are employed by each district to conduct reading screening.
185 Section 57C. (a) Each district shall assess each student’s reading ability and progress in
186 literacy skills from kindergarten through at least grade 3, using a screening assessment approved
187 by the department of elementary and secondary education, not less than twice per school year.
188 The department shall update approved screening assessments to remain current with best
189 practices and evidence-based assessments. The screening assessment of the student’s reading
190 ability and progress shall be consistent with section 2 of chapter 71B, the department’s dyslexia
191 guidelines issued pursuant to section 57A and literacy guidelines issued pursuant to section 57B.
192 (b) If a screening assessment determines that a student is significantly below relevant
193 grade level benchmarks in specific literacy skills, the district shall determine which actions
194 within the general education program will meet the student’s needs, including, but not limited to,
195 differentiated or supplementary evidence-based reading instruction and ongoing monitoring of
196 progress. Not later than 30 days after a screening result that determines that a student is
197 significantly below the relevant grade level benchmark, the district shall inform the student’s
198 parent or guardian of the screening results and the school’s response on what actions within the
199 general education program will meet the student’s needs and shall offer the student’s parent or
200 guardian the opportunity for a follow-up discussion.
