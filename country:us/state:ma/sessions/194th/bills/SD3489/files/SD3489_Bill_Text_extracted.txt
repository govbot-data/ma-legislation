Executive Summary  
 
Notes on Data  
 
This report is written in accordance with Section 34 of Chapter 22C of the Massachusetts General Laws. The 20 24 Hate Crime Report for Massachusetts 
was compiled using data voluntarily supplied by police depar tments across the Commonwealth.  The Executive Office of Public Safety & Security 
(EOPSS) received a total of 467 reports of incidents of hate crime ( down  from 557 in 20 23) from 82 municipal police departments,  13 campus police 
agencies  and the Massachusetts State  Police . In addition, 295 agencies  submitted “zero reports” (indicating that they had experienced no bias -motivate d 
incidents) and there were 37 non-reporting agencies .  In 20 24, reporting agencies and zero -reporting agencies covered approximately 98% of the 
population of Massachusetts.   After receipt and state data quality check s, EOPSS  submits all Massachusetts hate crime data to the FBI for further  
analysis, verification,  and inclusion in its  annual Hate Crime Statistics  publication.   EOPSS continues to verify and update hate crime reports after 
submission to the FBI, and as a result, the data in this report may not match the FBI’s published data.   
 
Data was collected utilizing the National Incident Based Reporting System (NIBRS) . 2020 was the last year that the FBI collected UCR  or Summary 
Reporting System (SRS)  data, fully transitioning to NIBRS for the 2021 data collection. Agencies  report Hate Crimes  by exporting data from their 
records management system  (RMS) in accordance with the FBI’s  NIBRS  program and  uploa ding their files to EOPSS , or by m anually entering pertinent 
data fields through EOPSS ’ Sidearm data portal .  EOPSS then extracts the required data for analysis and forwards it to the FBI .  It is important to note 
that several of the categories displayed in this report are only available in NIBRS, so comparisons to prior years may be misleading  or incomplete as 
certain data elements were  previously  not captured via UCR .  
 
Bias Motivation  
 
A “hate crime,” which can be referred to interchangeably as a “b ias crime ” or “bias -related / motivated crime ,” is not reported as a stand -alone offense , 
but rather as part of a separate criminal violation , ranging from vandalism to harassment to violent crimes.  Of the 481 hate crime  offenses reported in 
2024, the most frequen t offense s were Vandalism / Damage / Destruction of Property  (38%), which is often graffiti or the defacing of property  and 
Intimidation  (21%). Simple Assault  and Aggravated Assault incidents both had a  significant portion of offenses  accounting for  19.3% & 12.9% 
respectively.  In terms of violent crime, t here were 62 bias-motivated Aggravated Assaults reported in 20 24, which is up slightly from  the 59 reported 
in 20 23. In 202 4, as in 202 3, there were  0 bias-motivated homicides,  after reporting 2 in 2021 . Historically, Vandalism and Intimidation are the top 
two offenses reported during bias incidents  and in recent years have each accounted for  approximately 30% of offenses.  (See Table 1 .)    
 
In 2024, similar to years past,  prejudice  against race  / ethnicity or national origin was the most widely reported bias motivation  representing 42.7% of 
the total  (down  from 45.1% in 20 23).  Bias against religious groups surpassed  bias against sexual orientation with 34.3% (up from 24.6% in 20 23).   
Sexual orientation was third  with 16.3% of the total ( down from 19.7%  in 20 23). (See Figure 1 & Table 2 .)

 Victims and Offenders  
 
As in previous years, males were the most frequent victims ( 63.9% of known victims ).  This number has  been in the 50 -60% range for the past  several 
years  whereas prior numbers reached  70% of victims being reported as male  as recently as 2012 .  The known offenders of hate crimes continue to be 
predominantly male with 77.4% of offenders identified as males .  Females were victims in 36.1% of incidents where the sex of the victim was identified .  
Females a s a percentage of offenders  where sex was determined  accounted for  22.6% (down  from 24.8% in 20 23).   (For a breakdown of victim s and 
offender s by gender, please refer to Tables 14 and 16.)  When the race of the victim was determined by reporting  agencies, w hites were the most 
frequent racial category as victims 60.4% (up from  58.3% in 20 23).  White offenders accounted for 70.9% of known offenders down  from 73% in 
2023).  African American  victim s of both genders comprised  33.3% of the total victim population, up from 32.6% the previous year .  African American  
offenders comprised 25.6% of offenders, up from 24.2% the previous year.   (See Table 15.) 
 
Victims were reported to have some type of injur y in 13.7% of cases . This is consistent with last year’s 13.6% and  above  past years , where typical 
numbers hover ed around  7-8% of incidents resulting in some injury. Of the 64 total injuries, 14 resulted in a major injury of some type  which is the 
exact same number as in 2023 . (See Table 17.) 
 
Location  
 
Bias incidents reported by police  agencies  in Massachusetts occur in a variety of location s, both public and private.  In 20 24, 26.8% of inc idents 
occurred at a residence or home,  while a n additional 14.6% occurred  on a highway or street .  Combining all school categories resulted in  13.3%, which 
is exactly the same as 2023 . (See Table  11.)  Other location types with 10 or more  incidents were other / unknown; church / synagogue / temple / 
mosque; restaurant; parking / drop lot / garage ; Government / public building; commercial / office building; and park / playground . Religious locations 
were reported 30 times or in 6.2% of incidents . This is a decrease from the 35 reported in 2023 , however a significant  increase from  the 15 incidents 
reported in  2022.  
 
Targets  
 
Individuals  and private property  were  the primary  targets for hate crimes in the Commonwealth, with 77% of the reports, down  from 77.7% in 20 23.  
Business  and Government targets  were the second  most  frequent target s in 20 24 with 7.9% of the reports . (See Table 10.) 
 
 
Hate Crimes Historical Perspective  
 
Over the past 25 years, the numbers and the categories of bias motivations ha ve remained fairly consistent.  Between 2000 and 2002, ap proximately 
500 bias motivat ions were reported each year.  From 2003 through 201 8, the number declined to an average of about 3 60 incidents  per year and has 
remained extremely consistent over this extended period.  Over this time -period, the most frequently reported bias motivations have remained similar: 
Anti-Black bias has consistently been most frequent, followed by Anti-Gay, Anti-Semitic and Anti-White . The 557 incidents reported in 20 23 were

 the highest reported since 2002  and was a 26.5% increase over 2022. 2024 saw a significant decrease of 16% from  2023. The most notable change 
however, was the Anti -Jewish incidents surpassed anti -Black incidents for the first time  as the most frequent bias type.   
 
 
 
Definitions  
 
Hate Crime    Any criminal act to which a bias motive is evident as a contributing factor.  The MA Hate Crime Reporting Act  of 1991  
covers bias on account of race, religion, ethnicity, handicap, gender, or sexual orientation.  Hate crimes can be reported 
only by law enforcement agencies.  See M.G.L . c. 22C, § 32 for more details.  
 
Zero Report    An official report by a police department stating no hate crimes were reported to the department during the reporting 
period.  
 
Non-reporting agencies  Local police departments , and other law enforcement agencies , which are not in compliance with the provisions of the 
Reporting Act.  These agencies failed to indicate how many, if any, bias crimes occurred in their jurisdiction.  
 
Reporting agencies  Local police departments , as well as other law enforcement agencies , who have voluntarily reported the incidence of 
identified bias crimes in their jurisdiction.  Agencies can comply with the Reporting Act by reporting the number of bias 
crimes, even if zero.   
 
Reports by Law Enforcement Agencies  
 
In the statistics that follow, it should be noted that a single incident report may include multiple offenses, bias motivatio ns, victims or offenders.  As a 
result, totals in some categories may exceed the number of reports.  Similarly, some reports omit i nformation in certain categories, resulting in totals 
lower than the number of incident reports collected.  
 
Hate crimes were reported by a total of 97 different law enforcement agencies .  A total of 467 hate crime reports were filed by th ese agencies, which 
included 481 separate offenses  and 510 reported biases .  Boston reported 149 hate crimes in 20 24, which is  down  from 192 in 20 23.  Other agencies 
that reported 10 or more hate crimes were: Newton (43), Cambridge  (34), Natick  (12), Danvers  (10) and Salem (10) .  (See Figure 7  and Table 1 9.)

Categories of Bias Reported  
 
 
Of the 510 bias motivations for particular incidents  reported, race  / ethnicity  / national origin bias constituted the largest category of reported motives, 
with 42.7% of the total  (down  from 45.1% in 20 23).  Offenses related to religious affiliation  were the second  most prevalent, with 34.3% (up from

Categories of Crimes Reported  
 
Of the  481 total offenses  reported, Vandalism / Destruction of Property  was the most frequently reported with 183 offenses, representing 38% of the 
total.  Intimidation  was the second most frequent ly reported  offense, with 101 incidents representing 21% of the total offenses .  Assaults also formed 
a significant portion of incidents , accounting for 19.3% and 12.9% respectively.  Combining Aggravated and Simple Assaults would yield 155 assaults 
where offenders physical ly confronted a victim , which is a decrease  compared to  last year’s total of 161.

 Victim and Offender Profile  
 
An Individual / Person (including private property) was the target in 77% of cases ( down  from 77.7% in 20 23).  Business  and Government targets were  
second, with 7.9%. Religious  targets were chosen  in 22 incidents or 3.9% of incidents (up from 2.7% the previous year) .  
 
In the 366 NIBRS incidents where the victim’s race was reported, white s were victims  60.4% of the time  (up from 58.3% in 20 23), while  African 
Americans  were victims in 33.3% of cases (down  from 32.6% in 20 23). The victims were male in 63.9% of cases. This year, the most common age 
group s reporting hate crimes w ere 41-45 (11.4%) and  21-25-year-olds (10.7%).  (See Table 8 & 13.) In 2023, the most frequent age groups reporting 
were 21 -25 with 14.24% and 31 -35 with 14%.  
 
The perpetrator s of hate crimes w ere white in 70.9% of cases ( down  from 73% in 20 23) and African American  in 25.6% of cases ( up from 24.2% in 
2023), in the 254 cases where this information was identified .  The perpetrator was male in 77.4% of known cases ( up from 75.2% in 20 23), and o f the 
cases where the offender (s)’s age group was identified , the most frequently reported age group was 31-35 with 14.7 . In 2023, the most frequent age 
group was 11-15 with 15.2%.

 Characteristics of the Incident s 
 
 
The most common location for  hate crime incidents  to occur in 20 24 was a residence / home  (26.8%, up from 24.6% in 20 23); followed by highway / 
road / street / sidewalk  (14.6%, down from 18.3% in 2023 ) and School – College / University  (7.3%) which is down by two (2) incidents in 2023 (37 
incidents in 2023 and 35 in 2024) .    (See Table 11.) 
 
Weapons of some type were reported in 142 incidents , which is up from 141 in 20 23.  In cases where  the type of  weapon was identified , hands  / fist / 
feet were used  the most  frequently  (47.2%, down  from 48.2% in 20 23).  Other weapons were the second most frequent ly listed in 25.4% of cases . Some 
type of firearm was  utilized in 4 incidents, up from 2 in 20 23 and significantly down from 2021 where  a firearm was used in 11 incident s. (See Table 
12.) 
 
In 20 24, there were 64 incidents where injuries were reported. Minor injuries were reported in 50 incidents . This year, there were some other major 
injuries. There were 4 Broken Bones, 2  lacerations , 2 internal injuries, 3 other major injuries , 2 victims lost consciousness  and 1 incident where a 
victim lost teeth .   (See Table 17 .)
