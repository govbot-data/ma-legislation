1  SECTION 1. Chapter 6A of the General Laws is hereby amended by inserting after 
2 section 16GG the following 4 sections:- 
3  Section 16HH. For the purposes of this section and sections 16II to 16KK, inclusive, the 
4 following words shall have the following meanings unless the context clearly requires otherwise: 
5  “Abusive treatment”, physical, verbal or mental abuse, including, but not limited to, 
6 bullying, directed at or committed by individual home care consumers, surrogates, family 
7 members, home care workers, other caregivers and any other individuals present in the home 
8 care consumers’ household when the home care worker is providing home care services. 
9  “Executive office”, the executive office of health and human services. 
10  “Home care agency”, a business, including a sole proprietorship, nonprofit organization 
11 or other entity, that: (i) provides home care services to consumers in the consumers’ residences 
12 for compensation; (ii) represents itself as a home care agency by name, advertising or 
13 communications to the public or uses the terms “home care agency” or “home care” in the 
14 entity’s name; or (iii) procures, offers, refers or provides or attempts to provide home care 

2 of 15 15 services by matching individuals seeking home care services to entities that provide home care 
16 services; provided, however, that “home care agency” shall not include: (A) an entity operated 
17 by the federal government or the commonwealth that provides home care services; (B) an entity 
18 that limits its business to the provision of house cleaning services; (C) an entity designated as an 
19 aging services access point pursuant to section 4B of chapter 19A; (D) a hospice program 
20 licensed under section 57D of chapter 111; or (E) a home health agency as defined in section 
21 51K of said chapter 111. 
22  “Home care consumer”, an individual receiving home care services or any legal 
23 representative of such individual. 
24  “Home care services”, supportive services provided to an individual in their residence to 
25 enable that individual to remain in their residence safely and comfortably, including, but not 
26 limited to, assistance with eating, toileting, dressing, bathing, transferring, ambulation and other 
27 activities of daily living, housekeeping, personal laundry and companionship; provided, 
28 however, that “home care services” shall not include: (i) hospice services provided by an entity 
29 subject to licensure under section 57D of chapter 111; (ii) home health services provided by an 
30 entity subject to licensure under section 51K of said chapter 111; (iii) services provided by a 
31 personal care attendant in the commonwealth’s personal care attendant program as defined in 
32 130 CMR 422.000; or (iv) services provided under the MassHealth adult foster care program as 
33 defined in 130 CMR 408.402. 
34  “Home care worker”, an individual employed by a home care agency who provides home 
35 care services to a consumer in the consumer’s residence. 

3 of 15 36  “PCA quality home care workforce council”, the council established in section 71 of 
37 chapter 118E.  
38  “Personal care attendant”, as defined in section 70 of chapter 118E. 
39  “Surrogate”, as defined in said section 70 of chapter 118E. 
40  Section 16II. (a) No person or entity shall: (i) establish, maintain, operate or hold itself 
41 out as a home care agency; (ii) provide home care services; (iii) or use the words “home care” to 
42 describe its services without a home care agency license issued pursuant to subsection (b). 
43 Licensure pursuant to said subsection (b) shall be required for home care agencies that directly 
44 employ home care workers and home care agencies that contract with entities that employ home 
45 care workers. 
46  (b)(1) The executive office shall issue for a term of 3 years, and may renew for like 
47 terms, a home care agency license to any person or entity that the executive office deems 
48 responsible and suitable to establish or maintain a home care agency and meets the requirements 
49 of the rules and regulations promulgated pursuant to subsection (e). A license issued under this 
50 subsection shall not be transferable or assignable. 
51  (2) The executive office may suspend, revoke or refuse to renew for cause a home care 
52 agency license in accordance with regulations promulgated by the department pursuant to 
53 subsection (e); provided, however, that the office shall provide home care agencies an 
54 opportunity for appeal of such suspension, revocation or refusal or renewal. 
55  (3) License application and renewal fees shall be established pursuant to section 3B of 
56 chapter 7. 

4 of 15 57  (c) If the executive office determines a licensee has failed or refused to comply with 
58 requirements established under this section or the regulations promulgated thereunder, the 
59 executive office may: (i) deny an application for licensure; (ii) modify, suspend or revoke a 
60 license; or (iii) issue a fine of not more than $500 for each day of such failure or refusal to 
61 comply.  
62  (d) The executive office, in collaboration with the executive office of aging and 
63 independence and the department of public health, may conduct surveys and investigations to 
64 monitor and enforce compliance with this section. 
65  (e)(1) The executive office shall, in consultation with the executive office of aging and 
66 independence, the department of public health and the home care oversight advisory council 
67 established in section 16KK, issue rules and promulgate regulations for the licensing and conduct 
68 of a home care agency, which shall include, but not be limited to:   
69  (i) a requirement for background checks for home care workers, which shall include, but 
70 not be limited to: (A) state and national fingerprint-based criminal background checks; (B) state 
71 or county criminal history checks for each location outside of the commonwealth in which the 
72 home care worker is known to have lived or worked during the previous 5 years; (C) a review of 
73 the United States Office of Inspector General List of Excluded Individuals/Entities; (D) a review 
74 of the nurse aide registry pursuant to section 72J of chapter 111; (E) if applicable, a review of 
75 professional certifications or other credentials; and (F) for home care workers who transport 
76 home care consumers, verification of motor vehicle licensure, auto insurance and driving records 
77 for not less than the previous 5 years; 

5 of 15 78  (ii) minimum standards for consumer-specific service plans and contracts that home care 
79 agencies shall develop with home care consumers, which shall include: (A) a detailed description 
80 of services, including services the home care agency is subcontracting to another agency or 
81 provider; (B) a written unit rate and total cost of services, inclusive of any additional fees or 
82 deposits; and (C) home care agency contact information for consumer questions; 
83  (iii) minimum coverage requirements for workers’ compensation insurance and liability 
84 insurance for the home care agency; 
85  (iv) maintenance of a payroll process for home care agencies; 
86  (v) annual and ongoing training and competency requirements for home care workers and 
87 home care agency staff that interact with home care consumers; provided, however, that 
88 minimum training and competency requirements shall include: (A) confidentiality and privacy 
89 rights of home care consumers; (B) navigating infection control and communicable diseases; (C) 
90 handling of emergencies, including safety and falls prevention; (D) observing, reporting and 
91 documenting changes in home care consumer needs and environment; (E) identifying and 
92 reporting suspected abuse, neglect or misappropriation of property; (F) understanding 
93 Alzheimer’s disease and dementia, including person-centered care, activities of daily living, 
94 safety and dementia-related behaviors and communication; and (G) employee rights and 
95 remedies under the commonwealth’s wage and hour laws; and provided further, that the 
96 regulations shall require that trainings shall be culturally and linguistically competent for the 
97 trainee; 
98  (vi) policies and procedures to ensure home care workers have safe working conditions, 
99 adequate safety training and a process for submitting complaints including, but not limited to, 

6 of 15 100 safe evacuation protocols and emergency backup care for such evacuations to avoid disciplinary 
101 actions; 
102  (vii) a requirement for the development and maintenance of an emergency preparedness 
103 plan; 
104  (viii) quality metrics and standards for services provided to home care consumers, 
105 including reporting mechanisms for tracking performance of home care agencies; 
106  (ix) annual reporting requirements for home care agencies on quality of care and any 
107 significant changes related to home care agency finances; 
108  (x) requirements and written processes for the timely reporting of home care consumer 
109 complaints; and  
110  (xi) requirements for home care agencies to provide adequate equipment and supplies for 
111 home care workers. 
112  (2) The executive office shall avoid unnecessary duplication of other long-term care 
113 services and supports, including home health agency licensure and aging services access points 
114 monitoring, for home care agency licensure, reporting and oversight requirements.  
115  (f) The executive office shall: 
116  (i) review and process licensure applications and perform a suitability review to ensure 
117 that the applicant meets the requirements and conditions of licensure, including, but not limited 
118 to: 

7 of 15 119  (A) a review of the state debarment list maintained by the operational services division 
120 and federal Office of Inspector General List of Excluded Individuals/Entities;  
121  (B) a competency review, which shall require all individuals with not less than 5 per cent 
122 ownership stake in the home care agency to submit the individual’s name, contact information, 
123 businesses in which they have not less than a 5 per cent ownership interest, any civil or criminal 
124 findings against said individual and a completed background check; and 
125  (C) a review to ensure that applicants have the financial capacity to provide ongoing 
126 home care services while maintaining compliance with labor and employment laws; 
127  (ii) establish a process for a change of ownership of a home care agency that includes 
128 advanced notice to home care consumers and home care workers;  
129  (iii) work with other applicable state agencies to investigate and resolve complaints 
130 against a licensee under this section; and 
131  (iv) publicly post a list of licensed home care agencies on the executive office’s website. 
132  Section 16JJ. (a) The executive office, in consultation with the home care worker and 
133 consumer abuse stakeholder advisory committee established in subsection (b), MassHealth, the 
134 executive office of aging and independence, the department of public health and the PCA quality 
135 home care workforce council, shall adopt minimum standards and procedures for addressing 
136 abusive treatment of home care workers, personal care attendants and home care consumers. The 
137 standards and procedures shall: (i) address abusive treatment, including the connection between 
138 abusive behavior by home care consumers and disruptive behavioral disorders; (ii) recommend 
139 appropriate training and support for home care workers, personal care attendants and home care 

8 of 15 140 consumers; (iii) ensure a safe and healthy work environment for home care workers and personal 
141 care attendants; (iv) recognize the privacy rights of home care consumers; and (v) recognize the 
142 responsibilities of the consumer-employers of personal care attendants.  
143  (b) There shall be a home care worker and consumer abuse stakeholder advisory 
144 committee within the executive office, which shall consist of: the secretary of aging and 
145 independence or a designee, who shall serve as chair; the assistant secretary for MassHealth or a 
146 designee; the commissioner of public health or a designee; the chairs and ranking minority 
147 members of the joint committee on aging and independence or their designees; and 10 persons to 
148 be appointed by the governor, 1 of whom shall be a personal care attendant, 1 of whom shall be a 
149 home care worker, 1 of whom shall be a consumer of personal care attendant services who shall 
150 be a member of the PCA quality home care workforce council, 1 of whom shall be a home care 
151 consumer, 1 of whom shall be a representative of the statewide independent living council, 1 of 
152 whom shall be a representative of the Disability Law Center, Inc., 1 of whom shall be a 
153 representative of Massachusetts Aging Access Association, Inc., 1 of whom shall be a 
154 representative of the Home Care Aide Council, 1 of whom shall be a representative of the Home 
155 Care Alliance of Massachusetts, Inc. and 1 of whom shall be a representative of the Local 1199 
156 Service Employees International Union. The members of the advisory committee shall serve 
157 without compensation. 
158  (d) The advisory committee shall advise the executive office on addressing abusive 
159 treatment of home care workers, personal care attendants and home care consumers and shall 
160 periodically conduct studies and issue reports containing its findings and recommendations, 
161 which shall include, but shall not be limited to best practices and recommendations on: (i) 
162 reporting abusive treatment and post-incident debriefing of abusive treatment, including best 

9 of 15 163 practices for standards and procedures for reporting abuse to the home care workers’ employers 
164 or appropriate state entities, including, in the case of personal care attendants, the PCA quality 
165 home care workforce council or MassHealth, without retaliation and while retaining the right to 
166 report any criminal activity to law enforcement; provided, however, that such standards shall 
167 include, but not be limited to, best practices for a post-incident debriefing process for affected 
168 home care workers and home care consumers following abusive treatment; 
169  (ii) tracking and retaining records of abusive treatment, including best practices for 
170 standards and procedures for the executive office to track and monitor reports of home care 
171 worker abuse and reports of home care consumer abuse or neglect; 
172  (iii) informing home care workers and personal care attendants of potentially unsafe 
173 working environments, including best practices for standards and procedures requiring 
174 employers of home care workers or other relevant state entities to regularly inform home care 
175 workers and personal care attendants of patterns and signs of abusive treatment by home care 
176 consumers that may indicate a potentially unsafe working environment; provided, however, that 
177 the standards and procedures shall balance respect for consumer privacy with the need to ensure 
178 home care worker and personal care attendant safety and ensure continuity of care; 
179  (iv) employee training, including best practices for standards and procedures for home 
180 care consumer orientations and home care worker trainings on recognizing, preventing and 
181 addressing abuse including: (A) abusive treatment in the home care setting; (B) escalation cycles 
182 and effective de-escalation techniques including, but not limited to, techniques to assist with 
183 home care consumers with neurocognitive disorders; and (C) culturally competent and peer-to-
184 peer trainings and strategies to prevent physical harm with hands-on practice or role play; and 

10 of 15 185  (v) employer internal policies, including best practices for standards and procedures to: 
186 (A) prevent abusive treatment among employees; (B) offer resources to employees for coping 
187 with the effects of violence; and (C) develop labor-management workplace safety committees.  
188  (e) The advisory committee shall submit such reports containing findings and 
189 recommendations, including drafts of proposed legislation or regulations, if any, to carry out its 
190 recommendations, with the secretary of health and human services, the clerks of the senate and 
191 house of representatives, the senate and house committees on ways and means, the joint 
192 committee on aging and independence and the joint committee on public health. 
193  Section 16KK. (a) There shall be a home care oversight advisory council within the 
194 executive office, which shall consist of: the commissioner of public health or a designee, who 
195 shall serve as chair; the secretary of aging and independence or a designee; the assistant secretary 
196 for MassHealth or a designee; the chairs and ranking minority members of the joint committee 
197 on aging and independence or their designees; and 4 persons to be appointed by the governor, 1 
198 of whom shall be a representative of the Home Care Aide Council, 1 of whom shall be a 
199 representative of the Home Care Alliance of Massachusetts, Inc., 1 of whom shall be a 
200 representative of Massachusetts Aging Access Association, Inc. and 1 of whom shall be a 
201 representative of Local 1199 Service Employees International Union. 
202  (b) The advisory council shall advise the executive office on the oversight of licensed 
203 home care agencies and shall assist in the development of regulations promulgated by the 
204 executive office pursuant to subsection (e) of section 16II. The advisory council shall advise the 
205 executive office on:  

11 of 15 206  (i) the process of implementing home care agency licensure requirements pursuant to 
207 section 16II;  
208  (ii) existing licensure, reporting and oversight requirements of long-term care services 
209 and support systems and other relevant state agencies, including the provider monitoring 
210 conducted by the aging services access points, to avoid duplication and conflicting requirements; 
211  (iii) developments in other states relative to the licensure of home care agencies; 
212  (iv) processes to ensure that the home care agency licensure process, where applicable, 
213 aligns and avoids duplication with licensure for home health agencies and with state oversight 
214 processes already in place through the aging services access points, the home care worker 
215 registry established in section 4D of chapter 19A and the nurse aide registry established in 
216 section 72J of chapter 111; 
217  (v) the development of a process for the commonwealth to conduct a statewide home care 
218 market analysis and issue a report or otherwise publish information on current available home 
219 care services in all regions; and 
220  (vi) any additional subjects that the advisory council and the executive office deem 
221 necessary and appropriate.

12 of 15 227  SECTION 3. (a) There shall be a special commission to study and report on the creation 
228 of a statewide long-term services and supports benefit program, which shall research, analyze 
229 and report on issues including, but not limited to: (i) incorporating a long-term services and 
230 supports benefit program into existing state benefit plans; (ii) criteria for participation, including, 
231 but not limited to, working adults; (iii) mandatory enrollment; (iv) providing individuals with 
232 functional or cognitive limitations with nonmedical services and supports to help individuals 
233 remain in their communities; (v) offsetting the cost of chronic and disabling conditions; (vi) 
234 expanding the options for individuals in need of long-term services and supports, including, but 
235 not limited to, insurance options for the costs associated with functional or cognitive disability; 
236 (vii) aligning with the MassHealth programs and other publicly funded resources, including, but 
237 not limited to, the home care program pursuant to section 4 of chapter 19A; and (viii) the 
238 demands on the long-term care workforce and how such workforce can be prepared to meet 
239 future needs.

14 of 15 273  SECTION 4. The executive office of health and human services shall promulgate 
274 regulations for the temporary licensure of home care agencies and shall issue temporary licenses 
275 for home care agencies within 180 days of the effective date of this act; provided, however, that 
276 the executive office of health and human services shall issue a temporary license to an applicant 
277 upon verification that such applicant is currently under contract with an aging services access 
278 point or MassHealth. Such temporary licenses shall be valid until the date established by the 
279 executive office by which entities providing home care services are required to be licensed as 
280 home care agencies. 
281  SECTION 5. The executive office of health and human services shall, in consultation 
282 with the executive office of aging and independence, the department of public health and the 
283 home care oversight advisory council established in section 16KK of chapter 6A of the General 
284 Laws, adopt rules and promulgate regulations pursuant to subsection (e) of section 16II of said 
285 chapter 6A for the licensing and conduct of home care agencies within 1 year of the effective 
286 date of this act. 
287  SECTION 6. The executive office of health and human services shall establish a date not 
288 later than 1 year after adopting rules and promulgating regulations pursuant to this act by which 
289 entities providing home care services shall be licensed as home care agencies. 
290  SECTION 7. The home care worker and consumer abuse stakeholder advisory committee 
291 established in subsection (b) of section 16JJ of chapter 6A of the General Laws shall meet not
