An Act relative to access to air conditioning and relief from oppressive heat. 
 
 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives in General Court assembled, and by the authority 
of the same, as follows: 
 
1  SECTION 1. Chapter 186 of the General Laws, as appearing in the 2022 Official Edition, 
2 is hereby amended by inserting after Section 14 the following section:-    
3  Section 14A. (a) For the purposes of this section, the following words shall have the 
4 following meanings: 
5  “Portable cooling device” includes air conditioners and evaporative coolers, including 
6 devices mounted in a window or that are designed to sit on the floor but not including devices 
7 whose installation or use requires alteration to the dwelling unit.   
8  (b)  A landlord may not prohibit or restrict a tenant from installing or using a portable 
9 cooling device of the tenant’s choosing, unless:   

2 of 5 10  (1) The installation or use of the device would:   
11  (i) Violate building codes or state or federal law;   
12  (ii) Violate the device manufacturer’s written safety guidelines for the device;   
13  (iii) Damage the premises or render the premises uninhabitable; or   
14  (iv) Require amperage to power the device that cannot be accommodated by the power 
15 service to the building, dwelling unit or circuit;   
16  (2) If the device would be installed in a window and:   
17  (i) The window is a necessary egress from the dwelling unit;   
18  (ii) The device would interfere with the tenant’s ability to lock a window that is 
19 accessible from outside;   
20  (3) The device requires the use of brackets or other hardware that would damage or void 
21 the warranty of the window or frame, puncture the envelope of the building or otherwise cause 
22 significant damages;   
23  (4) The restrictions require that the device be adequately drained to prevent damage to the 
24 dwelling unit or building; or   
25  (5) The restrictions require that the device be installed in a manner that prevents risk of 
26 falling; or   
27  (6) The restrictions require that the device be:   
28  (i) Installed or removed by the landlord or landlord’s agent;   

3 of 5 29  (ii) Subject to inspection or servicing by the landlord or landlord’s agent; or   
30  (iii) Removed from October 1 through April 30;  
31  (7) The landlord provides cooling to the dwelling unit through a central air conditioning 
32 system.   
33  (c) A landlord may not enforce a restriction on portable cooling devices against a tenant 
34 allowed under subsection (b) of this section unless the restrictions are in writing and delivered to 
35 the tenant.   
36  (d) A landlord is immune from liability for any claim for damages, injury or death caused 
37 by a portable cooling device installed by the tenant.   
38  (e) A landlord who must limit portable cooling devices for a building under subsection 
39 (b)(1)(iv) of this section shall prioritize allowing the use of devices for individuals who require a 
40 device to accommodate a disability. A landlord is not responsible for any interruption in 
41 electrical service that is not caused by the landlord, including interruptions caused by an 
42 electrical supply’s inability to accommodate use of a portable cooling device.   
43  SECTION 2. Chapter 184 of the General Laws, as appearing in the 2022 Official Edition, 
44 is hereby amended by inserting after Section 23D the following section:-   
45  Section 23E. (a) For the purposes of this section, the following words shall have the 
46 following meanings:-   
47  “Portable cooling device” includes air conditioners and evaporative coolers, including 
48 devices mounted in a window or that are designed to sit on the floor but not including devices 
49 whose installation or use requires alteration to the dwelling unit.   

4 of 5 50  (b)A provision in a condominium’s governing document that restricts or prohibits the 
51 installation or use of a portable cooling device, is void and unenforceable, unless:    
52  (1) The installation or use of the device would:    
53  (i) Violate building codes or state or federal law;    
54  (ii) Violate the device manufacturer’s written safety guidelines for the device;    
55  (iii) Interfere with the common elements of the condominium; or    
56  (iv) Require amperage to power the device that cannot be accommodated by the power 
57 service to the building, unit or circuit;     
58  (2) The device would be installed in a window and:    
59  (i) The window is a necessary egress from the unit;    
60  (ii) The device would interfere with the unit owner’s ability to lock a window that is 
61 accessible from outside;    
62  (iii) The device requires the use of brackets or other hardware that would damage or void 
63 the warranty of the window or frame, puncture the envelope of the building or otherwise cause 
64 significant damages;   
65  (iv) The restrictions require that the device be adequately drained to prevent damage to 
66 the dwelling unit or building; or    
67  (v) The restrictions require that the device be installed in a manner that prevents risk of 
68 falling; or    

5 of 5 69  (3) The building is cooled through a central air conditioning system;  
70  (4) The restrictions are only to require that the device be:    
71  (i) Installed by building maintenance or a licensed contractor; or    
72  (ii) Removed from October 1 through April 30.   
73  SECTION 3. Notwithstanding any special or general law to the contrary, the department 
74 of housing and community development, in collaboration with the department of public health 
75 shall study whether to apply for funding to offer include cooling assistance in the application to 
76 the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services for the Low Income Home Energy 
77 Assistance Program. The study shall include, but not be limited to: (i) the number of households 
78 that could qualify for cooling assistance; (ii) the potential health impacts of offering cooling 
79 assistance including the impact on the rate of heat related illness and the effect for those 
80 suffering from chronic illnesses (iii) whether offering cooling assistance would impact the ability 
81 of the state to offer heating assistance (iv) the number of individuals that could qualify for 
82 cooling assistance that reside in heat islands. The secretary of the department of housing and 
83 community development shall report the findings of this study and any actions taken pursuant 
84 this section to the house and senate committees on ways and means, the joint committee on 
85 telecommunications, utilities and energy, the joint committee housing and the clerks of the house 
86 of representatives and senate not later than July 1, 2024.
