An Act relative to personal financial literacy education. 
 
 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives in General Court assembled, and by the authority 
of the same, as follows: 
 
1  SECTION 1. Chapter 29 of the General Laws is hereby amended by inserting after 
2 section 2LLLLLL the following section:- 
3  Section 2MMMMMM. (a) There shall be established and set up on the books of the 
4 commonwealth a separate, non-budgeted fund known as the Personal Financial Literacy 
5 Education Fund, which shall be administered by the commissioner of elementary and secondary 
6 education. The fund shall be credited with: (i) revenue from appropriations or other money 
7 authorized by the general court and specifically designated to be credited to the fund; (ii) funds 
8 from public and private sources such as gifts, grants and donations; and (iii) interest earned on 
9 such revenues. Amounts credited to the fund shall not be subject to further appropriation and any 
10 money remaining in the fund at the end of a fiscal year shall not revert to the General Fund. 
11  (b)(1) Amounts credited to the fund shall be expended, without further appropriation, for 
12 purposes related to educating middle school and high school students on personal financial 
13 literacy, including, but not limited to: (i) procurement, development and distribution of personal 

 
2 of 5 14 financial literacy curricular materials, including educational resources and other necessary 
15 materials; (ii) professional development opportunities, including trainings, seminars, conferences 
16 and materials for educators to use in the teaching of personal financial literacy; and (iii) the grant 
17 program established in subsection (d). 
18  (2) In expending amounts credited to the fund, the commissioner may prioritize: (i) 
19 underserved communities across the commonwealth, including those public schools or school 
20 districts with high concentrations of economically-disadvantaged students; and (ii) schools 
21 implementing a personal financial literacy education program for the first time consistent with 
22 the standards set by the department of elementary and secondary education pursuant to section 
23 102 of chapter 71. 
24  (c) Amounts received from private sources shall be approved by the commissioner of 
25 elementary and secondary education and subject to review before being deposited in the fund to 
26 ensure that pledged funds are not accompanied by conditions, explicit or implicit, on the 
27 implementation of personal financial literacy education programming that may be detrimental to 
28 the neutral and rigorous teaching of personal financial literacy or unduly influence the direction 
29 of personal financial literacy education policy. The review shall be made publicly available on 
30 the department of elementary and secondary education’s website. 
31  (d) There shall be a competitive grant program to promote the instruction of middle 
32 school and high school students on personal financial literacy developed and administered by the 
33 department of elementary and secondary education for all public schools and school districts. 
34 The department may expend funds from the fund for the grant program. The department may 

 
3 of 5 35 provide funds and other resources to public schools and school districts as needed to ensure that 
36 every public school and school district has the opportunity to apply for grants. 
37  (e) Annually, not later than October 1, the commissioner of elementary and secondary 
38 education shall report to the joint committee on education, the house and senate committees on 
39 ways and means and the clerks of the house of representatives and the senate on the fund’s 
40 activities. The report shall include, but not be limited to: (i) the source and amount of funds 
41 received; (ii) the expenditures made from the fund and the purpose of such expenditures; (iii) any 
42 grants provided pursuant to subsection (d); (iv) anticipated revenue and expenditure projections 
43 for the next year; (v) the number of schools and school districts that have used funds to 
44 implement a new program or enhance or maintain current programming; and (vi) the number of 
45 schools and school districts that applied for, but were not granted, funding. The report shall be 
46 made publicly available on the department of elementary and secondary education’s website.

56 progress of personal financial literacy programs in public schools and school districts to the joint

63  SECTION 4. Chapter 71 of the General Laws is hereby amended by adding the following 
64 section:- 
65  Section 102. (a) Every school district shall, for the purpose of educating middle school 
66 and high school students, provide instruction on personal financial literacy consistent with 
67 standards established by the department.  
68  (b) Instruction on personal financial literacy shall include, but shall not be limited to: (i) 
69 earning and spending income, local, state and federal taxes, charitable giving, methods of 
70 payment, consumer protection, balancing ledgers and checkbooks and budgeting; (ii) long-term 
71 savings, the role of banks and financial institutions, simple and compound interest, financial 
72 regulation and planning for the future; (iii) using credit and making investments, risks of various 
73 financial instruments and basic diversification of assets; (iv) protecting and insuring assets, 
74 preventing identity theft and avoiding online scams; (v) emerging technologies in the financial 
75 industry, a basic understanding of cryptocurrencies, online commerce and computer stock-
76 trading, how to evaluate digital media content relating to personal finance matters and how to 

 
5 of 5 77 recognize risk; and (vi) rights and responsibilities of renting or buying a home or making other 
78 large purchases or investments. 
79  (c)(1) The department shall assist schools in providing: (i) instruction on personal 
80 financial literacy by facilitating access to high-quality curricular materials; and (ii) professional 
81 development opportunities, including trainings, seminars, conferences and materials for 
82 educators to use in the teaching of personal financial literacy. 
83  (2) The department may provide trainings, seminars, conferences and materials for 
84 educators to use in the teaching of personal financial literacy in person or through the use of 
85 synchronous or asynchronous audio, video, electronic media or other telecommunications 
86 technology. 
87  (d) Nothing in this section shall require a school district to require personal financial 
88 literacy instruction in every year of middle school and high school; provided, however, that 
89 personal financial literacy education and instruction shall be utilized during appropriate times in 
90 middle school and high school curricula, as determined by the school district.  
91  SECTION 5. Section 4 shall take effect beginning in the 2026-2027 school year.
