Community Based Juvenile Justice Programs
Office of the Norfolk District Attorney
January 29, 2026
During 2025, the Norfolk District Attorney’s office administered over twenty community
based juvenile justice programs (school safety forums) serving twenty-seven communities in the
County of Norfolk. These programs were designed to help coordinate the efforts of school
systems, law enforcement, the Norfolk County Juvenile Probation Department, the
Massachusetts Department of Children and Families and the Massachusetts Department of Youth
Services, as appropriate, to help develop positive interventions for court-involved and at-risk
juveniles. Additionally, these confidential forums may serve to identify those juveniles whose
violent behavior could potentially pose a danger to school systems and communities, and to help
coordinate informational resources and other potential interventions designed to help maintain
school safety and deter the recurrence of violence.
These collaborative school safety forums have been in place in the Norfolk District for
more than twenty years. The Norfolk District Attorney’s Office has taken a leadership position
in scheduling these meetings, and in trying to facilitate and maintain open lines of
communication between the partner agencies. The Norfolk District Attorney’s Office
encourages and supports the regular participation of relevant agencies, as appropriate. Partner
agencies may choose to explain the specific services and interventions they may have available,
as well as any relevant criteria and protocols for accessing such services. The forums have been
an effective venue for agencies to share informational resources. The Norfolk District
Attorney’s Office assigns an Assistant District Attorney from the Norfolk District Attorney’s
Juvenile Justice Unit to each confidential forum to ensure consistency with towns and to assist in
communication. This Office provides information to these towns and school systems in order to
better protect victims present in the school systems with the juvenile and to update the school
systems so they may best assist the juvenile going forward.
These collaborative agencies include Juvenile Probation, the Department of Children and
Families, the Department of Youth Services, as well as the schools and local law enforcement.
The office works very closely with School Resource Officers (SROs) to enhance the Office’s
knowledge of school incidents as well as to learn best ways to support schools and juveniles.
General school safety concerns, precautions, and perceived trends are also discussed to further
support school and community prevention efforts. In addition to the work NDAO has done for
many years, in 2025, the Office has worked to implement a new diversion program through
MYDP, Massachusetts Youth Diversion Program, and has been the largest referral source to this
new program amongst the courts and law enforcement. This program has enabled the NDAO to
avail more juveniles of increased and improved resources to aid them and their families while
keeping these juveniles from gaining a criminal record.
The Norfolk Juvenile Court in Dedham services the nineteen communities of Avon,
Canton, Dedham, Dover, Foxborough, Franklin, Medfield, Medway, Millis, Needham, Norfolk,
Norwood, Plainville, Sharon, Stoughton, Walpole, Wellesley, Westwood, and Wrentham. There
were approximately 100 felony cases prioritized for discussion at the community based juvenile

justice programs in this service area. The Juvenile Session of the Quincy District Court services
the seven communities of Braintree, Cohasset, Holbrook, Milton, Quincy, Randolph, and
Weymouth. There were approximately 150 felony cases prioritized for discussion at the
community based juvenile justice programs in this service area. There were approximately 6
felony cases prioritized for discussion in Brookline. Additional adult co-defendant court
proceedings were also discussed as appropriate.
Currently, the Dedham Juvenile Court has a delinquency session two days per week and
the Quincy Juvenile Court has a delinquency session three days per week, with every other week
being two days a week. Brookline Juvenile Court holds a delinquency session once per month.
The Norfolk District Attorney’s Office provides strategic, targeted prevention, education
and training to our 28 communities and 34 public school districts. In 2025, following a review
of our caseload and diversion referrals, we continued to work on enhancing and expanding our
programming and better utilizing the diversion resources available to our office.
The NDAO offered the following in 2025:
1. Hosted an in-person Peer Leadership Breakfast Meeting for Norfolk County Public
School Student Leaders at the Tirrell Room in Quincy to kick-off the school-year long
Peer Leadership Program;
2. Hosted four (4) virtual Peer Leadership meetings for Norfolk County public school
student leaders focused on prevention topics, including;
a. Vaping education and Narcan training, in collaboration with the SAFE Coalition;
b. Advanced Internet Safety, in collaboration with Norfolk Advocates for Children
(NAC);
c. Distracted and impaired driving, in collaboration with AAA;
d. Healthy relationships and appropriate boundaries, in collaboration with DOVE;
3. Hosted an in-person Peer Leadership Conference for Norfolk County Public School
Student Leaders at the Cross Insurance Pavilion at Gillette Stadium in Foxborough,
featuring trainings by:
a. Minding Your Mind;
b. The 84 Group;
c. Drug Story Theater;
d. Dover Y2Y;
e. In Control Crash Prevention;
4. Provided Until Help Arrives, an interactive critical safety training created by the Federal
Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), to Norfolk County Communities;
5. Provided the Good Decision Class in partnership with Bay State Community Services as
part of the Norfolk District Attorney’s Diversion Program, and allowed public high
schools to incorporate the class into their disciplinary action involving substance use and
related risk-taking behaviors, even when students were not court-involved;

6. Provided access to a master’s-level clinician at no cost for behavioral and mental health
evaluations and referrals, if necessary, for teens and young adults in Norfolk County, in
partnership with Bay State Community Services;
7. Hosted quarterly Community Coalition meetings with community partners focused on
substance use prevention;
8. Hosted quarterly School/Youth Resource Officer meetings focused on substance use
prevention and behavioral intervention;
9. Hosted quarterly Medical Review meetings with medical professionals for Norfolk
County Community Coalition leaders, Law Enforcement Officers, Fire Departments; the
Department of Public Health, School Nurses, and other community partners;
10. Hosted countywide Mandated Reporter trainings for schools, school resource officers,
police departments, and fire departments in partnership with Norfolk Advocates for
Children (NAC), followed by guided tours of the NAC facility;
11. Hosted an in-person train-the-trainer anti-bullying program in collaboration with the
Massachusetts Aggression Reduction Center (MARC);
12. Provided grant funding for NaloxBoxes placed in public school buildings and other
municipal facilities throughout Norfolk County;
13. Replenished Naloxone supplies for Norfolk County public school districts;
14. Provided Narcan training to Norfolk County public school staff and municipal
employees;
15. Awarded Safe Celebration Grants in the amount of $500 to Norfolk County public high
schools to offset the cost of alcohol-free post-prom and graduation events;
16. Hosted the Making Norfolk County a Safe Place to Live Conference at the Tirrell Room
in Quincy, highlighting Norfolk District Attorney’s Office programs, trainings, grants,
and services related to school safety, behavioral health, and mental health for Norfolk
County Community Coalition leaders, Law Enforcement Officers, Fire Departments; the
Department of Public Health, School Administrators, and other community partners;
17. Provided grant funding for Community Resource Dogs to support police departments and
schools in serving children in crisis and strengthening trust between agencies and local
children and families;
18. Provided the FireSense diversionary program to reduce juvenile fire setting and educate
youth on the dangers associated with fire-related behavior;
19. Provided Third Millennium diversionary programming to diverted youth, including
Conflict Wise, Diversity Equity & Inclusion, Nicotine and Other Drugs, Respect &
Resolve, THC 101, Under the Influence, and Shoplifting;
20. Provided Fatal Vision Education, a live in-person alcohol and marijuana impairment
simulation demonstrating the effects of substances on vision, reaction time, memory, and
problem-solving;

21. Hosted live Disabled Persons Protection Commission (DPPC) trainings Norfolk County
Community Coalition leaders, Law Enforcement Officers, Fire Departments; the
Department of Public Health, School Administrators, and other community partners;
22. Facilitated Advanced Internet Safety trainings for Norfolk County youth, parents, and
professionals;
23. Partnered with NCMEC to offer train-the-trainer Internet safety opportunities and
expanded access for county professionals to additional specialized training opportunities;
24. Provided Community Resource Dog visits to Norfolk County schools and community
events;
25. Conducted a Designate a Water Watcher public awareness campaign prior to the summer
season to promote water safety and prevent water-related injuries and fatalities;
26. Participated in multiple community policing and public safety outreach events across
Norfolk County, including National Night Out and First Responders Day;
27. Provided In Control Crash Prevention training to Norfolk County High Schools;
28. Provided IROC2 digital citizenship and media literacy training programming to Norfolk
County Middle Schools;
29. Partnered with Sandy Hook Promise to support Norfolk County school districts in
implementing the Start with Hello program and Anonymous Reporting System; and
30. Distributed Commercial Sexual Exploitation of Children (CSEC) and Human Trafficking
personal care kits to Norfolk County Police Departments.
