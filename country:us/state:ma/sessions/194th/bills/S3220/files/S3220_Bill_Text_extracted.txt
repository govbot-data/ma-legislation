2  "SECTION ___. Chapter 29 of the General Laws is hereby amended by inserting after 
3 section 2NNNNNN the following section:- 
4  Section 2OOOOOO. (a) There is hereby established in the office of the state treasurer a 
5 separate, non-budgeted special revenue fund known as the Massachusetts Secure Choice Savings 
6 Fund which shall be administered by the state treasurer. The fund shall be credited with: (i) 
7 money from the payment of fees, penalties and other payments due to the Massachusetts secure 
8 choice savings program established in section 64H; (ii) appropriations or other money authorized 
9 or transferred by the general court and specifically designated to be credited to the fund and any 
10 funds appropriated by the federal or local governments; (iii) private contributions and publicly or 
11 privately-funded grants; and (iv) any interest earned on the assets of the fund. Monies in the fund 
12 that are unexpended at the end of the fiscal year shall not revert to the General Fund and shall be 
13 available for expenditure in the subsequent fiscal year. Section 38 shall not apply to any 
14 investment of the fund. 

2 of 12 15  (b)The fund shall cover all expenses associated with the administration of the 
16 Massachusetts secure choice savings program not otherwise covered by the program, including, 
17 but not limited to, expenses related to program compliance and oversight responsibilities. 
18  (c)The Massachusetts secure choice savings board, as established in section 64G, shall 
19 establish guidelines regarding administration of the fund. 
20  SECTION ___. Said chapter 29 is hereby further amended by inserting after section 64E 
21 the following 5 sections:-

23 following words shall, unless the context clearly requires otherwise, have the following 
24 meanings: 
25  “Board”, the Massachusetts secure choice savings board established pursuant to section 
26 64G. 
27  “Client” or “client company”, as defined in section 192 of chapter 149. 
28  “Code”, as defined in section 1 of chapter 62. 
29  “Commissioner”, the commissioner of revenue. 
30  “Client” or “client company”, as defined in section 192 of chapter 149. 
31  “Department”, the department of revenue.

34 under chapter 62. For purposes of this section, an employee who is a covered employee under a

38 enterprise in the commonwealth, whether for-profit or not-for-profit, that has: (i) at no time

62  Section 64G. (a)(1) There is hereby established the Massachusetts secure choice savings 
63 board. The board shall consist of the following 5 members: the state treasurer or a designee, who 
64 shall serve as chair; the comptroller or a designee; the secretary of the commonwealth or a 
65 designee; a public representative with expertise in retirement savings plan administration or 
66 investment, or both, who is representative of participating employees, appointed by the governor; 
67 and a public representative with expertise in retirement savings plan administration or 
68 investment, or both, who is representative of participating employers, appointed by the state 
69 treasurer. 
70  (2) Each member shall be appointed for a term of 4 years; provided, however, that the 
71 public representative of employers shall be appointed initially for a term of 3 years; and provided 
72 further, that all members shall be eligible for reappointment. A vacancy in the term of an 
73 appointed board member shall be filled for the balance of the unexpired term in the same manner 
74 as the original appointment. Members of the board shall serve without compensation but shall be 
75 reimbursed for reasonable expenses incurred in the performance of their official duties. 

5 of 12 76  (3) The board may appoint or engage agents. The board, the individual members of the 
77 board, any other agents appointed or engaged by the board and all persons serving as program 
78 staff shall discharge their duties with respect to the program solely in the interest of the 
79 program’s enrollees and beneficiaries. 
80  (b) The board shall be responsible for ongoing fiduciary administrative oversight of the 
81 program for the purpose of promoting greater retirement savings for private-sector employees in 
82 a convenient, low-cost and portable manner, pursuant to paragraph (1) of subsection (a) of 
83 section 64H. 
84  (c) The board shall report annually to the state treasurer, governor, comptroller, secretary 
85 of the commonwealth and the house and senate committees on ways and means. The report shall 
86 include, but shall not be limited to: (i) an audited financial report, prepared in accordance with 
87 generally accepted accounting principles; (ii) a summary of the benefits provided by the 
88 program, including the number of enrollees; and (iii) the percentage and amounts of investment 
89 options and rates of return. 
90  (d) All agencies of the commonwealth shall cooperate as requested by the board in the 
91 performance of their duties under this section, including, unless otherwise prohibited, the sharing 
92 of relevant data as the parties shall mutually agree. 
93  Section 64H. (a) There is hereby established in the office of the state treasurer the 
94 Massachusetts secure choice savings program, subject to appropriation. The program shall be 
95 developed and administered by the state treasurer with ongoing fiduciary administrative 
96 oversight provided by the board for the purpose of promoting greater retirement savings for 
97 private-sector employees in a convenient, low-cost and portable manner. 

6 of 12 98  (b)(1) The state treasurer shall design, establish and operate the program in a manner that: 
99 (i) accords with best practices for retirement savings vehicles; (ii) maximizes participation, 
100 savings and sound investment practices; (iii) maximizes simplicity, including ease of 
101 administration for participating employers and enrollees; (iv) provides an efficient product to 
102 enrollees by pooling investment funds; and (v) ensures the portability of benefits. 
103  (2) The state treasurer and the department shall maintain, on their websites, information 
104 for employers on the requirements of the program and information on retirement plans an 
105 employer may offer as an alternative to the program, including, but not limited to, a defined 
106 benefit plan, 401(k) plan, a Simplified Employee Pension (SEP) plan or a Savings Incentive 
107 Match Plan for Employees (SIMPLE) plan. 
108  (a) The state treasurer shall prepare a written statement of investment policy that includes 
109 a risk management and oversight program for consideration and adoption by the board. 
110  (b) The state treasurer may contract with practitioners, administrators, investment 
111 managers and other entities to design, administer and provide investment options under the 
112 program. Any practitioner, administrator, investment manager or other entity with which the 
113 state treasurer contracts shall comply with all applicable federal and state laws, rules and 
114 regulations and all rules, policies and guidelines promulgated by the board with respect to the 
115 program and the investment of the fund, including, but not limited to, the investment policy. Any 
116 practitioner, administrator, investment manager or other entity with which the state treasurer 
117 contracts shall provide such reports as the board deems necessary to assess performance. 

7 of 12 118  (c)The state treasurer shall assess the feasibility of multistate or regional agreements to 
119 administer the program through shared administrative and operational resources and may enter 
120 into those agreements if deemed beneficial to the program. 
121  (f)(1) The commonwealth, the board, each member of the board or other commonwealth 
122 official, other commonwealth boards, commissions or agencies, or any member, officer or 
123 employee thereof, and the program: (i) shall have no responsibility for compliance by individuals 
124 with the conditions and other provisions of the Code that determine which individuals are 
125 eligible to make tax-favored contributions to IRAs, in what amount and in what time frame and 
126 manner; (ii) shall have no duty, responsibility or liability to any party for the payment of any 
127 benefits under the program, regardless of whether sufficient funds are available under the 
128 program to pay such benefits; (iii) do not and shall not guarantee any interest rate or other rate of 
129 return on or investment performance of any contribution or account balance; and (iv) are not and 
130 shall not be liable or responsible for any loss, deficiency, failure to realize any gain or any other 
131 adverse consequences, including, but not limited to, any adverse tax consequences or loss of 
132 favorable tax treatment, public assistance or other benefits incurred by any person as a result of 
133 participating in the program. 
134  (2) The debts, contracts and obligations of the program shall not be considered the debts, 
135 contracts and obligations of the commonwealth, and neither the faith and credit nor the taxing 
136 power of the commonwealth shall be pledged directly or indirectly to the payment of the debts, 
137 contracts and obligations of the program. 

8 of 12 138  (3) Participating employers shall not have any liability for an employee’s decision to 
139 participate in, or opt out of, the program or for the investment decisions of the board or of any 
140 enrollee. 
141  (4) A participating employer shall not be a fiduciary, or considered to be a fiduciary, over 
142 the program. A participating employer shall not bear responsibility for the administration, 
143 investment options or investment performance of the program. A participating employer shall 
144 not be liable with regard to investment returns, program design and benefits paid to program 
145 participants. 
146  (g) All agencies of the commonwealth shall cooperate as requested by the state treasurer 
147 in the performance of their duties under this section, including, unless otherwise prohibited, the 
148 sharing of relevant data as the parties shall mutually agree. 
149  Section 64I. (a) The Program shall be implemented, and enrollment of employees shall 
150 begin, within 24 months after the effective date of this Act. The provisions of this Section shall 
151 be in force after the Board opens the Program for enrollment. 
152  (1) Each employer shall establish a payroll deposit retirement savings arrangement to 
153 allow each employee to participate in the program on a timeline set by the board. 
154  (2) Employers shall automatically enroll each employee, who has not opted out of 
155 participation, in the program and shall provide payroll deduction retirement savings 
156 arrangements and deposit, on behalf of each such employee, these funds into the program. 
157  (3) Employers shall retain the option at all times to set up any type of employer-
158 sponsored retirement plan, including, but not limited to, a defined benefit plan, a 401(k), a 

9 of 12 159 Simplified Employee Pension (SEP) plan, a Savings Incentive Match Plan for Employees 
160 (SIMPLE) plan, or a plan sponsored by a PEO with which the client company has a professional 
161 employer agreement as such terms are defined in section 192 of chapter 149, instead of having a 
162 payroll deposit retirement savings arrangement to allow employee participation in the program. 
163  (b)(1) Enrollees shall have the ability to: (i) select a contribution level into the program; 
164 or (ii) opt out of participation in the program. The contribution level may be expressed as a 
165 percentage of wages or as a dollar amount up to the deductible amount for the enrollee’s taxable 
166 year under section 219(b)(1)(A) of the Code. Enrollees may change their contribution level at 
167 any time and that election shall be honored as soon as administratively feasible. If an enrollee 
168 fails to select a contribution level using the form prescribed, the enrollee shall contribute the 
169 default contribution rate of 6 per cent, with an annual escalation of 1 per cent and up to 10 per 
170 cent, of their wages to the program. 
171  (2) Enrollees may select an investment option from the permitted investment options 
172 available under the program. Enrollees may change their investment option at any time. If an 
173 enrollee fails to select an investment option, the enrollee shall be placed in a qualified default 
174 investment alternative specified by the program. 
175  (3) An enrollee may terminate their participation in the program at any time in a manner 
176 prescribed by the program. 
177  (c)(1) In the case of a PEO relationship as such term is defined in section 192 of chapter 
178 149: 

10 of 12 179  (i) if a PEO offers a qualified retirement plan to a covered employee, the client company 
180 is considered as offering a qualified retirement plan and shall not be considered an employer 
181 required to automatically enroll each employee in the program as described in this section; and 
182  (ii) if a client company is an employer required to automatically enroll each employee in 
183 the program, the client company, not the PEO, shall be responsible for complying with all 
184 applicable requirements under this section. 
185  (2) Nothing in this section shall prevent an employer from entering into a professional 
186 employer agreement with a PEO under which the PEO agrees to assist the client company with 
187 the performance of some or all of the client company’s responsibilities under this section. 
188  Section 64J. (a) An employer who fails without reasonable cause to enroll an employee, 
189 who has not elected out of participation, in the program within the time prescribed by the state 
190 treasurer, in consultation with the department, shall be subject to a penalty equal to: 
191  (i) $250 for each employee for each calendar year or portion of a calendar year during 
192 which the employee neither was enrolled in the program nor had elected out of participation in 
193 the program, and any appropriate official of the commonwealth may bring a civil action to 
194 require the employer to enroll the employee and shall recover such costs and reasonable 
195 attorney’s fees as may be allowed by the court; and 
196  (ii) for each calendar year beginning after the date a penalty has been assessed with 
197 respect to an employee, $500 for any portion of that calendar year during which such employee 
198 continues to be unenrolled without electing out of participation in the program, and any 
199 appropriate official of the commonwealth may bring a civil action to require the employer to 

11 of 12 200 enroll the employee and shall recover such costs and reasonable attorney’s fees as may be 
201 allowed by the court. 
202  (b) No penalty shall be imposed under subsection (a) for any failure for which it is 
203 established that the employer, subject to liability for the penalty, did not know that the failure 
204 existed and exercised reasonable diligence to meet the requirements of this section or where: 
205  (i) the employer subject to liability for the penalty exercised reasonable diligence to meet 
206 those requirements; and 
207  (ii) the employer complies with those requirements with respect to each employee by the 
208 end of the 90-day period beginning on the first date the employer knew, or exercising reasonable 
209 diligence would have known, that the failure existed. 
210  (c) In the case of a failure that is due to reasonable cause and not to willful neglect, all or 
211 part of the penalty may be waived to the extent that the payment of the penalty would be 
212 excessive or otherwise inequitable relative to the failure involved. 
213  (d) If a participating employer fails to transmit a payroll deduction contribution to the 
214 program on the earliest date the amount withheld from the enrollee’s compensation may 
215 reasonably be segregated from the participating employer’s assets, but not later than the 15th day 
216 of the month following the month in which the enrollee’s contribution amounts are withheld 
217 from their paycheck, the failure to remit such contributions on a timely basis shall be subject to 
218 the same sanctions as employer misappropriation of employee wage withholdings, including 
219 those pursuant to sections 148 and 150 of chapter 149 and to the penalties specified in subsection 
220 (a). 

12 of 12 221  (e) Except as provided in this subsection, all information received by the department from 
222 returns filed by an employer or from any investigation conducted under this section shall be 
223 confidential, except for official purposes within the department or pursuant to official procedures 
224 for collection of penalties assessed under this section. Nothing contained in this section shall 
225 prevent the commissioner from publishing or making available to the public reasonable statistics 
226 concerning the operation of this section wherein the contents of returns are grouped into 
227 aggregates in such a way that the specific information of any employer shall not be disclosed. 
228 Nothing contained in this section shall prevent the commissioner from divulging information to 
229 an authorized representative of the employer or to any person pursuant to a request or 
230 authorization made by the employer or by an authorized representative of the employer. 
231  (f) Civil penalties and fees collected under this section shall be deposited into the fund. 
232  (g) The department may promulgate rules and regulations as necessary or proper for the 
233 administration and enforcement of this section."
