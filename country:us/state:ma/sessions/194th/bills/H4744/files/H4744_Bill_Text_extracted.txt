An Act relative to energy affordability, clean power and economic competitiveness.
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives in General Court assembled, and by the authority
of the same, as follows:
1 SECTION 1. The general court finds and declares that:
2 (a) federal actions related to offshore wind, including the suspension of new approvals
3 for offshore wind projects and the reconsideration of previously approved offshore wind
4 projects and designated offshore wind areas have created regulatory uncertainty, delayed
5 construction schedules, and threatened hundreds of millions of dollars in private investment for
6 offshore wind projects in the commonwealth;
7 (b) the United States Department of Transportation cancelled a previously-awarded
8 $34,000,000 grant intended to support the development of an offshore wind staging and port
9 facility in the City of Salem, thereby disrupting an important component of the commonwealth’s
10 strategy to build regional supply-chain capacity and delaying job creation, private-sector
11 investment and regional industrial growth;
1 of 106

12 (c) the regional independent grid system operator, ISO-New England, has stated that as
13 demand for electricity grows, New England must maintain and add to its energy infrastructure to
14 ensure system reliability and that delays in the availability of new resources, regardless of
15 technology, will adversely affect the region’s economy and industrial growth;
16 (d) the United States Environmental Protection Agency has terminated $156,000,000 in
17 funding for the Solar for All Program in Massachusetts, hindering solar development in low-
18 income and historically disadvantaged communities;
19 (e) the United States has withheld approximately $63,000,000 in funding from the
20 commonwealth to develop electric vehicle charging infrastructure under the national electric
21 vehicle infrastructure program, threatening the commonwealth’s ability to deploy the charging
22 stations needed to meet its clean transportation goals;
23 (f) the United States Congress has enacted legislation accelerating the phase-out of clean
24 electricity investment and production tax credits for offshore wind and solar projects, altering
25 advanced manufacturing production credits for offshore wind components, eliminating
26 residential clean energy and personal electric vehicle tax credits, and constraining states’
27 authority to adopt more rigorous vehicle emissions standards, together erecting substantial
28 barriers to deployment of renewable generation, electrification of vehicles and homes, and
29 private investment in the clean energy sector;
30 (g) the cumulative effect of these federal actions threaten the commonwealth’s planned
31 jobs, supply-chain development, emissions reductions, and energy-equity goals, and materially
32 increases the cost, time, regulatory risk and ratepayer burden of advancing clean energy
33 objectives; and
2 of 106

34 (h) the commonwealth must establish emissions reduction goals that responsibly advance
35 progress toward the 2050 targets while balancing environmental objectives with the need to
36 avoid undue financial burden on ratepayers, and to ensure that the benefits and costs of the clean
37 energy transition are shared equitably across all communities, with particular attention to low-
38 income households and those historically overburdened by energy and environmental inequities.

50 SECTION 3. Said chapter 21N is hereby further amended by adding the following
51 section:-
52 Section 13. (a) All state agencies, departments, authorities, and instrumentalities with
53 jurisdiction over energy, climate, emissions, utilities, building standards, portfolio standards,
54 transportation electrification, or related regulatory actions shall be subject to this section.
3 of 106

55 (b) No regulation, administrative action, program implementation, procurement
56 requirement, or policy issued by a state entity under this chapter or any other general or special
57 law undertaken to comply with or contribute to the compliance of statewide greenhouse gas
58 emission limits and sublimits established under this chapter or to advance the commonwealth’s
59 clean energy goals, shall take effect unless the entity has determined, based on reasonably
60 available data, that such action will not impose unreasonable adverse impacts on:
61 (i) the affordability of energy, utility, or related costs for residential ratepayers, including
62 low-income and fixed-income households; or
63 (ii) the operating costs or economic competitiveness of Massachusetts businesses,
64 including commercial, industrial, and small business customers.
65 (c) Prior to any such action taking effect, the agency or department shall prepare an
66 affordability and competitiveness assessment that, at a minimum:
67 (i) identifies expected cost impacts on residential, commercial, and industrial ratepayers;
68 (ii) evaluates whether those impacts are reasonable in light of projected benefits,
69 available funding mechanisms, and cost-mitigation measures;
70 (iii) considers cumulative impacts in combination with existing requirements; and
71 (iv) consults with relevant stakeholders, including ratepayer advocates, business
72 organizations, and utilities, as appropriate.
73 (d) If the assessment identifies a potential for unreasonable adverse impacts, the agency
74 shall either:
4 of 106

75 (i) modify the proposed action to avoid or mitigate such impacts; or
76 (ii) adopt alternative compliance pathways, timelines, or cost-containment measures to
77 ensure affordability and competitiveness are preserved.
78 (e) The completed assessment shall be made publicly available at least 30 days before the
79 final adoption or implementation of the proposed action.
80 (f) Failure to comply with the requirements of this section shall constitute grounds for
81 administrative reconsideration or judicial review under chapter 30A.
82 (g) Nothing in this section shall be construed to prohibit the Commonwealth from
83 pursuing long-term greenhouse gas reduction, renewable energy deployment, or energy-
84 efficiency measures, provided such actions comply with the affordability and competitiveness
85 standard established herein.
86 (h) For purposes of this section, the social value of emissions reductions shall only be
87 considered where such reductions are demonstrably identified by said entity and realizable from
88 non-carbon energy sources.
