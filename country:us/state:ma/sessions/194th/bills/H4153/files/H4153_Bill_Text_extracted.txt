An Act establishing a charter for the city of Somerville. 
 
 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives in General Court assembled, and by the authority 
of the same, as follows: 
 
1  SECTION 1. The following shall be the charter for the city of Somerville: 
2  PREAMBLE 
3  We, the people of Somerville, with our diversity in culture and thought, in order to 
4 reaffirm the liberties of the people with respect to the conduct of our local government, adopt 
5 this charter to continue and enhance the city’s strong traditions of the pursuit of justice; 
6 empowerment of residents; ethical, transparent and responsive leadership; wise use of public 
7 resources; representation for all; and an engaged populace. We expect that our government will 
8 be approachable, accountable, equitable, inclusive, and respectful towards all people, and it will 
9 strive to provide all residents an equal opportunity to participate fully in the economic, cultural 
10 and intellectual life of the city. 
11  ARTICLE 1 

2 of 50 12  INCORPORATION; SHORT TITLE; DEFINITIONS 
13  SECTION 1-1: INCORPORATION 
14  The residents of the City of Somerville within the territorial limits established by law, 
15 shall continue to be a municipal corporation, a body corporate and politic, under the name "City 
16 of Somerville.” 
17  SECTION 1-2: SHORT TITLE 
18  This instrument shall be known and may be cited as the City of Somerville Charter 
19 (“charter”). 
20    
21  SECTION 1-3: SEPARATION OF POWERS 
22  The administration of the fiscal, prudential and municipal affairs of the city shall be 
23 vested in an executive branch led by a mayor and a legislative branch consisting of a city 
24 council. The legislative branch shall never exercise any executive power and the executive 
25 branch shall never exercise any legislative power. 
26  SECTION 1-4: POWERS OF THE CITY 
27  Subject only to express limitations on the exercise of any power or function by a 
28 municipal government in the constitution or General Laws, it is the intention and the purpose of 
29 the voters of the city, through the adoption of this charter, to secure for themselves and their 
30 government all of the powers it is possible to secure as fully and as completely as though each 
31 power were specifically and individually enumerated in this charter. 

3 of 50 32  SECTION 1-5: CONSTRUCTION 
33  The powers of the city under this charter are to be construed liberally in favor of the city 
34 and the specific mention of any particular power is not intended to limit the general powers of 
35 the city as stated in section 1-4. To the extent that any provision of this charter shall conflict with 
36 any special act or law adopted by the city to the contrary, the provisions of this charter shall 
37 prevail. 
38  SECTION 1-6: INTERGOVERNMENTAL RELATIONS 
39  Subject only to express limitations in the constitution or General Laws, the city may 
40 exercise any of its powers or perform any of its functions and may participate in the financing 
41 thereof, jointly or in cooperation, by contract or otherwise, with the commonwealth or any of its 
42 agencies or political subdivisions, or with the United States government or any of its agencies.

89  ARTICLE 2 
90  LEGISLATIVE BRANCH 
91  SECTION 2-1: COMPOSITION; TERM OF OFFICE; ELIGIBILITY 

6 of 50 92  (a) Composition - There shall be a city council consisting of 11 members which shall 
93 exercise the legislative powers of the city. Four of these members, to be known as councilors at-
94 large, shall be nominated and elected by the municipal voters at large. Seven of these members, 
95 to be known as ward councilors, shall be nominated from and elected by the municipal voters in 
96 each ward, with 1 ward councilor to be elected from each of the 7 wards into which the city is 
97 divided under section 7-7. 
98  (b) Term of Office - The term of office for councilors shall be 2 years each, beginning on 
99 the first business day in January following the municipal election. 
100  (c) Eligibility - Any statewide voter residing in the city shall be eligible to hold the office 
101 of councilor at-large. Any statewide voter residing in the ward from which election is sought 
102 shall be eligible to hold the office of ward councilor. If the city council determines that a ward 
103 councilor or a councilor at-large has removed from the city during the councilor’s term, that 
104 office shall immediately be deemed vacant and filled in the manner provided in section 2-12. A 
105 ward councilor who removes from the ward in which the councilor was elected and who remains 
106 a municipal voter of the city may continue to serve the balance of the term to which elected. 
107  SECTION 2-2: PROHIBITIONS 
108  (a) Holding Other City Position – Except as otherwise provided by this charter, a member 
109 of the city council shall hold no other compensated city position. A former member of the city 
110 council shall not hold any compensated appointed city position for 1 year after termination of 
111 their service on the city council, unless the city council approves a waiver of this provision by 
112 affirmative vote of 8 members of the city council. This section shall not prevent a city employee 

7 of 50 113 who vacated a position to serve as a member of the city council from returning to the same 
114 position upon the expiration of the term for which that person was elected. 
115  (b) Interference with Administration - The city council or any member of the city council 
116 shall not give orders or directions to any employee of the city appointed by the mayor, either 
117 publicly or privately. 
118  SECTION 2-3: COMPENSATION 
119  The members of the city council shall receive compensation for their services as set by 
120 ordinance. An ordinance increasing or reducing the compensation of the members of the city 
121 council shall not be effective unless: 
122  (1) it is adopted by affirmative vote of 8 members of the city council; 
123  (2) it is adopted prior to the last 6 months of the city council’s term; and 
124  (3) it provides that the compensation increase or reduction is to take effect upon the 
125 organization of the city government following the next regular municipal election. 
126  SECTION 2-4: GENERAL POWERS 
127  Except as otherwise provided by the General Laws or by this charter, all powers of the 
128 city shall be vested in the city council which shall provide for the performance of all duties and 
129 obligations imposed upon the city by law. 
130  SECTION 2-5: EXERCISE OF POWERS; QUORUM; RULES 

8 of 50 131  (a) Exercise of Powers - Except as otherwise provided by General Laws or by this 
132 charter, the legislative powers of the city council may be exercised in a manner determined by 
133 the city council. 
134  (b) Quorum - Once a quorum is present, a majority vote shall be required to adopt any 
135 ordinance, except as otherwise provided by General Laws or by this charter. 
136  (c) Rules of Procedure - The city council shall adopt rules regulating the procedures of 
137 the city council, which shall be subject to adoption, amendment or repeal by vote of the city 
138 council.  
139  (d) Meetings - Regular meetings of the city council shall be held at a time and place fixed 
140 by ordinance. 
141  (e) Special meetings of the city council shall be held at the call of the president or at the 
142 call of any 6 or more members, for any purpose. Except in an emergency as declared by the city 
143 council president, notice of the meeting shall be delivered to the city clerk at least 3 business 
144 days in advance of the time set and shall specify the date, time, location and purpose for which 
145 the meeting is to be held. The city clerk shall post an agenda for such meeting at least 2 business 
146 days in advance of the time set.   
147  (f) All sessions of the city council and every city council committee or subcommittee 
148 shall, at all times, be open to the public, unless otherwise specified by law. 
149  (g) A full, accurate, up-to-date account of the proceedings of the city council shall be 
150 maintained by the city clerk, which shall include a record of each vote taken and be made 
151 available with reasonable promptness following each meeting. Executive session minutes shall 

9 of 50 152 be made available as soon as publication of the minutes would not defeat the purpose of the 
153 executive session, unless otherwise provided by law.

155  SECTION 2-6: ELECTION, TERM, AND POWERS OF CITY COUNCIL PRESIDENT 
156 AND VICE-PRESIDENT 
157  (a) Election and Term - As soon as practicable after the councilors-elect have been 
158 qualified following each regular municipal election, as provided in section 7-4, the city council 
159 shall elect from among its members a president and vice-president who shall serve for 1-year 
160 terms. The member of the council with the most years of service shall preside at the election. If 2 
161 or more members have the same term of service the councilor with the last name that is first in 
162 alphabetical order from among such councilors shall preside. The conduct of all elections of the 
163 city council president and vice-president shall otherwise be prescribed within the rules of the city 
164 council. 
165  (b) Powers and Duties - The president shall have the following powers and duties: 
166  (1) determine the agenda for city council meetings; 
167  (2) preside at all meetings of the city council, regulate its proceedings, and decide all 
168 questions of order, provided, however, that the vice-president shall preside in the absence of the 
169 president; 
170  (3) appoint all members of committees of the city council, whether special or standing; 
171  (4) have the same powers to vote upon measures coming before the city council as any 
172 other member of the city council; and 

10 of 50 173  (5) perform any other duties consistent with the office that are established by this charter, 
174 ordinance or other vote of the city council. 
175  SECTION 2-7: APPOINTMENTS OF THE CITY COUNCIL 
176  (a) City Clerk - The city council shall appoint a city clerk to serve at the pleasure of the 
177 city council and until a qualified successor is chosen. The city clerk shall have the powers and 
178 duties relating to the keeping of records and vital statistics, the issuance of licenses as are 
179 provided to city clerks by the General Laws and such additional powers and duties as may be 
180 provided by law or by other vote of the city council. 
181  (b) Advisory Legal Counsel - Subject to appropriation, the city council may secure legal 
182 services, when needed, to seek a secondary legal opinion. The city council shall make a 
183 reasonable request for legal services, and that request shall not be unreasonably denied by the 
184 mayor. The city solicitor and any advisory legal counsel shall consult to the extent practicable 
185 prior to the release of any secondary opinion. The legal services provided to the council shall not 
186 include representation of the council or any councilor in any litigation, or the issuance of formal 
187 legal opinions on behalf of the city. The city solicitor shall remain the only authorized officer of 
188 the city in all legal matters involving the city’s government. 
189  (c) Other Staff - Subject to appropriation, the city council may employ staff as it deems 
190 necessary. 
191  (d) Removals and Suspensions of City Council Staff - City council appointments may be 
192 removed at the sole discretion of the city council subject to limitations and requirements imposed 
193 by federal and state laws, rules or regulations.  

11 of 50 194  SECTION 2-8: CITY COUNCIL CONFIRMATION OF CERTAIN APPOINTMENTS 
195  (a) Department Heads - The mayor shall refer to the city council for confirmation and 
196 simultaneously file with the city clerk the name of each person the mayor appoints as a 
197 department head. These appointments shall be considered confirmed 30 days from the date of the 
198 first regularly scheduled city council meeting after the date on which notice of the appointment 
199 was filed with the city clerk, unless the city council within said 30 days shall reject the 
200 appointment by the affirmative vote of 8 members. However, if no city council meeting is held 
201 within the subsequent 30 days of the first meeting, the city council shall instead have 30 days 
202 from the second regularly scheduled meeting after the appointment to reject the appointment. 
203 The city council shall not unreasonably reject an appointment and shall accompany a rejection 
204 with a written statement describing the reason, which shall be delivered to the mayor and placed 
205 on file with the city clerk within 30 days of the city council’s vote rejecting an appointment. The 
206 question on rejection of any appointment made by the mayor shall not be subject to the 
207 procedure of charter objection provided in section 2-9(b) of this charter. 
208  (b) Multiple Member Bodies - The mayor shall refer to the city council and 
209 simultaneously file with the city clerk the name of each person the mayor desires to appoint as a 
210 member of a multiple member body. The city council shall have 60 days after the date on which 
211 notice of the proposed appointment was filed with the city clerk to vote to approve or reject the 
212 appointment, with approval not to be unreasonably withheld. If the city council does not take 
213 action on the appointment within 60 days the appointment shall be deemed approved. Multiple 
214 member body members may be removed at the discretion of their appointing authority. 

12 of 50 215  (1) All appointments to quasi-judicial bodies and regulatory bodies shall be for terms 
216 established by administrative order or the General Laws. Any member of a quasi-judicial body or 
217 regulatory body appointed to a successive term shall be subject to confirmation by the city 
218 council under section 2-8(b) upon the expiration of each term. 
219  (2) In the event of a vacancy on a multiple member body where the seat has remained 
220 vacant in excess of 1 year and the mayor has not referred to the city council any proposed 
221 appointees to fill the vacancy, the city council president may present to the mayor the names of 
222 up to 3 persons as recommendations for appointment. The procedure for selecting names of 
223 persons for presentation to the mayor shall be established within the rules of the city council. 
224  (3) The mayor shall twice annually, in February and August, post a complete list of the 
225 vacancies on all multiple member bodies, as well as the procedures for individuals to apply to 
226 become a member of such bodies. This posting shall be in addition to, and not a substitute for, 
227 regular posting for the purpose of filling vacancies as they arise. 
228  (c) Police and Fire Employees - The mayor shall refer to the city council and 
229 simultaneously file with the city clerk the name of each person the mayor desires to appoint or 
230 promote as a member or officer of the police department or the fire department. The city council 
231 shall not unreasonably withhold confirmation of appointments, shall adhere to any merit 
232 principles identified in applicable law, including, but not limited to applicable civil service law, 
233 and shall accompany a rejection with a written statement describing the reason, which shall be 
234 delivered to and placed on file with the city clerk within 30 days of that rejection. The question 
235 on confirmation of any appointment submitted by the mayor shall not be subject to the procedure 
236 of charter objection provided in section 2-9(b) of this charter.  

13 of 50 237  SECTION 2-9: ORDINANCES AND OTHER MEASURES 
238  (a) Measures - Every adopted measure shall become effective at the expiration of 10 days 
239 after adoption or upon the signature of the mayor, whichever occurs first, unless the ordinance or 
240 this charter provides otherwise. An ordinance shall not be amended or repealed except by 
241 another ordinance adopted in accordance with this charter. 
242  (b) Charter Objection - On the first occasion that the question on adoption of a measure is 
243 put to the city council, a single member may object to the taking of the vote and postpone the 
244 vote until the next meeting of the city council, whether regular or special. If 2 or more members 
245 object, the vote shall be postponed until the next regular meeting. This procedure shall not be 
246 used more than once for any specific matter regardless of whether it has been amended. A 
247 charter objection shall have privilege over all motions provided, however, that it shall be raised 
248 prior to or at the call for a vote by the presiding officer and all debate shall cease. 
249  SECTION 2-10: ACCESS TO INFORMATION 
250  (a) In General - The city council may make investigations into the affairs of the city and 
251 into the conduct and performance of any city agency. Absent compelling circumstances or an 
252 emergency which shall be declared on record and noted in the city council minutes, the city 
253 council shall give a minimum of 14 days’ notice to any person it may require to appear before it 
254 under this section. In the case of compelling circumstances or emergency, 7 days’ notice shall be 
255 allowed. The notice shall include specific questions on which the city council seeks information 
256 and any person called to appear before the city council under this section shall not be required to 
257 respond to any question not relevant or related to those questions presented in advance and in 
258 writing. The mayor shall receive a copy of any notice issued under this section at the same time 

14 of 50 259 as the person who is requested to appear before the council. A majority vote of the city council 
260 shall be required to issue notice under this section. 
261  (b) Department Head - The city council may require, by majority vote, specific 
262 information from a department head or their designee on any matter related to the municipal 
263 services, functions and powers or duties which are within the scope of responsibility of such 
264 department head and related to the official duties and responsibilities of the city council. The 
265 department head or their designee shall not be required to answer questions relating to any other 
266 matter outside the scope of the matter noticed or provide information the release of which is 
267 prohibited by law. 
268  (c) Mayor - The city council may request, by majority vote, specific information from the 
269 mayor on any municipal matter related to the official duties and responsibilities of the city 
270 council. The mayor or their designee shall appear before the city council and respond to the 
271 questions. The mayor or their designee shall not be required to answer questions relating to any 
272 other matter outside the scope of the matter noticed or be required to provide information the 
273 release of which is prohibited by law. The mayor may bring to this meeting any assistant, 
274 department head or other city officer or employee that the mayor may consider necessary to 
275 assist in responding to the questions posed by the city council. 
276  SECTION 2-11: GROUP PETITIONS 
277  The city council shall hold a public hearing and act with respect to every petition which is 
278 addressed to it and which is signed by at least 50 municipal voters as certified by the board of 
279 election commissioners. The hearing shall be held by the city council or by a committee or 
280 subcommittee thereof, and the city council shall act on the petition within 3 months of filing with 

15 of 50 281 the city clerk. Hearings on 2 or more petitions filed under this section may be held at the same 
282 time and place. At least 14 days before the hearing, the city clerk shall notify the 10 petitioners 
283 whose names first appear on each petition, publish a general summary of the subject matter of 
284 the petition, and post notice of the date and time of the public hearing. A hearing shall not be 
285 held upon any subject more than once in a 12-month period, as determined by the city council 
286 president.  
287  SECTION 2-12: FILLING OF VACANCIES 
288  (a) Councilor At-Large - When a vacancy exists for an at-large city council seat and more 
289 than 180 days remain in the term, the city clerk shall notify the city council and the chairperson 
290 of the board of election commissioners of the vacancy within 7 days. Within 7 days after 
291 notification, the chairperson of the board of election commissioners or a designee shall certify, in 
292 writing, to the city clerk that the defeated candidate for the office of councilor at-large with the 
293 next highest number of votes at the prior municipal election, if willing and able to serve, shall fill 
294 the remainder of the existing term. The city clerk shall administer the oath of office to the person 
295 within 15 days after certification and the person shall take the seat. If the person who is eligible 
296 declines the office, is not eligible and willing to serve, or fails to take the oath of office within 
297 the time period set forth in this section, then the person with the next highest number of votes for 
298 councilor at large at the prior municipal election who is eligible and willing to serve shall take 
299 the seat under the same circumstances as above. If no such candidate for the office can serve, the 
300 city council shall call a special election to fill the seat for the remainder of the term. If the 
301 vacancy occurs with 180 days or fewer in the existing term, the vacancy shall be filled as above; 
302 provided, however, if no candidate is available to take the seat, the seat shall remain vacant until 
303 the next regular municipal election, and the person elected shall take office upon certification of 

16 of 50 304 the vote, and serve for the remainder of the existing term as well as the term to which elected. 
305 The city clerk shall administer the oath of office to them at the next meeting of the city council. 
306  (b) Ward Councilor - If a vacancy occurs in the office of ward councilor and more than 
307 180 days remain on the existing term, the city council shall immediately order a special election 
308 to fill the vacancy for the remainder of the unexpired term. If a vacancy occurs 180 or fewer days 
309 before the end of the term, the seat shall remain vacant until the next regular municipal election 
310 for the office and the person elected shall fill the vacancy for the remainder of the unexpired 
311 term and the term to which elected. The city clerk shall administer the oath of office to the 
312 person at the next meeting of the city council. 
313  ARTICLE 3 
314  EXECUTIVE BRANCH 
315  SECTION 3-1: MAYOR: ROLE; TERM OF OFFICE; ELIGIBILITY 
316  (a) Role - The chief executive officer of the city shall be a mayor, elected by the 
317 municipal voters of the city at large. The role of mayor shall be a full-time position. 
318  (b) Term of Office - The term of office for mayor shall be 2 years, beginning on the first 
319 business day in January following the municipal election. 
320  (c) Eligibility - Any statewide voter residing in Somerville shall be eligible to hold the 
321 office of mayor. 
322  SECTION 3-2: PROHIBITIONS 

17 of 50 323  The mayor shall not hold another compensated city position or other elected public 
324 office. A former mayor shall not hold a compensated appointed city office or city employment 
325 for 1 year after termination of their service as the mayor. Any former mayor shall not receive 
326 compensation for contracted work authorized during their tenure as the mayor. This subsection 
327 shall not prevent a city officer or other city employee who has vacated a position to serve as the 
328 mayor from returning to the same office or other position of city employment held when the 
329 position was vacated. This prohibition shall not apply to persons covered by a leave of absence 
330 under section 37 of chapter 31 of the General Laws. 
331  SECTION 3-3: COMPENSATION 
332  The mayor shall receive compensation for their services as set by the city council by 
333 ordinance. An ordinance increasing or reducing the compensation of the mayor shall not be 
334 effective unless: 
335  (1) it is adopted by affirmative vote of 8 members of the city council; 
336  (2) it is adopted prior to the last 6 months of the mayor’s term; and 
337  (3) it provides that the compensation increase or reduction is to take effect upon the 
338 organization of the city government following the next regular municipal election. 
339  SECTION 3-4: EXECUTIVE POWERS 
340  (a) Executive Powers - The executive powers of the city shall be vested solely in the 
341 mayor and may be exercised by the mayor either personally or through the city agencies under 
342 the general supervision and control of the office of the mayor. The mayor shall cause this charter, 
343 laws, ordinances and other orders of the city government to be enforced and shall cause a record 

18 of 50 344 of all official acts of the executive branch of the city government to be kept. The mayor shall 
345 supervise, direct, and be responsible for the efficient administration of all city activities and 
346 functions placed under the control of the mayor by law or by this charter. The mayor or their 
347 designee shall have the sole power to sign, seal, execute and deliver in behalf of the city, deeds 
348 and leases of land sold or leased by the city, and other deeds, agreements, contracts, leases, 
349 indentures and assurances on behalf of the city. 
350  (b) Supervision of City Agencies - The mayor shall exercise general supervision and 
351 direction over all city agencies, unless otherwise provided by law or by this charter. Each city 
352 agency shall furnish to the mayor any information or materials the mayor may request and as the 
353 needs of the office of mayor and the interest of the city may require. The mayor shall be 
354 responsible for the efficient and effective coordination of the activities of all city agencies and 
355 may call together for consultation, conference and 
356  discussion, at reasonable times, all persons serving the city. 
357  (c) Multiple Member Bodies - The mayor shall be, by virtue of the office, a member of 
358 every appointed multiple member body of the city. The mayor may attend any meeting of an 
359 appointed multiple member body of the city, including executive sessions, to participate in the 
360 discussions of that body, provided, however, that the mayor shall not have the right to vote on 
361 any matter before a multiple member body which they serve on solely by virtue of their office. 
362  SECTION 3-5: APPOINTMENTS BY THE MAYOR 
363  (a) Department Heads - The mayor shall appoint, subject to confirmation by the city 
364 council under section 2-8(a), all department heads for whom no other method of appointment or 
365 selection is provided by this charter. Department heads serve at the discretion of the mayor 

19 of 50 366 subject to the limitations and requirements imposed by federal and state laws, rules or 
367 regulations. 
368  (b) City Attorney - The city attorney shall be appointed by the mayor, subject to 
369 confirmation by the city council under section 2-8(a). The city attorney shall be appointed to a 2-
370 year term. Whenever the mayor appoints or reappoints a city attorney the mayor shall submit the 
371 name of a person to serve as city attorney for confirmation under section 2-8(a), and the 
372 provisions of such 2-8 (a) shall apply to both an appointment and reappointment, provided 
373 however that a reappointment shall not be rejected except by negative vote of 8 members of the 
374 city council. If the mayor fails to submit the name of a person serving as the city attorney for 
375 confirmation of reappointment within 30 days of the expiration of the term, the city clerk shall 
376 place an item on the agenda for such reappointment at the next regular city council meeting. 
377  (c) Multiple Member Bodies - The mayor shall appoint, subject to confirmation by the 
378 city council under section 2-8(b), all members of multiple member bodies for whom no other 
379 method of appointment or selection is provided by administrative order or General Laws. All 
380 members shall serve terms as defined by administrative order or General Laws. Upon the 
381 expiration of the term of any member of a multiple member body, a successor shall be appointed 
382 in a like manner. The mayor shall fill any vacancy for the remainder of the unexpired term of any 
383 member of a multiple member body. 
384  (d) Police and Fire Employees - The mayor may appoint and promote, subject to 
385 confirmation by the city council, members and officers of the police department and fire 
386 department subject to requirements of the state’s Civil Service law. 

20 of 50 387  (e) City Employees - The method of appointment for all other city employees shall be as 
388 prescribed by administrative order. 
389  SECTION 3-6: APPOINTMENT OF CHIEF ADMINISTRATIVE OFFICER 
390  The mayor shall appoint a chief administrative officer to coordinate and direct the 
391 operations and functions of municipal government. The chief administrative officer shall be 
392 appointed by the mayor, subject to confirmation by the city council under section 2-8(a). The 
393 appointee shall be chosen on the basis of appropriate administrative and executive qualifications 
394 and shall have a combination of experience, training, or education to perform the duties of the 
395 office. Whenever the mayor appoints a chief administrative officer, the mayor shall submit the 
396 name of a person to serve as a chief administrative officer for confirmation under section 2-8(a). 
397  SECTION 3-7: TEMPORARY APPOINTMENTS TO CITY OFFICES 
398  Whenever a temporary or permanent vacancy occurs in a city office that is appointed

510 employee who has vacated a position in order to serve as a member of the school committee 
511 elected by ward from returning to the same office or other position of city employment held at

518  (2) it is adopted prior to the last 6 months of the school committee’s term; and

520 government following the next regular municipal election.

584  FINANCIAL PROCEDURES 
585  SECTION 6-1: FISCAL YEAR 
586  The fiscal year of the city shall begin on July 1 and end on June 30, unless another period 
587 is required by the General Laws. 
588  SECTION 6-2: COMMUNITY AND COUNCIL BUDGET INPUT 
589  The city council shall hold a community budget hearing on or before February 15 of each

591 community budget hearing 14 days in advance of the hearing. The mayor shall, at the first 
592 regular meeting of the city council of each year, solicit budget priorities from all city councilors. 
593  SECTION 6-3: ANNUAL BUDGET MEETING 
594  On or before March 1 of each year, the mayor shall call a joint meeting of the city council 
595 and school committee, including the superintendent of schools, to review the financial condition 
596 of the city, revenue and expenditure forecasts for at least 3 years and other relevant information 
597 prepared by the mayor in order to develop a coordinated budget. 

30 of 50 598  SECTION 6-4: SUBMISSION OF OPERATING BUDGET; BUDGET MESSAGE 
599  On or about May 30, the mayor shall submit to the city council a proposed operating 
600 budget for all city agencies for the next fiscal year. The proposed operating budget shall include 
601 the school budget, as adopted by the school committee, which shall be submitted to the mayor on 
602 or about May 15. The proposed operating budget shall be accompanied by a budget message and 
603 supporting documents. The budget message shall explain the operating budget in fiscal terms and 
604 in terms of work programs for all city agencies. It shall outline the proposed fiscal policies of the 
605 city for the next fiscal year, describe important features of the proposed operating budget and 
606 include any major variations from the current operating budget, fiscal policies, revenues and 
607 expenditures together with reasons for these changes. The proposed operating budget shall 
608 provide a complete fiscal plan of all city funds and activities and shall be in the form the mayor 
609 deems desirable, provided, however, that the budget proposals relative to elected officials shall 
610 identify the cost of compensation and the cost of benefits for such officials. The mayor and the 
611 superintendent of schools shall coordinate the dates and times of the school committee's budget 
612 process under the General Laws. 
613  SECTION 6-5: ACTION ON THE OPERATING BUDGET 
614  (a) Public Hearing - The city council shall post a notice of the proposed operating budget 
615 as submitted by the mayor. The notice shall state: (1) the times and places where copies of the 
616 entire proposed operating budget are available for inspection by the public; and (2) the date, time 
617 and place when a public hearing on the proposed operating budget shall be held by the city 
618 council, at least 7 days after posting of the notice. The city council shall not act on the budget 
619 until after the public hearing has occurred. 

31 of 50 620  (b) Adoption of the Budget - The city council shall take action on the proposed operating 
621 budget, which may have amendments, before the end of the fiscal year. In amending the 
622 proposed operating budget, the city council may delete or decrease amounts except expenditures 
623 required by law, provided, however, that the city council shall not increase any specific item or 
624 the total of the proposed operating budget except on the recommendation of the mayor or unless 
625 otherwise authorized by the General Laws. If the city council fails to act on an item in the 
626 proposed operating budget prior to the beginning of the fiscal year, that amount shall, without 
627 any action by the city council, become a part of the appropriations for the year and be available 
628 for the purposes specified. 
629  (c) Availability of the Operating Budget - In addition to any other posting requirements, 
630 immediately after the submission of the proposed budget to the city council, the mayor or their 
631 designee shall post the entire budget document on the city website, where it shall remain posted 
632 during the city council review process contained in this article. After the enactment of the budget 
633 the mayor or their designee shall post the final budget on the city website where it shall remain 
634 throughout the fiscal year for which it is in effect. The final budget shall reflect any amendments 
635 made by the city council and approved by the mayor and shall indicate that it is the final budget. 
636  SECTION 6-6: CAPITAL IMPROVEMENT PROGRAM 
637  (a) Submission - The mayor shall submit a capital improvement program to the city 
638 council on or about November 1 of each year. The mayor shall annually revise the information

641  The capital improvement program shall include:

647  (4) the estimated annual cost of operating and maintaining each           facility and piece

652  (2) the date, time, and place of a public hearing on the plan to be held by the city council

661 city in the form of an Annual Comprehensive Financial Report to be conducted by a certified 

33 of 50 662 public accountant or a firm of certified public accountants, which has no personal interest, direct

676 intentionally expend in a fiscal year sums in excess of the appropriations, awards, grants or gifts

34 of 50 684  SECTION 7-1: PRELIMINARY ELECTIONS

699  (2) At-Large City Councilor - at least 100 certified signatures;

35 of 50 704  (b) Forms - Signatures of municipal voters shall be made on a form prescribed by the

721  (2) Offices of City Councilor At-Large - the 8 people who receive the highest number of

36 of 50 726  (3) Preliminary Results; Tied Vote - If there is a tie among candidates for the last

731  (e) Condition Making Preliminary Unnecessary - If the time for filing statements of 
732 candidates to be on the ballot for any preliminary election has expired, and the number of 
733 statements filed with the city clerk for an office is not more than 2 for the office of mayor, any

736 candidates shall be voted on for the office at the regular or special municipal election. The city

738 nomination to the office shall not be made, and a preliminary election shall not be held for the

839  SECTION 8-10: OATHS OR AFFIRMATIONS 
840  (a) Officials Elected in Regular Municipal Elections - On the first business day in January 
841 of each even-numbered year, the city council members-elect, the school committee members-
842 elect and the mayor-elect, shall meet and take an oath or affirmation to faithfully discharge the 
843 duties of their office. The oath or affirmation shall be administered by the city clerk, the assistant 
844 city clerk, a judge of a court of record or by a justice of the peace. Each official taking the oath 
845 or affirmation shall also sign a written version, which shall be kept in a bound book maintained 
846 by the city clerk. If the mayor-elect or any member-elect of the city council or school committee 
847 is absent on the day the oath is administered, the oath or affirmation shall be administered at any 
848 time within 10 days of the first business day of January, unless circumstances beyond the control 
849 of the officer-elect prevent such action, but in no instance shall the time period to be sworn to 
850 office extend beyond 30 days from the first business day of January.  

42 of 50 851  (b) Other Elected Officials and Appointed Positions - Except as otherwise provided by 
852 law, every person who is elected or appointed to an office or as a member of a multiple member 
853 body shall take an oath or affirmation before performing any act under this election or 
854 appointment. A record of this oath or affirmation shall be kept by the city clerk. 
855  SECTION 8-11: LIMITATION ON OFFICE HOLDING 
856  Unless otherwise allowed by law or this charter, a person shall not simultaneously hold 
857 more than 1 office or position of employment with the city. This section may be waived by the 
858 mayor by filing a notice of the waiver with an explanation and justification with the city clerk. 
859  SECTION 8-12: FELONY CONVICTION 
860  An elected official who has been convicted of a state or federal felony while holding 
861 office shall be deemed to have vacated the office. 
862  SECTION 8-13: ENFORCEMENT OF CHARTER PROVISIONS 
863  It shall be the duty of the mayor to see that this charter is faithfully followed and 
864 complied with by all city agencies and employees. Whenever it appears to the mayor that a city 
865 agency or employee is not following this charter, the mayor shall, in writing, cause notice to be 
866 given to that agency or employee directing compliance with this charter. Whenever it appears to 
867 the city council that the mayor is not following this charter, the city council shall, by resolution, 
868 direct the attention of the mayor to those areas in which it believes there is a failure to comply 
869 with this charter. The procedures made available in chapter 231A of the General Laws may be 
870 used to determine the rights, duties, or other legal relations arising under this charter, including
