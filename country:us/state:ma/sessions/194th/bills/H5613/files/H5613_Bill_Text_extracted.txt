2 116K the following section:- 
3  Section 116L. (a) The municipal police training committee, in consultation with the 
4 executive office of aging and independence, the Alzheimer’s Disease and Related Disorders 
5 Association - West, the Massachusetts Coalition of Police, Inc., Massachusetts Chiefs of Police 
6 Association and the Massachusetts Police Association, Inc., shall establish and implement a

16  (i) dementia and symptoms associated with dementia; 
17  (ii) communication issues, including how to communicate respectfully and effectively 
18 with the individual who has dementia to determine the most appropriate response and effective 
19 communication techniques to enhance collaboration with caregivers; 
20  (iii) techniques for understanding and approaching behavioral symptoms and identifying 
21 alternatives to physical restraints; 
22  (iv) identifying and reporting incidents of abuse, neglect and exploitation to the 
23 protective services system established in section 16 of chapter 19A; 
24  (v) protocols for contacting caregivers when a person with dementia is found wandering,

27  (c) All law enforcement officers shall complete not less than 1 hour of biannual in-service

29 minimum credit hours required for in-service education.

144  SECTION 6. Said chapter 111 is hereby further amended by inserting after section 53H 
145 the following section:- 
146  Section 53I. (a) The department shall require acute care hospitals to allow a family 
147 member or other caregiver for patients with Alzheimer’s disease or other dementias, or for 
148 patients exhibiting symptoms of dementia or cognitive impairment, or a patient who presents 
149 with symptoms of dementia or cognitive impairment based on the assessment of the physician 
150 overseeing their care in the hospital, to remain with the patient at all times that are medically 
151 appropriate, including, but not limited to, while in the emergency department and while admitted 
152 as an inpatient. Caregivers for patients living with Alzheimer’s disease or other dementias shall 
153 not be required to adhere to restricted hospital visiting hours, unless it has been deemed unsafe 
154 for the patient, family member or caregiver. 
155  (b) The department shall require acute care hospitals to create policies and protocols to 
156 ensure that a family member, caregiver or personal legal representative responsible for a patient 
157 with Alzheimer’s disease or other dementia or a patient who presents with symptoms of 
158 dementia or cognitive impairment based on the assessment of the physician overseeing their care 

9 of 16 159 in the hospital is contacted as soon as possible following admission to the emergency department 
160 or hospital if the patient presents to the hospital without a family member, caregiver or personal 
161 legal representative; provided, however, that the hospital shall only contact a family member, 
162 caregiver or personal legal representative: (i) if the hospital has received consent from the 
163 patient, if possible, to do so; and (ii) to the extent consistent with federal and state law or 
164 regulation, and in the reasonable judgment of the hospital. If the patient is incapacitated or not 
165 able to provide consent, a health care provider may share the patient’s information with a family 
166 member, caregiver or personal legal representative responsible for a patient with Alzheimer’s 
167 disease or other dementia as long as the health care provider determines, based on their 
168 professional judgment, that it is in the best interest of the patient.  
169  (c) The department shall require acute care hospitals to create policies and protocols to 
170 ensure that a family member, caregiver or personal legal representative responsible for a patient 
171 living with Alzheimer’s disease or other dementia or a patient who presents with symptoms of 
172 dementia or cognitive impairment based on the assessment of the physician overseeing their care 
173 in the hospital is contacted prior to the patient’s discharge to ensure a safe discharge, including 
174 suitable transport from the hospital, and review the discharge plan; provided, however, that the 
175 hospital shall only contact a family member, caregiver or personal legal representative: (i) if the 
176 hospital has received consent from the patient, if possible, to do so; and (ii) to the extent 
177 consistent with federal and state law or regulation, and in the reasonable judgment of the 
178 hospital. If the patient is incapacitated or not able to provide consent, a health care provider may 
179 contact a family member, caregiver or personal legal representative responsible for the patient 
180 with Alzheimer’s disease or other dementia as long as the health care provider determines, based 
181 on their professional judgment, that it is in the best interest of the patient. If a family member, 

10 of 16 182 caregiver or personal legal representative is not able to be contacted or if the patient declines 
183 contact, the patient living with Alzheimer’s disease or other dementia shall meet with a hospital 
184 social worker or other professional who can assess for discharge safety and other supports 
185 needed prior to discharge. 
186  (d) Notwithstanding subsections (a) to (c), inclusive, this section shall not apply during a 
187 public health emergency or state of emergency declared by the governor relevant to the actions 
188 of the hospital. 
189  (e) The department shall promulgate regulations to implement this section.

196  SECTION 8. Said chapter 111 is hereby further amended by adding the following 3 
197 sections:- 
198  Section 250. (a) The department, in consultation with the executive office of aging and 
199 independence, the advisory council on Alzheimer’s disease research and treatment established 
200 pursuant to subsection (b) of section 16AA of chapter 6A and any additional stakeholders as 
201 determined by the department, shall develop a public awareness campaign on brain health, 
202 Alzheimer’s disease and other dementias. The department shall incorporate the public awareness 
203 campaign into existing and relevant public health outreach programs. 

11 of 16 204  (b)(1) The public awareness campaign on brain health, Alzheimer’s disease and other 
205 dementias shall: 
206  (i) educate health care providers on: (A) the importance of early detection and timely 
207 diagnosis of cognitive impairment; (B) validated cognitive assessment tools; (C) current and 
208 emerging treatment options; (D) the value of a Medicare Annual Wellness visit for cognitive 
209 health; and (E) the Medicare and Medicaid care planning billing codes for individuals with 
210 cognitive impairment;  
211  (ii) increase public understanding and awareness of: (A) early warning signs of 
212 Alzheimer’s disease and other types of dementia; (B) the value of early detection and diagnosis; 
213 and (C) how to reduce the risk of cognitive decline, particularly among persons in diverse 
214 communities who are at greater risk of developing Alzheimer’s disease and other types of 
215 dementia; and  
216  (iii) inform health care professionals and the general public of: (A) dementia care 
217 coordination services for those living with Alzheimer’s disease and other dementias; and (B) 
218 other resources and services available to individuals living with dementia and their families and 
219 caregivers. 
220  (2) For the materials for the general public, the department shall provide uniform and 
221 consistent guidance on Alzheimer’s disease and other dementia in nonclinical terms. The 
222 department shall: (i) ensure cultural relevancy and health literacy in developing materials; and 
223 (ii) provide the information to diverse populations who are at higher risk for developing 
224 dementia.  

12 of 16 225  (c) The department shall include the federal Centers for Disease Control and Prevention’s 
226 Healthy Aging Program’s modules on subjective cognitive decline and caregiving in the annual 
227 behavioral risk factor surveillance system survey on a rotating annual basis to collect prevalence 
228 data on Alzheimer’s disease and other dementias, track trends over time and analyze data to 
229 direct public health programs and resources. 
230  Section 251. As used in this section, “Healthy Brain Initiative Road Map” shall mean the 
231 federal Centers for Disease Control and Prevention’s collaborative approach to fully integrate 
232 cognitive health into public health practice and reduce the risk and impact of Alzheimer’s disease 
233 and other dementias. 
234  Biannually, the department shall report on the department’s work on the Healthy Brain 
235 Initiative Road Map. The department shall submit the report to the clerks of the house of 
236 representatives and the senate, the joint committee on public health and to the advisory council 
237 on Alzheimer’s disease research and treatment established pursuant to subsection (b) of section 
238 16AA of chapter 6A. 
239  Section 252. (a) Not less than every 5 years, each hospital licensed pursuant to section 51 
240 shall complete an operational plan for the recognition and management of patients with dementia 
241 or delirium in acute-care settings and submit the plan to the department for approval. Not later 
242 than 90 days of receipt, the department shall review the plan and approve the plan or offer 
243 amendments to the plan. Upon final approval of the plan by the department, the hospital shall 
244 implement the plan.  
245  (b) The operational plan shall include: (i) education and training of clinical and non-
246 clinical staff; (ii) providing a dementia- and delirium-appropriate environment; (iii) recognition 

13 of 16 247 of dementia and delirium; (iv) patient management and treatment, including, but not limited to, 
248 how to manage symptoms, treatment protocols and side effect management; (v) transition 
249 planning to improve and provide safe admissions, transfers and discharges, including, but not 
250 limited to, protocols to ensure that patients living with dementia are safe and have a staff 
251 member or caregiver present during discharge or transfer; (vi) advance care planning 
252 information; (vii) caregiver communication and coordination, including, but not limited to, 
253 protocols to ensure that contact to a caregiver has been attempted upon arrival and prior to 
254 discharge if the patient agrees; and (viii) additional recommendations made by the Alzheimer’s 
255 and related dementias acute care advisory committee established pursuant to section 1 of chapter 
256 228 of the acts of 2014 and any additional guidance issued by the Massachusetts Health and 
257 Hospital Association, Inc. or the department. 
258  (c) Each hospital may update the operational plan more frequently as needed. 
259  (d) Each hospital shall provide a copy of its operational plan to the advisory council on 
260 Alzheimer’s disease research and treatment established pursuant to subsection (b) of section 
261 16AA of chapter 6A upon approval. 
262  (e) Each hospital shall provide an electronic copy of its operational plan to each 
263 employee upon approval by the department. Upon request by an employee the hospital shall 
264 provide a printed copy of the plan to its employees, 
265  (f) Each hospital shall keep a copy of the operational plan on file and shall make it 
266 available for review by the public by posting it to the hospital’s website.
