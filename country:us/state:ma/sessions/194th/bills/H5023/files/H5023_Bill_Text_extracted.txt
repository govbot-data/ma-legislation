An Act relative to sexual harassment workplace training.
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives in General Court assembled, and by the authority
of the same, as follows:
1 Chapter 151B of the General Laws, as appearing in the 2022 Official Edition, is hereby
2 amended by striking out Section 3A and inserting in place thereof the following section:-
3 Section 3A: Employers' policies against harassment; preparation of model policy;
4 education and training programs
5 (a) All employers, employment agencies and labor organizations shall promote a
6 workplace free of all forms of unlawful harassment, including sexual harassment.
7 Notwithstanding any other provision of this section, the requirements of subsections (b) through
8 (e) shall not apply to employers that employ twenty-five (25) or fewer employees.
9 (b) Every employer shall:
10 (1) adopt a policy against unlawful harassment, including sexual harassment, which shall
11 include:
1 of 5

12 (i) a statement that all forms of unlawful harassment, including sexual harassment, in the
13 workplace are unlawful and will not be tolerated, and that explains that it is unlawful for a person
14 to be subject to harassment because of the person’s sex or any other characteristic protected by
15 section 4 of this chapter;
16 (ii) a statement that it is unlawful to retaliate against an employee for filing a complaint
17 of unlawful harassment, including sexual harassment, or for cooperating in an investigation of a
18 complaint for unlawful harassment;
19 (iii) a description and examples of unlawful harassment, including sexual harassment;
20 (iv) a statement of the range of consequences for employees who are found to have
21 committed unlawful harassment, including sexual harassment;
22 (v) a description of the process for filing internal complaints about harassment and the
23 contact information of the person or persons to whom complaints should be made; and
24 (vi) the identity of the appropriate state and federal employment discrimination
25 enforcement agencies, and directions as to how to contact such agencies.
26 (2) provide to all employees an individual written copy of the employer's policy against
27 unlawful harassment by January 1, 2027; provided, however, that an existing employee that is
28 changing positions shall be provided a copy at such time of the change in position; provided
29 further that a new employee shall be provided such a copy at the time of hire.
30 (c) The commission shall prepare and make available to employers subject to this section
31 a model policy and poster consistent with federal and state statutes and regulations, which may
32 be used by employers for the purposes of this section.
2 of 5

33 (d) An employer's failure to provide the information required to be provided by this
34 section shall not, in and of itself, result in the liability of said employer to any current or former
35 employee or applicant in any action alleging unlawful harassment. An employer's compliance
36 with the notice requirements of this section shall not, in and of itself, protect the employer from
37 liability for unlawful harassment of any current or former employee or applicant.
38 (e) Except as provided herein, this subsection shall not apply to employers that employ
39 twenty-five or fewer employees. Employers and labor organizations are required to provide
40 employees with training to prevent unlawful harassment, including sexual harassment, within six
41 months after the employee is hired, promoted within the employer. An employer that does not
42 use the model training developed by the Massachusetts commission against discrimination must
43 ensure that the training that they use meets or exceeds the following minimum standards.
44 The training must:
45 (1) be focused on compliance with the legal requirements of employment non-
46 discrimination law and on preventing unlawful harassment, including sexual harassment, in the
47 workplace;
48 (2) include an interactive component, whether in-person, on-line, or remote; provided that
49 trainings are led by one or more qualified trainers which allows participants to ask questions and
50 receive answers in a timely fashion; and in the case of on-line trainings, the online program must
51 allow participants to ask questions anonymously about the topics covered in the training and
52 receive responses from the trainer(s) within two business days;
53 (3) be a minimum of one hour every year;
3 of 5

54 (4) include an explanation and examples of unlawful harassment and unlawful retaliation
55 consistent with guidance issued by the Massachusetts commission against discrimination;
56 provided, however, that for employers with 100 or more employees, the examples and scenarios
57 should be tailored to the specific type of workplace or industry;
58 (5) include information on internal and external remedies available to victims of
59 harassment;
60 (6) include bystander intervention training consistent with guidance issued by the
61 Massachusetts commission against discrimination; and
62 (7) include information on the responsibilities of supervisory and managerial employees
63 to address unlawful harassment and unlawful retaliation.
64 (8) include, in the case of on-line training, standards and requirements that demonstrate
65 the active participation of the employee in the training, such as showing time spent watching
66 videos, answering questions and otherwise, engaging in the program. On-line trainings shall also
67 include the ability of employees to ask anonymous questions about the topic and receive
68 responses from the trainer(s) within two business days. The employer shall maintain a record of
69 the employee questions asked and the responses provided for a minimum of five (5) years, while
70 preserving the anonymity of the employees.
71 Each employee must receive training on an annual basis, starting January 1, 2027. An
72 employer’s or labor organization’s compliance with the training requirements of this section
73 shall not, in and of itself, protect the employer or labor organization from liability for unlawful
74 harassment of any current or former employee or applicant. All employers shall keep a record of
75 their employees’ completion of all trainings required by this subsection (e). Such records may be
4 of 5

76 electronic. Employers shall maintain such records for at least 5 years and must be made available
77 to the Attorney General or the Massachusetts Commission Against Discrimination on request.
78 (f) The Attorney General may promulgate any rules, regulations or guidelines that are
79 necessary and appropriate to effectuate the purposes of this section.
