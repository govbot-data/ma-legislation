The budget language requires information on the number of vouchers and costs of vouchers by
municipality and county. We have attached the information we currently have, but the data is somewhat
limited. We currently have data for roughly 94% and 93% of MRVP and AHVP vouchers, respectively.
We are actively working to add data from the remaining housing agencies by coordinating with their
software vendors to set up data transmissions to the State Oversight of Housing Assistance (SOHA) data
warehouse.
For information regarding waiting lists, voucher issuance timing, and extensions, the Executive Office of
Housing and Livable Communities (EOHLC) utilizes reports from the Common Housing Application for
Massachusetts Programs (CHAMP). EOHLC’s Division of Public Housing launched CHAMP to the
public in April 2019 and has been successful in making the application process more accessible for state-
aided public housing, as well as providing EOHLC with important data on applicants and the tenant
selection process. EOHLC added AHVP into CHAMP in October 2020 and added MRVP in September
2023.
In FY2025, 193 mobile MRVP vouchers were issued through CHAMP. Mobile MRVP voucher holders
took an average of 92 days from voucher issuance to lease start date. Nineteen (19) mobile MRVP voucher

holders did not find a unit before the voucher expiration date. None of these voucher holders received an
extension. Currently, 78 mobile MRVP vouchers are distributed, with voucher holders searching for or
waiting approval on a unit. In FY2025, 68 MRVP project-based vouchers, which are vouchers assigned
to a specific unit, were issued through CHAMP. Note that most of the waiting lists for MRVP project-
based vouchers are held by individual property owners, so EOHLC cannot track issuance through CHAMP
or another centralized system.
In FY2025, 66 mobile AHVP vouchers were issued through CHAMP. Mobile AHVP voucher holders
took an average of 68 days from voucher issuance to lease start date. Seven (7) mobile AHVP voucher
holders did not find a unit before the voucher expiration date. None of these voucher holders received an
extension. Currently, 8 mobile AHVP vouchers are distributed via CHAMP, with holders searching for
or waiting approval on a unit.
It is important to note EOHLC paused mobile voucher issuance in January 2025. That pause is ongoing.
Additional MRVP and AHVP mobile vouchers were issued outside of CHAMP in FY2025. For MRVP
most of these vouchers were issued to project-based voucher households who needed to move for health
or safety reasons. EOHLC has partnered with MassHealth’s Money Follows the Person program and set
aside 100 mobile AHVP vouchers. To date, over 100 applications have been submitted, over 100 vouchers
have been issued (some were not used for a variety of reasons), 40 households are in housing search, and
60 households have leased units. Of those households issued a voucher, 67 received one automatic
extension, 27 received a second extension, and seven have received a third extension. All extensions after
the first one were due to a reasonable accommodation.
EOHLC can report on the number of unique applicant households in CHAMP. As of November 4, 2025,
there are 215,357 unique households in CHAMP that have applied for an MRVP voucher, and there are
90,600 unique households in CHAMP that have applied for an AHVP voucher. These applicant totals
include both the 4,494 applicants who applied to MRVP and AHVP, and 60,684 applicants that have
applied for MRVP, AHVP, and public housing. While the budget language requests that EOHLC report
on families, CHAMP counts households, which may be families or single individuals. CHAMP is also
not the only place one can apply for an MRVP voucher. Many waiting lists for project-based MRVP
vouchers are maintained by the property owner. EOHLC has no access to or insight into these waiting
lists.
EOHLC has taken two important actions to reduce the wait time for households to lease up their voucher.
First, for both AHVP and MRVP, EOHLC has raised mobile voucher subsidy limits to better reflect the
housing market and aligned rents with 110% of Small Area Fair Market Rents (SAFMR) effective March
1, 2024. SAFMR sets rents at the ZIP code level, instead of at an area-wide level. This means that rents
will better reflect market rents in a specific neighborhood, allowing voucher participants greater choice
when deciding where to live. Second, effective with leases beginning September 1, 2023, MRVP can
cover security deposits, and AHVP can cover security deposits, first month’s rent, and last month’s rent.
EOHLC has found that these start-up costs can be a burden to households. By applying a voucher to cover
these costs, households can more easily move into a unit.
Lastly, the budget language requests information on project-based vouchers. See attached for a chart of
project-based vouchers awarded in FY2025 by program. For both MRVP and AHVP, all units with a
2

project-based voucher attached are affordable, the program income limit is 80% AMI, and total rental cost
to the household is 25% or 30% of net income.
Should you have any questions regarding this data, please do not hesitate to contact Seth Nadeau, Director
of Legislative Affairs, at seth.nadeau@mass.gov.
