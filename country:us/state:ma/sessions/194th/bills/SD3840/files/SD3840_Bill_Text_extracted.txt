Programs Division 
Pathways to Recovery- Pathways to Recovery is a four-month substance 
use treatment program available to both sentenced and pretrial populations. 
The program utilizes an evidence-based, trauma-informed approach to 
address substance use disorders and support behavioral change. Oversight is 
provided by an independent licensed clinician, who supervises program staff 
and ensures the integrity and effectiveness of the curriculum through ongoing 
clinical guidance and review. 
Program Manager Alexis Flaherty MS CHW 
• Unit DN1 Sentenced program: 13 were admitted to the program, 2 non­
disciplinary discharges, 0 disciplinary discharges, and O graduated the 
program. The average number of participants each month for the 
quarter was 9. 
• Unit FS1 Trial program -22 were admitted to the program, 3 disciplinary 
discharges, 14 non-disciplinary discharges, and O completed the 
program. Non-disciplinary discharges are released at court, bailed out, 
and programs placed. Average number of participants each month for 
the quarter is 17 
• Narcan/Harm Reduction kits were offered to all releases, 40 were 
accepted. 
• Number of Narcan Boxes dispended from our Lobby machines 19 
• AA and Refuge Recovery volunteers held groups in both sentenced and 
trial programs 
• Brockton Neighborhood Health Center provides a Recovery Coach to 
offer a Relapse Prevention class and community outreach unit FS1. 
• Plymouth Recovery Center proves Relapse prevention and community 
outreach/resources in the DN1 Program. 

Conflict Resolution: A comprehensive 4-month conative behavioral program 
designed to address Criminal behavior, anger management, conflict, and 
victim impact. The objective is to help identify patterns, choices, and identify 
thinking errors. The goal is to assist them in finding alternative behaviors, 
healthy responses, emotional regulation, and reducing consequences. 
Program Manager Sandra Crispin. 
• 16 new participants began the program this quarter, and O graduated. 
They will graduate next month. 1 program failure for discipline, 6 non­
disciplinary discharges. The average number of participants each 
month in the program was 11 
Making Changes Program: 3-month Reentry preparation and life skills-based 
program. Ideally participants join this program 6 to 9 months prior to their 
release. The goal is discharging planning and identifying community 
resources to support each individual reentry plan and needs. 
Reentry staff coordinator Mollie Simmons. 
• 16 participants began the program, graduated from the program, 3 
program failures for disciplinary reasons, 9 discharged non-disciplinary. 
The average number of participants each month in the program for the 
quarter is 12.
