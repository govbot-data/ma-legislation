An Act to protect consumers from contributing to inhumane animal testing for cosmetics. 
 
 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives in General Court assembled, and by the authority 
of the same, as follows: 
 
1  SECTION 1. Chapter 140 of the General Laws, as appearing in the 2022 Official Edition, 
2 is hereby further amended by inserting after Section 174H the following new section: 
3  174I. Selling of animal tested cosmetics. 
4  1. For the purposes of this section the following terms shall have the following meanings: 
5  (a) "Cosmetic", shall mean articles intended to be rubbed, brushed, poured, sprinkled, or 
6 sprayed on, introduced into, or otherwise applied to the human body or any part thereof for 
7 cleaning or for enhancing, concealing or changing the appearance, including but not limited to 
8 personal products such as deodorant, shampoo and conditioner. 
9  (b) "Animal testing", shall mean the internal or external application of a cosmetic, either 
10 in its final form or any ingredient thereof, to the skin, eyes, or other body part of a live non-
11 human vertebrate. 
12  (c) "Cosmetic Ingredient", shall have the same meaning as defined in 21 CFR 700.3(e). 

2 of 4 13  (d) "Cosmetic Manufacturer", shall mean any person whose name appears on the label of 
14 a cosmetic pursuant to the requirements of 21 CFR 701.12. 
15  (e) "Supplier", shall mean any entity that supplies, directly or through a third party, any 
16 ingredient used by a cosmetic manufacturer in the formulation of a cosmetic. 
17  2. Except as otherwise provided in this section, it shall be unlawful for a cosmetic 
18 manufacturer to import for profit, sell or offer for sale in the state, any cosmetic which the 
19 cosmetic manufacturer knew or reasonably should have known that animal testing was 
20 conducted or contracted by or on behalf of the cosmetic manufacturer or any supplier of the 
21 cosmetic manufacturer if the animal testing was conducted after the effective date of this section. 
22 No provision of this subsection shall be construed to apply to a test method conducted for the 
23 purposes of medical research. 
24  3. This section does not apply to animal testing that is conducted: 
25  (a) As a requirement of any federal or state regulatory agency if: 
26  (i) the cosmetic or a cosmetic ingredient in the cosmetic which is being tested is in wide 
27 use and cannot be replaced by another ingredient which is capable of performing a similar 
28 function; and 
29  (ii) a specific human health problem relating to the cosmetic or cosmetic ingredient is 
30 substantiated and the need to conduct animal testing is justified and supported by a detailed 
31 protocol for research that is proposed as the basis for the evaluation of the cosmetic or cosmetic 
32 ingredient; and 

3 of 4 33  (iii) there does not exist a method of testing other than animal testing that is accepted for 
34 the relevant purpose by a federal or state regulatory agency. 
35  (b) As a requirement of any regulatory agency of a foreign jurisdiction, if no evidence 
36 derived from such testing was relied upon to substantiate the safety of a cosmetic sold within 
37 Massachusetts by the cosmetic manufacturer. 
38  (c) For any product or cosmetic ingredient which is subject to the requirements under 21 
39 USC subchapter V. 
40  (d) For purposes not related to cosmetics as required by any federal, state or foreign 
41 regulatory agency, provided that no evidence derived from such testing was relied upon to 
42 substantiate the safety of a cosmetic sold within the state by the cosmetic manufacturer, unless: 
43  (i) documentary evidence exists that the intent of the animal testing was unrelated to 
44 cosmetics; and 
45  (ii) there is a history of the use of the cosmetic ingredient unrelated to cosmetics for a 
46 minimum of twelve months. 
47  4. This section does not apply to a cosmetic: 
48  (a) If, in its final form, such cosmetic was tested on animals before the effective date of 
49 this section, even if the cosmetic is manufactured on or after such date. 
50  (b) If a cosmetic ingredient contained in such cosmetic was tested on animals and sold in 
51 Massachusetts before the effective date of this section, even if such cosmetic ingredient is 
52 manufactured on or after such date. 

4 of 4 53  5. This section may not be construed to prevent a cosmetic manufacturer from reviewing, 
54 assessing or retaining data resulting from exempted animal testing or from past animal testing 
55 results. 
56  6. No municipality or other political subdivision of Massachusetts may establish or 
57 continue any prohibition on or relating to animal testing for cosmetics, as defined in this section 
58 that is not identical to the prohibitions established in this section. 
59  7. The attorney general may, upon a determination that there is a reasonable likelihood of 
60 a violation of this section, review any testing data on which a cosmetic manufacturer has relied 
61 in determining the safety of a cosmetic or a cosmetic ingredient sold in Massachusetts. Any 
62 information related to a trade secret, as defined in section 42 of chapter 93 or 18 U.S.C. § 
63 1839(3) and per Fair Packaging and Labeling Program, 15 U.S.C. § 1454(c)(3), disclosed under 
64 this section shall be protected and the attorney general shall enter into a protective order with the 
65 cosmetic manufacturer before receipt of such information. The attorney general shall take other 
66 appropriate measures as necessary to preserve the confidentiality of the information produced 
67 pursuant to this section. The attorney general may bring an action or special proceeding in the 
68 supreme court for a judgment enjoining the continuance of such violation and for a civil penalty 
69 of not more than five thousand dollars for the first violation and not more than one thousand 
70 dollars per day if the violation continues. 
71  SECTION 2. This shall take effect 6 months after the passage of this act.
