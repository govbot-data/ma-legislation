An Act establishing Medicare for All in Massachusetts. 
 
 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives in General Court assembled, and by the authority 
of the same, as follows: 
 
1  SECTION 1. (a) There shall be a special commission on universal health care 
2 implementation to: (i) conduct an investigation and study single-payer models, assess costs, and 
3 analyze the feasibility of establishing a single-payer health care financing system in the 
4 commonwealth; (ii) develop and issue recommendations based on its findings relative to the 
5 implementation of a single-payer health care system or how best to achieve universal health care 
6 in the commonwealth, and the proposed health care financing of said system; and (iii) ensure 
7 equitable access to quality health care services for all residents of all identities to support health 
8 care as a human right, without discrimination based on age, financial or employment status, 
9 ethnicity, race, religion, gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, marital status, 
10 national origin, disability, previous health conditions or diagnoses, and geographic location. 
11  (b) As used in this section, “single-payer health care financing system” means a universal 
12 system used by the state to pay the cost of health care services and goods in which: 

2 of 5 13  (i) Institutional providers are paid directly for health care services or goods by the state or 
14 paid by an administrator that does not bear risk in contracting with the state; 
15  (ii) Institutional providers are paid with global budgets that separate capital budgets, 
16 established through regional planning, and operational budgets; 
17  (iii) Group practices are paid directly for health care services or goods by the state, by an 
18 administrator that does not bear risk in contracting with the state, by the employer of the group 
19 practice or by an institutional provider; and 
20  (iv) Individual health care providers are paid directly for health care services or goods by 
21 the state, by their employers, by an administrator that does not bear risk in contracting with the 
22 state, by an institutional provider or by a group practice. 
23  (c) The commission shall consist of: the executive director of the health policy 
24 commission or a designee, who shall serve as chair; the secretary of health and human services 
25 or a designee; the secretary of administration and finance or a designee; the secretary of labor 
26 and workforce development or a designee; the commissioner of revenue or a designee; the 
27 commissioner of insurance or a designee; the executive director of the center for health 
28 information and analysis or a designee; the chairs of the joint committee on health care financing 
29 or their designees; the assistant secretary for MassHealth or a designee; the executive director of 
30 the Massachusetts Health Connector or a designee; the executive director of the Massachusetts 
31 Campaign for Single Payer Healthcare (Mass-Care); and 19 members to be appointed by the 
32 governor, 1 of whom shall be a member of the Massachusetts Biotechnology Council; 1 of whom 
33 shall be a member of the AFL-CIO; 1 of whom shall be a member of the Massachusetts Health & 
34 Hospital Association; 1 of whom shall be a member of the Massachusetts Association of Health 

3 of 5 35 Plans, Inc.; 1 of whom shall be a member of the Massachusetts Nurses Association; 1 of whom 
36 shall be a member of the Associated Industries of Massachusetts (AIM); 1 of whom shall be a 
37 member of the Retailers Association of Massachusetts, Inc.; 1 of whom shall be a member of the 
38 Blue Cross Blue Shield of Massachusetts, Inc.; 1 of whom shall be a member of the 
39 Massachusetts League of Community Health Centers; 1 of whom shall be a member of Health 
40 Care for All, Inc.; 1 of whom shall be a member of the Massachusetts Medical Society; 1 of 
41 whom shall be a member of the Massachusetts Health Quality Partners; 1 of whom shall be a 
42 member of the Massachusetts Public Health Association; 1 of whom shall be the president and 
43 chief executive officer of the Massachusetts Association for Mental Health or a designee; 1 of 
44 whom shall be a member of The Arc of Massachusetts or a designee; 1 of whom shall be a health 
45 economist from a Massachusetts academic institution; 1 of whom shall be a health economist 
46 with expertise in payment reform; and 2 of whom shall be persons with lived experience 
47 accessing health care services and navigating the health care system. The chair of the universal 
48 health care implementation commission may use their discretion to appoint no more than two 
49 additional members to assist in carrying out the duties of the commission. 
50  (d) The commission shall issue a report and develop recommendations evaluating single-
51 payer health care financing systems and models of health care delivery, and the feasibility of 
52 implementing such systems in the commonwealth. Such recommendations shall include, but not 
53 limited to: (i) models pursued by other states and implemented by other countries in which health 
54 care is publicly administered and is financed by taxes; (ii) methods to ensure that all residents 
55 have access to the health care that they need including, but not limited to, primary care, 
56 behavioral health care, mental health care, dental care, vision care, hearing aids, home health 
57 care, nursing home care, long-term care, hospice care, and any other important health care needs; 

4 of 5 58 (iii) the cost of implementing a single-payer health care model to the commonwealth’s health 
59 care system over 1, 2, 5 and 10 years; (iv) cost control methods, including the reduction of 
60 administrative cost and inefficiencies, (v) methods and models for funding the single-payer 
61 system, including, but not limited to, federal funding opportunities and federal matching funds; 
62 (vi) resource planning, including an assessment of available resources to avoid unnecessary 
63 duplication and any additional resources needed for underserved populations and geographic 
64 areas; (vii) the effects on the labor market, including an evaluation of current workforce 
65 capabilities, displacement of commercial insurer workforce, and additional needs to ensure 
66 appropriate staffing levels and quality, culturally and linguistically competent care is delivered; 
67 (viii) the utilization of services under a single payer system; (ix) methods to ensure timely access 
68 to needed medical and dental care; (x) methods to ensure compliance and integration with federal 
69 law, including any federal waiver requests, Medicare integration, and any ERISA limitations; 
70 (xi) effect on providers, including reimbursement methods and the transition from existing public 
71 and private payment systems to a single-payer reimbursement model; (xii) the impact of a single-
72 payer health care financing system on health care facilities, including rural and safety-net 
73 hospitals, employers, and the health care industry within the commonwealth; and (xiii) estimated 
74 transition costs, including the information technology infrastructure, claims processing systems, 
75 and administrative resources required to implement and operate a single-payer health care 
76 financing system; (xiv) patient satisfaction in jurisdictions using such systems; (xv) the impact of 
77 a single-payer health care financing system on collectively bargained health benefits, including 
78 union-sponsored and multiemployer health plans; (xvi) an assessment on the readiness of key 
79 health care and public institutions to carry out a single-payer health care model and strategies to 
80 reduce the complexities and administrative burdens on participants in the health care workforce 

5 of 5 81 and to address workforce challenges; and (xvii) any further research the commission deems 
82 necessary to fulfill its obligations as established in this section and complete its duties. 
83  (e) The commission may, in consultation with the center for health information analysis, 
84 recommend a methodology to develop a single-payer health care financing system benchmark 
85 and may compare health care expenditures from calendar years 2024, 2025 and 2026 calendar 
86 years to the single-payer health care financing system benchmark. 
87  (f) The commission shall meet quarterly, with the ability of the chair to convene 
88 additional meetings and subgroups as needed to carry out the duties of the commission. 
89  (g) The health policy commission shall issue a report of the special commission’s 
90 findings with recommendations to the clerks of the house of representatives and the senate, the 
91 house and senate committees on ways and means, the joint committee on health care financing, 
92 the executive office of health and human services, and the division of insurance not later than 
93 July 31, 2028.
