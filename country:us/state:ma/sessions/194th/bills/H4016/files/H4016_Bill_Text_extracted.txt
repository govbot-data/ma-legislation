An Act regarding human nature. 
 
 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives in General Court assembled, and by the authority 
of the same, as follows: 
 
1  SECTION 1. The General Laws are hereby amended by inserting after chapter 233 the 
2 following chapter:- 
3  CHAPTER 233A. 
4  NEUROSCIENCE IN CRIMINAL PROCEEDINGS. 
5  Section 1. As used in this chapter, the following words shall, unless the context clearly 
6 requires otherwise, have the following meanings:- 
7  "Fight or flight response", an involuntary physiological reaction occurring in response to 
8 a perceived harmful event, attack or threat to survival that may temporarily overwhelm a person's 
9 capacity for reason, conscience control and judgment rendering the person temporarily incapable 
10 of resisting or controlling an impulse that leads to the commission of an act without the 
11 occurrence of a mind directing it thereby impairing the ability to exercise deliberate restraint or 
12 select alternative means of avoiding a physical confrontation.. 

2 of 5 13  "Neuroscience evidence", scientific evidence derived from the study of the structure or 
14 function of the nervous system, brain and neural development, including but not limited to, 
15 neuroimaging, neuropsychological assessments and empirical research findings regarding brain 
16 function, development and trauma responses offered to demonstrate cognitive impairment or 
17 limitations at the time of the alleged criminal act. 
18  Section 2. In any criminal proceeding, evidence regarding the activation of fight or flight 
19 responses shall be admissible when relevant to the issue of a defendant's criminal liability, intent 
20 or culpability. 
21  Section 3. Neuroscience evidence regarding fight or flight responses may be presented to 
22 establish that a defendant, at the time of the alleged criminal act, had fight or flight responses 
23 activated such that the defendant could not control their conduct and so did not have the capacity 
24 to use their conscious mind but reacted based on instincts, with their conscience and judgment 
25 overwhelmed by fight or flight responses. 
26  Section 4. (a) In any criminal proceeding in which a defendant asserts self‐defense, the 
27 defendant shall be permitted to introduce neuroscience evidence to demonstrate that, due to the 
28 involuntary fight‐or‐flight response, it may have been cognitively impossible for the defendant 
29 to: 
30    (i) avail themselves of proper means to avoid physical combat, and 
31    (ii) use no more force than was necessary under the circumstances. 
32  (b) When credible neuroscience evidence is presented establishing that an involuntary 
33 fight‐or‐flight response impaired the defendant’s cognitive capacity at the time of the incident, 

3 of 5 34 the court shall consider that impairment as mitigating the defendant’s inability to meet the 
35 requirements described in subsection (a). 
36  (c) In such instances, the burden shall shift to the prosecution to prove, beyond a 
37 reasonable doubt, that the defendant’s actions exceeded what was objectively necessary despite 
38 the demonstrated cognitive limitations. 
39  Section 5. In cases involving provocation, neuroscience evidence may be introduced to 
40 establish the impact of fight or flight responses on a defendant's capacity for reflection or 
41 restraint, consistent with the principles articulated in Commonwealth v. Escobar, 493 Mass. 694 
42 (2024). 
43  Section 6. In felony-murder cases, neuroscience evidence may be introduced to address 
44 the application of constructive malice where a defendant had no intention to commit a violent act 
45 outside of the underlying felony, but whose fight or flight responses were triggered during the 
46 commission of the felony. 
47  Section 7. When there is evidence indicating that fight or flight responses were triggered 
48 and caused a lack of criminal responsibility or mitigated culpability, the court shall provide 
49 proper instructions to the jury explaining the impact of fight or flight responses on a defendant's 
50 ability to make a conscious decision. 
51  Section 8. Nothing in this chapter shall be construed to create a new affirmative defense, 
52 but rather to provide for the admissibility of scientific evidence relevant to existing legal 
53 standards of criminal responsibility, intent, and culpability. 

4 of 5 54  SECTION 2. Section 79 of chapter 233 of the General Laws, as appearing in the 2022 
55 Official Edition, is hereby amended by adding the following paragraph:- 
56  Records and reports of examinations related to a defendant's neurological functioning, 
57 including assessments of fight or flight responses, shall be admissible as evidence of the 
58 condition of the defendant, subject to the provisions of chapter 233A. 
59  SECTION 3. Chapter 278 of the General Laws, as so appearing, is hereby amended by 
60 adding the following section:- 
61  Section 36. (a) In any criminal case where the evidence raises the issue of the impact of 
62 fight or flight responses on a defendant's mental state, the court shall instruct the jury on the 
63 relationship between neuroscience evidence regarding fight or flight responses and the legal 
64 standards for criminal liability, in accordance with the standards established in chapter 233A. 
65  (b) In cases where neuroscience evidence is introduced, the court shall instruct the jury 
66 that: 
67    (i) credible neuroscience evidence may show that an involuntary fight‐or‐flight 
68 response can significantly impair an individual’s cognitive ability to make rational decisions in 
69 the heat of the moment; and 
70    (ii) such evidence must be given due weight in determining whether the defendant’s 
71 actions met or fell short of the statutory self‐defense requirements. 
72  (c) The jury shall be further instructed that if neuroscience evidence establishes a marked 
73 impairment in the defendant’s cognitive functioning at the time of the incident, this impairment 

5 of 5 74 may negate, in whole or in part, the failure to adhere to the requirements of avoiding physical 
75 combat or using no more force than necessary.
